The earlier he goes to bed the later he gets up, and the later he goes to bed the earlier he gets up. How can mortality follow so absurd an example?

Well, I embarked on my railway, content that all was arranged in order. But on getting out at Matale I was pleasantly hailed by an obvious clergyman, comparatively young. “Good morning; I conclude you are going to Anuradapura; so am I; I’m glad we shall be fellow-passengers on that trying journey.”

This is what you are liable to. So much for certainties! I scarcely had the heart to disclose to him my own selfish but indispensable arrangements, but was however obliged to do so. “The fault is mine,” he said; “I ought to have inquired, as I generally do.” And here was what is commonly called a “fix,” for there was no train back, and he was on duty and I was on necessity; for the journey with a squeeze of two was for me impossible. As he was a thorough gentleman and quite sincere, I shall not shrink from the phrase in which he expressed his dilemma: “Dear, dear,” said he; “I am under a solemn engagement to preach, and I only wish to please God Almighty.”

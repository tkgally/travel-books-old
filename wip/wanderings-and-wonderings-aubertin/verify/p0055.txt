My attention was pointedly called to this fact, and the name of the young lady was then given me as a Miss Dumont. The explanation was startling enough. She herself was half-caste, having certain white blood in her veins, while her mother had none, being wholly Burmese. Therefore the daughter of mixed blood was waited on by the mother of pure. This strange circumstance somewhat serves to exemplify social relationship throughout Burma, and I soon came to know that in every case servants go down on their knees before their masters on receiving orders or delivering messages or food. This is so, as between themselves, and I presently observed this custom going on haughtily on board on the part of a master of the most ordinary type. I was told that Burmese servants are especially attached to European masters, and an instance of this was given me later by a young officer who was deploring having to leave his devoted servant behind.

Next day was Christmas Day, and throughout, the river was again vast—and this, though the water is always low at this time of year—but the banks were again monotonous. We passed the great Oil Station, Yeanang Young, with one solitary handsome group of trees; and here we witnessed a very picturesque landing and scattering abroad on the slopes of our costumed native passengers.

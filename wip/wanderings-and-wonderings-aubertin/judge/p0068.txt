(as Shelley beautifully writes) there is really no such thing as death. Byron wrote a sentimental line—

<verse>
“Ah! surely nothing dies but something mourns.”

In the above view this might be paraphrased—

<verse>
“Ah! surely nothing dies but something lives.”
</verse>

Hence, Siva the Destroyer is symbolized as Siva the Creator or Renovator, because in the “unending change” the dispersion of one contributes to the formation of the other. But you may remark for ever, and yet not paint Benares.

After becoming intoxicated with variety, if not yet overcome with fatigue, the last visit must be to the towering mosque of Aurungzebe, an awful intrusion, and in form a tyrannical one, upon the gloomy sanctity of Siva. But rival religions know nothing of consideration or forbearance, and always arrogate to themselves the truth, and this particular mosque is stated to have been the especial fruit of arrogance. When you have put on your shoes again, after visiting the Mosque, take courage, and take breath, and mount a minaret. The view will well reward you.

The church itself may be plain enough, but, in the Dalmatian expression of Byzantine forms, the campanile is almost always lovely. Lésina is a bishopric, and includes those of Brazza and Cúrzola, which were suppressed in 1830.

At the left of the town and rather beyond it is a curious stone seat or sofa, carved round into a half circle, and hedged in with aloes; I had not time to examine it, but, from a distance, it had a very Greek and quaint effect. I heard that the island is thickly planted with aloes, and that much cordage and cloth is made from its fibre: but I have reason to believe this is an exaggeration. When I came to enquire narrowly about the cultivation of the aloe, I never could find any place where it was really carried on, though very many people told me it was ‘not here, but farther on;’ and I imagine that, if it ever was cultivated and made into anything tolerably good, the manufacture has now died out.

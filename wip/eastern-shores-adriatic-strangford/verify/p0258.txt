The time may come, however, when the store-houses and factories of Spálato may gather round the whole of the gulf.

I do not think this day would be despairingly far off were some changes made in the government of Dalmatia. I heard the same cry everywhere, from high and low, master and peasant. The Dalmatian is poor, miserably poor, because he has no encouragement to improve his condition, no profit by which to benefit himself. The customs and the dues are so heavy, that they absorb more than could be earned from the products of his mountain soil. Even now, without any railway, Spálato is the port of Serayevo and Travnik, and Bosnia takes all her imports through Spálato—a trade which has made Sign the half-way stopping-place, a thriving and prosperous town in comparison with other towns.

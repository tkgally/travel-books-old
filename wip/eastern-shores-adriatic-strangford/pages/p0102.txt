The hardy and enterprising sailors and industrious agriculturists of the Southern Islands, much more genuine Greeks than his Albanian self, might form in time a desirable addition to his little kingdom; but demagogues and mob-servers were not so rare at Athens as to make him over eager for a gift which would encumber his assembly with the half-alien Dandolos and Pretenderis and Republicas. It is a further argument against sudden change, as well as an indication that change of some kind would soon have forced itself into notice as an ultimate necessity, that our people in the Islands, always on most cordial social terms with the Ionians as far as the intercourse went, had of late years paid some attention to the study of modern Greek. The Ionian gentlemen, as yet imperfectly Grecised, were becoming gradually more and more Greek; while the English, acquiring more and more knowledge of that language, were gaining the power of directly influencing the people for their good, and of discriminating between the wheat and the chaff of their nationalism.

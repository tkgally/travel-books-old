[[cont]]Place is occupied by a splendid building, with a lofty arcade in front of it, now advanced to its second story. This is to be the Palazzo di Giustizia and the University. Bajamonte has already built a large school for the sons of the Spalatian gentry some way out of the town, and another is being commenced for their daughters. These will be, if well carried out, an infinite gain not only to Spálato but to all Dalmatia; for the instruction given to Dalmatian boys is of the meanest kind, and the unfortunate girls get nothing beyond the commonest rudiments of education, unless their parents send them to Lubiana (Laybach)[[fnref *]] or Venice. A very few are sent to Vienna; but of the education given them there and at Laybach I heard the same complaint from all the parents—that it was too conventual to be of any use to them in the world. Padua, as I have said, is the only real education given to their sons, unless they are sent to the military college at Vienna.

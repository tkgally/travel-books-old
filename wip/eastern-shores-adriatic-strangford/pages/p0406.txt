A year later 300 Suliotes enrolled themselves under Lord Byron: the only result of which was, that they thoroughly disgusted their new master by their rapacity, pretension, and falsehood. His death followed a few months after, and from this time it is impossible to trace the Suliotes; they became absorbed among the Greeks in their general struggle, and were so entirely dispersed that the Suliote name has never reappeared anywhere but as belonging to an individual.

## II.

Since Chapter IV. was printed I have received a detailed account of the goose-fights that took place this last season at Skodra; and also of fights of another kind,—those of ducks. My informant had witnessed several sanguinary and long-contested battles between pairs of the latter birds; and he considers that the ducks are more <i>plucky</i> than the geese, while the larger birds are more furious and fierce.

Ducks are bred in great numbers on the Lake of Skodra, and preserved with care. At the end of the summer they are collected, and the skins of their lovely dark green heads are made into linings of cloaks for the richest men and pashas of Skodra. The sewing together is executed with extreme neatness; and nothing can be more beautiful than the effect of the rich and glistening hues of these linings, which are also excessively costly.

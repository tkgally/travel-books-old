Here and there two or three houses cluster round an old mosque or a ruined tower, but they are mostly dotted about singly: and the distances are so great that from one end to the other would take at least two hours’ walking. The ruined palace of Selim Bey stands commandingly on the summit of a lofty hill.

As we had divided upon the plain into three or four different paths, the first thing to be done when we had reached Delvino was to find each other; but this was not accomplished until we had wandered far and wide, loudly shouting, and enquiring from every man, woman, and child we could see. We were decidedly in difficulties, for it was the hour of the mid-day sleep, and our enquiries were made in Greek, while the seeming answers were given in Albanian—neither party in the least understanding the other. At last, after three hours of this work, we were all transferred into the Mudir’s or Bey’s house, while the servants were sent to unload the mules in a neighbouring olive garden. The Bey welcomed us with some hospitality, and entered into an animated conversation with Capt.

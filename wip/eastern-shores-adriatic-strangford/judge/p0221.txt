Cyprien Robert writes of ‘<i>Les quatre Albanies,</i>’ meaning the districts of the Gheghs, Tosks, Ljaps and Tchams, the last two, though affiliated with the second, being considered now to stand by themselves as separate divisions. I do not think my readers will thank me or be much the wiser if I fire off into their faces a mere repetition of these uncomfortable, snappish monosyllables, that fail to convey any idea of practical value which is not much better expressed in another way. Besides, such a division leaves out a great deal: ten districts are enumerated by Colonel Leake in his earliest and now rare work (‘Researches in Northern Greece and Albania’) which do not belong to any of these main branches. The true and intelligible division is that of religious denomination. This has the advantage of coinciding broadly with a natural geographical demarcation, and it also serves to indicate the past history as well as the present condition and future prospects of Albanian civilisation in its three forms—Catholic, Greek, and Mussulman.

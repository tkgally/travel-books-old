The Prince took me one afternoon into the old monastery close to the Palace. It is the residence of the Archbishop, who, to my great regret, was at that moment gone to St. Petersburg to be consecrated. Preparations were then commencing for a national fête to celebrate his return. I made acquaintance with the young Archimandrite, who also lives there, and who is a very great favourite of the Prince’s. He is a poet, and writes war songs, many of which the Prince sang to us. He wore a black silk dress, edged with crimson, and his tall figure and dark costume was often conspicuous in the sittings of the Senate. As he generally had a paper in his hand, I suspect he often attended to read out the documents which the old warriors of the mountains could not read for themselves.

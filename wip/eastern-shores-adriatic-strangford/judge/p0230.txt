The Albanians have no cultivated language by which to educate themselves, and easy-going Austria, though an Italian power, so to speak, in the Adriatic, cannot put her heart in the work of Italianising these people, which is the only way of training and educating them to become a European community. Nor, from common interests, and a now active sense of having to stand or fall with Turkey, to say nothing of good faith and respect for treaties, has she any wish to annex in this direction and assume direct rule herself.

It is the misfortune of these Northern Christians that, unlike their Southern brethren, who are confronted by Greek influence whichever way they turn, whether to Greece proper, Thessaly, or the sea, they have no Italian or Italo-Sclave frontagers of their own religion, and of a master-language. Between them and their co-religionists lies Montenegro, firmly knit together, aggressive and ardently anti-Catholic.

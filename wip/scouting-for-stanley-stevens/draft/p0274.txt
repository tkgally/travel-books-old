“Shoot him, Bwana; shoot him!” shouted the runners, excitedly.

“Shoot him? What for? The man is running away; he’s done nothing to us. Why should he be shot?”

“Shoot him; he’s a bad man. He’s running, running, Bwana; he’s running.”

“Take this gun and go and catch him if you can, and let’s see who he is, but don’t shoot him.”

Mfomo, Omali and Nivova took after the fugitive, but the bush was too thick, and so he escaped. Half an hour later we had the same experience over again, only with three natives, instead of one. The runners said they were some of Bushiri’s people, who had got scattered in the bush, and were on the lookout to join a party of their friends. At the sight of a white man, being but two or three, they ran away.

By three o’clock we were out of water. By four we were badly distressed, for the sun seemed to glare hotter and fiercer as the afternoon drew out. But succor of a very unexpected nature awaited us.

Nearing a small ravine I ordered Zaidi to repair to a certain clump of bushes to see if there was water. A minute later, “Bwana, bwana, njoe!” (master, master; come here!) shouted the runner, excitedly.

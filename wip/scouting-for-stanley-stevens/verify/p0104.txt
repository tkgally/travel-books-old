The dark remnants of the primeval woods contrasted beautifully with the lighter shade of the parks, and these again with the yet lighter fields of wimbi and the pale green of the banana groves; moreover, the far-reaching table-land sloping down toward our position placed the picture before us as on an easel. All this, easy enough to put in words, conveys but a meagre idea of the real beauty of the scene. Nor was this all nature had to show our wondering eyes in Machame. Hundreds of warriors, with spear and shield, their naked forms the only dark objects in the landscape, showed out in bold contrast and picturesque relief against the green groundwork of their surroundings as they stood and squatted in dense groups or stretched in long, irregular lines on the opposite brink of the cañon. Beyond all this was a dense mass of cloud that rested on the farther reaches of the green table-land and hid almost the whole of Kilimanjaro. But not all, for the higher strata of the clouds sometimes broke and revealed the eternal wreath of snow on Kibo, at whose very base we now seemed to be standing. Some day an artist will come and paint this picture I have feebly attempted to describe, and make himself famous.

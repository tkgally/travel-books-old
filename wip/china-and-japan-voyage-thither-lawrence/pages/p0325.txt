# Letter XXX.

[[right]] U. S. S. W<sc>achusett</sc>, Hankow, China, March 13th, 1867.

M<sc>y</sc> D<sc>ear</sc> R.:

Six hundred miles into the interior of China! As far away from salt water as those living in Ohio! If sight-seeing was our <i>only</i> object in view, we could not be much more favored.

The summary of places we have visited and sights we have seen during the past year, is a very varied, extensive, and interesting one. We have twice been the entire length of the coast of China, and several times over large portions of it, visiting all but two or three of its open ports, and many that are not yet opened to foreign intercourse; been in many of its cities and towns, and up several of its rivers; seen its great wonders, in fact, everything worthy of note, getting a good insight into the appearance, manner, and customs of its inhabitants; visited the principal places in Japan—seeing nearly everything worthy of note there, and getting an insight into the appearance, manners, and customs of the Japanese; passed through the inland sea (a trip in itself worth no small sum to sight-seers); explored somewhat the much talked-of, and, at present, interesting Corea; and, at last, we find ourselves six hundred miles in the interior of China.

The weather is quite warm for this season of the year, but far different from what it was upon our trip last summer. On the whole, it is as pleasant as we could expect, and the trip has been all that one could have desired.

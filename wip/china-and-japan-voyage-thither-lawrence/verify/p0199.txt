I must narrate an incident of our stay here, illustrative of the imitative faculties of the Chinese, and their lack of invention. As labor is very cheap, and Chinese tailoring good, many of the men have sent cloth ashore to the native tailors and had clothes made. They would seldom take the measure, but wanted a pattern. One man, wishing to have a pair of pants made, gave the tailor an old pair, adorned with several large patches, for a pattern. When the work was done and delivered, the new pants were a perfect imitation of the old ones, even to the size, form, and number of the patches which they contained, and what was better still, the tailor demanded extra pay for the extra labor.

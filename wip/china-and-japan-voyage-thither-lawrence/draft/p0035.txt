# Letters.

# Letter I.

[[right]] U. S. S. W<sc>achusett</sc>, Boston Harbor, March 1, 1865.

M<sc>y</sc> D<sc>ear</sc> R.:

A<sc>t</sc> last my long cherished desire is about to be attained—that of going to sea. You know how strong that desire has been, how long and eagerly I have striven to attain it, and now that success is about to crown my undertakings, you can better imagine my feelings than I can describe them. It was your earnest request that I would write you at length concerning what I might see, hear, or think, that might savor of interest to you, leaving me to select such matter as I might deem interesting. At the same time you promised that whatever should fail to awaken the interest anticipated should be as thankfully received as though it had accomplished the desired end, in short, promising in all cases to take the will for the deed. In view of such a request, so favorably conditioned, I could not do less than I did,—cordially promise that so far as it should lie within my power, I would gratify you.

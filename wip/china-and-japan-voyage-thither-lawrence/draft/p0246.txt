We taste and forthwith exclaim, “this is <i>tea!</i>” I have never tasted its equal before.” O ye afternoon New England tea-drinkers, could you but have some of this tea, methinks that that reputed flow of conversation for which you are famed, would resemble the rapid mountain torrent whose progress is not to be restrained by bond or barrier!

The Chinese have furnished us with tea as well as with cups, but few Europeans seem to understand the art of preparing the beverage, and are seldom seen to partake of it, for the sake of the pure taste of the tea. Tea-shops are everywhere to be seen throughout China, and a visitor is invariably presented with a cup of the beverage immediately upon his arrival. The Chinese seldom make “strong tea,” and never add milk or sugar.

Tea marks the period of our departure from Shanghai for Japan to-day, and is a good word with which to close this letter. May we soon have a tea-drinking together in the United States, and if the article is not equal to that we had in China, there will be more than enough else to compensate for the mere loss of tea.

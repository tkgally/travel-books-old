They use stockings and sandals similar to those worn by the Japanese, but with no separate place for the great toe. Altogether, their appearance is inferior to that of either the Chinese or Japanese.

The next day the chief’s son was sent by us with a communication to the king of Corea, and also with a letter to the presiding officer of the province, Chang-Yuen-Heen. The following is a copy of the latter:

<blockquote>
[[center]] (<i>Copy.—Translation.</i>)

[[right]] U<sc>nited</sc> S<sc>tates</sc> S<sc>teamer</sc> W<sc>achusett</sc>, Mouth of the Ta-tung River, Jan. 24, 1867.

S<sc>ir</sc>:

The Commander of the American Armed Vessel Wachusett sends greeting to the presiding officer of the Province of Chang-Yuen-Heen, and wishes to inform his excellency that he has come to the borders of Corea, not to engage in war nor any unlawful business; but is anxious that harmony and peace should exist, as heretofore, between America and Corea.

“He respectfully requests that you forward the accompanying document to your king with all due dispatch. It is hoped that the answer to the accompanying document will be returned without delay, that he may depart without delay, in peace, from where he is now lying at anchor in the harbor of Ta-ping.

[[center]] Very Respectfully, &c., &c.,

[[right]] (Signed) R<sc>obert</sc> W. S<sc>chufeldt</sc>,

[[right]] Commander of the United States Steamer Wachusett.

His Excellency the Presiding Officer of the Province of Chang-Yuen-Heen.
</blockquote>

But a far closer settlement and a richer reward can be gained in districts where it is possible to apply the system of irrigation by damming up river-courses. Land of which large tracts can only be used for feeding sheep can be made as fertile as the best in England. The growing of lucerne crops, for instance, would enable small farmers to go in for pig-raising, dairying, and raising early lambs for the market. Vegetables and fruit, and particularly potatoes, could be grown profitably, and the land under irrigation would support a population out of all proportion to its capabilities under unaltered conditions. Irrigation in Australia is therefore of the utmost importance, and the Governments of those States whose land is mostly in use, but not in the most productive use, are spending money and brains in furthering it.

## III

Here is one instance of many that might be quoted of success gained in this way. An assisted immigrant came to Queensland from Sweden and worked on a plantation until he had saved enough money to take up 200 acres of his own at 15<i>s.</i> an acre. He fenced and cleared the land himself, acre by acre, until he had fifty acres under cane; and he grew maize and sweet potatoes and started dairying besides. Soon he was able to buy another 320 acres for £1,000. He now owns 680 acres, some under cane, some under other crops, and is worth £4,000. And many of the most successful cane-growers in Queensland began in this same way—that is, with an assisted passage and no capital whatever.

The tendency for the last twenty years has been for the big estates to be cut up and divided into small holdings, for it has been found that more cane can be grown on a given area in this way than when it was worked by means of coloured labour by a big planter.

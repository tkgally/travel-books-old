They are in closer touch with the actual products of the wealth with which they deal. Their interests are more in actualities—in land, or wheat, or wool, or ships—and not only in the paper which represents them. They know about the land, and what is doing on it, and so it has come about that they can talk about these things with the pastoralists and agriculturists where in England a man of business and a country gentleman might have no topic of conversation in common. And the men who have made their way, who have had no special education, and speak with a rough tongue, take their places with the rest, and are not shut off into a little group of tolerated suspects.

This is all in accordance with Australian national feeling. There was a box in the centre of the grand stand for the vice-royalties, and they had their rooms behind, in which they entertained their guests at luncheon and tea; and the stewards of the Jockey Club had their luncheon-room, and I think a small private stand. But there was no enclosure into which admission was by privilege, and I was told that the Jockey Club, although the whole place was their property, had not been able to withstand popular feeling to the extent of reserving any further part of the ground or buildings for the use of their members.

He is clever, young, good-looking, athletic,—he played cricket for Oxford,—takes a serious view of his duties, and works hard at them. He ought to rise in future years to the highest places in the service of the Empire.

To mention a few of the others: Admiral Sir Day Bosanquet, Governor of South Australia, is one of the sailor-governors of whom Australians are particularly fond,—the memory of the late Admiral Sir Harry Rawson is almost idolized in New South Wales. South Australia is opening up new wheat lands faster than any State, and Sir Day Bosanquet, when I was in Adelaide, told me as much about them as if he were his own Minister of Lands, and had nothing else to occupy himself with. He had only been in the State a year, but he had already travelled into the northern desert, and, besides other journeys, had made a long camping-trip through little-travelled country, and himself discovered a fine new harbour where the future golden wealth of that district will probably be tapped for the benefit of the world.

Upon my return to England in ’86, I found that a new expedition was being organized for important work in Africa, and upon Mr. Stanley’s advice I accepted a responsible position offered me in this new enterprise, called the Sanford Exploring Expedition; the object of which was to visit all the villages along the banks of the Congo and its tributaries, and make thorough research into the marketable products of the land, with the ultimate object of establishing commercial exchange between the civilized world and the inhabitants of Central Africa.

Most of my time, during a brief holiday of ten weeks at home, was spent in superintending preparations for the new work, in buying the necessary provisions, general stores, and trading goods, in having all suitably packed in sixty-pound loads for their long overland journey into the interior, and in attending to their shipment.

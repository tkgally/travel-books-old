[[cont]]on such a festival day the little Shinto temple seems to have come suddenly to life; its buildings, empty certainly and innocent of all ornament save for the symbolic rope of straw with its pendent paper zigzags, are yet no longer deserted. The barrier is still before the main building, but hangings of purple cotton are looped up above it, stamped in white with, it may be, some ordinary cognisance, or, it may be, with the Imperial chrysanthemum, and over the entrance are probably crossed the white banners of the Rising Sun, for is not Shinto the Imperial faith, and is not its chief deity the “Imperial Ancestress”

—Goddess of the Sun? Under the few tall cryptomeria trees which give dignity to the bare and rather shabby court are sitting and standing little groups of onlookers—or are they worshippers?—all gay and laughing in quiet placid tones. Suddenly a few thumps on a big drum sound from the main building, and the figures of priests are seen within; quaint apparitions wearing the court robes of long ago—loose white silk garments hanging over wide trousers of the same material and bound in at the waist by a girdle.

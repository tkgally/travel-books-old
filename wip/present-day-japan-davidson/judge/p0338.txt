Amongst others, I cannot imagine Sir Henry Irving executing a <i>pas seul</i> on the stage, attired in two or three long trained skirts, and performing wonders with one or more paper fans the while. But it is true in so far as that he is a very prominent and striking actor, and a great supporter of the legitimate drama. This artist comes, as is usual in Japan, of a family devoted to the same art for many real or adoptive generations, and there has long been a Danjuro at the head of the theatrical profession. (Danjuro, by the way, is only a professional name; his real name is quite different.) There are other stars too in plenty—Kikugoro, for instance, also an excellent actor—and indeed in almost every play I have seen in a good theatre even the minor parts are well and carefully done.

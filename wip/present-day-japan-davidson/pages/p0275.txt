[[cont]]But the first sign of danger will put an end to all this, and however much may be prophesied to the contrary I cannot but feel sure that so long as this ancestral fire—“Yamato damashi”—endures, the island empire is perfectly safe, and her future as a nation is assured. Everything goes to show that the descendants of those who went so cheerfully to death for loyalty’s sake of old will sink all political rivalries in the presence of a foe without, or of economic difficulties within.

[[cont]]Japan no less than the rest of the planet. In 1868 the curtain in this particular theatre fell, and rose at once upon the new act that is now going on. A space of three hundred years is supposed to have elapsed—that is all: rather an unusual interval perhaps, seeing that the <i>dramatis personæ</i> remain practically the same, though the names of their parts have mostly been changed. The picturesque figures so conspicuous in the last act as Shogun, Daimyo, and Samurai are no longer seen; their <i>rôles</i> are those of the Prime Minister, the Viscount Somebody, and the Policeman respectively, but many of the performers remain the same.

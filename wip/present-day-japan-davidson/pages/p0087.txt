Of course you must know there is no public dining-room in a Japanese inn; your room is a convertible apartment—dining, drawing, and bedroom by turns. You get up in the morning, and when you come back from the excitements of the bath you find that every trace of your bed, and your mosquito curtains too, if it is the season for them, has vanished into space, and that nothing remains in the

Nothing else at all, for the room is now a sitting-room and furnished as such. You order a meal for a certain hour, and when it comes the room becomes a dining-room for the time being.

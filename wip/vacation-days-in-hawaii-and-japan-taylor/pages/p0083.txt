Upon these occasions the natives bring long garlands of flowers, called “leis,” four or five feet in length, which they place in lavish profusion about the necks and shoulders of the departing travelers. Just before the steamer casts off her moorings wreaths and flowers are flung over her in perfect abandon, shouts of “bon voyage!” mingle with cheers, music, and singing, and the excitement is intense.

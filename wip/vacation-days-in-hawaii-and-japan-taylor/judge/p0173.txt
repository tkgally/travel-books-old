The space is now so narrow and low that after going one hundred feet farther, we must stoop to prevent our heads from bumping against the roof. Now we creep through an opening of not more than three feet, and we continue in this cramped position for a distance of apparently about fifty feet, when we are at the end of the cave. How dark, damp, and still it is! Our flickering candles cast about us an uncanny yellow light, and many strange thoughts flit through our brains. What if this spot is really haunted by a powerful goddess, who could appear, and in the twinkling of an eye transport us to some undreamt of realm! I think it wise to offer a prayer to this goddess of luck to get us safely out of this wet and unattractive chamber.

After some twenty minutes more of this meditation, we retrace our steps, and gladly breathe the pure air again with a clear sky above us.

At the entrance to the cave we notice a Japanese athlete standing naked, excepting the loin-cloth about his waist, and poised on the very edge of a huge rock that overhangs the sea. The guide informs us that he is a diver, and for a few sen will plunge to the bottom of the sea, a depth of thirty feet, and, as an evidence of this feat, will bring up shells gathered there.

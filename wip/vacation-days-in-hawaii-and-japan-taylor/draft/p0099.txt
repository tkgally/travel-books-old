[[cont]]Mahukona, the first landing on the island of Hawaii, where we will remain three hours, while freight is being conveyed ashore.

Mr. W——invites us to land and breakfast with himself and family at his country residence close by. The boat is lowered and passengers are rowed ashore by natives.

Upon an eminence at our right a pretty cottage is perched, surrounded by plenty of shade trees. Here we receive a warm welcome and a good breakfast.

After breakfast I walk to the wharf to see the unloading of the cargo. As before, the steamer lies a mile from shore, and the fright is placed in boats and rowed to its destination, large timbers being thrown overboard and towed to the land. Live cattle, such as steers and horses, are lowered into the water and swim ashore.

Mahukona is a barren-looking place, although in certain sections there are large sugar plantations. It is about one hundred and twenty miles from Honolulu and eighty from Hilo, our destination.

With the good wishes of our hosts we say farewell, and our steamer goes on her way.

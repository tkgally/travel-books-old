At nine o'clock in the morning a carriage awaits us in the courtyard of the hotel, and, with steamer trunks, chairs, and rugs, we are driven to the Fulsom Street pier, where the “Australia” is moored. Our state-rooms, comfortable and well ventilated, are on the promenade deck. At exactly ten o'clock the whistle sounds, the steamer casts off her moorings, and with the bustle and confusion of departure, “Good-bye” and “God bless you,” in many keys, still ringing in our ears, we break away from the shore, and find ourselves out in the middle of the bay.

The steamer is to be our little world for the next six days. We are cut off from all communication with any human beings save those around us; dependent upon ourselves and each other. A slight feeling of sadness overcomes us, and we begin to talk of the loved ones in our far-off home.

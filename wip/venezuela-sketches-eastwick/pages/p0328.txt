In all these sorts, the cotton is separated from the seeds in carding-machines (máquinas de sierra), imported from the United States since the year 1818, up to which time the only machines known were the iron cylinders, by which only twenty-five to thirty-five pounds a day could be cleaned, and this frequently turned out of very bad quality, because many of the seeds were crushed and passed through the rollers to the deterioration of the cotton.

The following is an estimate of the cost and expenses of a quintal of cotton in the best lands of the province of Aragua, the ground being supposed to be covered with brushwood, and not with a virgin forest. The “tarea de socalo,” <i>i. e.</i>, the task or job (tarea) of clearing wood with a bill, is usually a space of thirty brazadas long by six wide, equal to seven hundred and twenty square yards.

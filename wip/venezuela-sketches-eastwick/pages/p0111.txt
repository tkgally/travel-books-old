“In what manner I returned to the house of Señor Rivas I know not. The open air, instead of sobering me, seemed to make me worse; but the first thing I distinctly recognized was a horribly cold sensation in my left hand. On drawing it towards me, a squelch of falling water followed, and I found I had been lying with my hand in the ewer, out of which I suppose I had been drinking. Getting up with a splitting headache, I dressed slowly, and had scarce refreshed myself with a cup of coffee, when somebody knocked at the door. I called out ‘Entrate,’ and, to my surprise, in stepped an Irish officer I knew by sight only, who, without a word of preface, handed me a challenge from O’Halloran.

“After reading the epistle twice, and looking a third time at the address, to make sure I was the party intended, I turned to Kelly—that was the name of the officer—and said: ‘Will you have the goodness to explain what this means? I think there must be some mistake.’

“‘Mistake, sir,’ said Kelly; ‘you’re mighty fond, sir, of that word “mistake.” Ye said it was a mistake last night; but, faith, sir, it’s a mistake that there’s only one way of clearing up.’ Then putting his hand into his pocket and producing another note, he handed it to me with great ceremony, saying, ‘Do you call that a mistake, sir?’

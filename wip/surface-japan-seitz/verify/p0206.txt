While the Shogun is but a memory forty-five years away, and the heir of the Tokugawa ranks merely with the other nobles whom his family so long dominated as the real rulers of Japan, the idea of a super-ruler does not vanish. The elder statesmen, much quoted in the West, are, or rather were, real. When Prince Ito lived, he, with Prince Yamagata and Count Inouye, guided the government. Now the field-marshal dominates. The government is his. The Prime Minister, Prince Katsura, abides by his bidding. Despite all the modernity that has come in, the Emperor is still to the mass a sacred personality, and care is taken to keep him so.

At Sysek everybody exerted themselves to the utmost to send us by the steam-boat. The landlord, an accomplished blackguard, offered us the choice of waiting four days for the Saam steamer, or taking his Vetturino horses, with which, for some hundreds of florins, he proposed, in ten days, to carry us to Semlin. Every one, even the postmaster himself, threw difficulties in the way of our having post-horses, and assured us we should find no horses, roads, food, carts, or doctors, on the road to Semlin; and that, at any rate, it would take twelve days, and vast amounts of florins, to reach it; that the road was full of robbers, and that we should, moreover, infallibly catch the marsh-fever, and die in twenty-four hours.

He was very glad, he said, that we had not passed through Bosnia, which he assured us was a country fit only for wild animals, and did not contain a single man of any education or intelligence.

“I was on the Bosnian frontier once,” he said, “fighting against the Ban Jellachich. It is a bad land; the people, uncivilized brutes, very few of whom are Moslems by blood, and the soil is so poor, that the keep of a horse, which comes to thirty piastres a day here in Bulgaria, costs eighty in Bosnia.”

I asked him what he thought of the far-famed city of Bosnæ-Serai, the garrison and stronghold of Bosnia, that military semi-republic which rejects the rule of a Pacha.

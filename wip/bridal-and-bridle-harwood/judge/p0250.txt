Eska-baba is one of the prettiest towns, Turkish or Christian, that I ever saw. The vines of the purple south, trellised up the sides of the houses, arch over the streets with their cool green foliage: from noble old trees, great and shady-like forest patriarchs, are heard the flapping wings and clashing bills of the sacred storks, great numbers of which privileged birds fly around the mosques, hover over their minarets, or perch on the gilded pinnacles of their rich domes, fretted and gold-embossed. The costumes of the people are picturesque and gaudy, and the environs are interesting.

Moreover, here and there are placed rows of huge stone posts, set upright in the ground and reaching across the street for the discouragement of wheel-carriages doubtless, and such like infidel machines. A horse does not squeeze himself between the said dwarf pillars too easily, and often makes his rider’s stirrup ring against them in the dark.

The roofs of Sophia also are much better fitted to screen turbaned smokers from the sun’s rays, when established at their thresholds, than to promote the comfort of equestrians, whose heads bump against their projecting eaves very inconveniently, and it is easy to break your bones, one way or other, on entering that fair city, especially by night.

The Pacha was a short, broad-made man, with a heavy figure, a dark complexion, grey or grizzled beard, and a face, good-humoured, save the grey and glancing eyes, which seemed as if the fire’s fierceness of the true Osmanlee only slept within him.

His dress consisted of a white damask dressing-gown, with loose sleeves and open breast, a vest of flowered silk drawn tight across the chest, and fitted with close sleeves; wide white Turkish trowsers, strapped down over French boots; a black stock, and stand-up collar, and a dark crimson cap, with a long full black tassel. His hands, I noticed, were like those of most Turks, very broad, large, and powerful.

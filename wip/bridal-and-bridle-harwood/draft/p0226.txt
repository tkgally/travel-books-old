The driver, old Hussein, or Suleiman, whichever his name might have been, was a man of a large bulky form, with a set of features formed to express the sentiments of fear and wonder only. His beard would have been in perfect keeping on the portrait of a patriarch. His waist was encircled by a heavy shawl, whose immense rolls were in turn clasped round by a huge black leather belt, two feet broad in front, and dwindling to a mere thong behind. In this were stuck a knife and an enormous Turkish pistol, about the calibre of a small carabine. The fellow’s turban was one of the most curious superstructures ever laid upon a human head.

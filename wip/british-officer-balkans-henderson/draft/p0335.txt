# Chapter XXIV

[[subtitle]] Environs of Sarajevo and All Souls’ Day—The Greek Orthodox and Others

[[subtitle]] Kosevo Valley—Gajtan factory—Catholic observances in remembrance of the dead—Cemeteries as a rendezvous—The Valley of Golgotha—Old Turkish gravestones—The water-works—Bosnian hedges—An officer’s misadventure—The <i>Eichen Waldchen</i>—New Serb church—The ancient church—Its foundation—Easter marriage mart—Gala dresses—Greek <i>papas</i>—A remarkable woman—An English benefactress—Her school and orphanage—The burning question in Bosnia—The Spanish Jews—Their dress and peculiarities—The Feast of Tabernacles.

WITHIN half-an-hour of Sarajevo lies the pretty Kosevo valley, at the extremity of which, ringed in by hills, there stands a <i>Gajtana</i> factory for the manufacture of the braid used in the ornamentation of Bosniak jackets and trousers.

This factory has changed hands several times, and is said not to pay very well, though, judging from the fact that a Bosniak’s clothes are invariably braided, there must be a good demand for this article.

We went over the factory and saw the process of braid-making from start to finish, and the finishing one seemed to us the most novel. It was conducted in a little compartment outside the factory, and the operation was superintended by one small Turk. The compartment contained nothing but a table on which was a machine all revolving iron wheels, plates, and ribbons. The surface was dotted over with tiny tapers. The braid, unrolling from the wheels, skimmed over the heads of the lighted tapers, which were designed to singe off the rough wholly hairs that give the braid a coarse appearance, leaving it smooth and fine.

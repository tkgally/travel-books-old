## Behind the Veil in Persia and Turkish Arabia

[[center]] <sc>an account of an englishwoman’s eight years’ residence amongst the women of the east</sc>

[[center]] B<sc>y</sc> M. E. HUME-GRIFFITH

[[center]] <i>With Thirty-seven Illustrations and a Map. Demy 8vo.</i> 16<i>s. net.</i>

The author, Mrs. Hume-Griffith, gives an entrancing account of her eight years’ sojourn amongst the Mohammedans of Turkish Arabia, and the inhabitants of Persia, the Land of the Lion and the Sun. She succeeds in describing in a very breezy manner the life, manners, and customs of these people <i>as seen from within.</i> Unlike so many who visit a country with a view to recording their impressions of a brief stay, and return with very superficial knowledge and distorted ideas, the result either of hurry or reserve, Mrs. Hume-Griffith has been so long in these countries as to be in full sympathy with the customs and feelings of their inhabitants. The nature of her work, too, has broken down the barrier of reserve which would be inevitable in the case of an ordinary visitor; she was a constant visitor at the Anderoons or harems of the rich, middle-class, and poor, and her impressions of the social life and character of both Turkish and Persian women are most interesting.

The photographs are unique, and graphically illustrate the manners and customs of the people. Only Persian women are here presented, for the Quran strictly forbids the representation in any way of the human form.

## Among the Wild Tribes of the Afghan Frontier

[[center]] <sc>a record of sixteen years’ close intercourse with the natives of the indian marches</sc>

[[center]] B<sc>y</sc> T. L. PENNELL, M.D., B.S<sc>c</sc>., F.R.C.S.

[[center]] <sc>with an introduction by</sc>

[[center]] FIELD-MARSHAL LORD ROBERTS, V.C., K.G.

[[center]] <i>With Thirty-seven Illustrations and Two Maps. Demy 8vo.</i> 16<i>s. net.</i>

The Author, Dr. Pennell, has lived amongst the frontier tribes for sixteen years, and has given us the result of his experience in an intensely interesting book. He was attached to a medical mission, for without this qualification his life would not have been worth an hour’s purchase. As it was, he was constantly in danger, but his indomitable courage and tact brought him through many a trying situation. Dr. Pennell’s keen observation and analytical mind have given us a clear insight into the character of these tribes. They are fierce, turbulent, and revengeful to the last degree: indeed, revenge appears to be the motive which influences every action of their lives. On the other hand, their hospitality almost amounts to a religious observance, and the person of a guest is held sacred. The Afghan is also brave, but in commercial transactions is a mere babe when matched against the wily Hindoo. Dr. Pennell’s observations on the religions and philosophies of India are of great interest, and the account of his travels as a Sadhu (Begging Pilgrim) is both instructive and amusing.

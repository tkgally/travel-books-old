# Chapter XXII
[[subtitle]] Northern Japan
[[subtitle]] By Sea to Hakodate. The Japanese Navy. Hakodate. The Island of Hokkaidō. The Hairy Aïnos. “Good Wine needs no Bush.” Aomori. The Northern Railway. Matsushima. Bandai-san. The Eruption of 1888. Flowers and Birds. From Inawashiro to Nikkō.

A<sc>fter</sc> a few days’ sightseeing in Tōkyō and Yokohama, we started for the north, first going to Hakodate by the <i>Matsuyama Maru</i>, a steamer of nearly three thousand gross and two thousand net tons. Although <i>yusen</i> seems to be the Japanese equivalent for “mail steamship,” <i>maru</i> is post-fixed to the names of all merchant steamers, and <i>kan</i> to the names of men-of-war.

There were no other passengers at the European first-class table, and only a Japanese lady and her two boys at the Japanese table; but we had the society at meals of the three ship’s officers, who belonged to the Imperial Naval Reserve. The captain had been engaged during the Chinese war in navigating a ship laden with water for the use of the Japanese troops in Korea. The food provided at the European table was very good and plentiful. Breakfast when you like, lunch at 12.30, tea at 3, and dinner at 6 <sc>p.m.</sc>

We left Yokohama about ten in the morning of a fine, bright June day, and had a four hours’ steam down Tōkyō Bay to the Nogima-zaki light. By the time we had finished dinner the white light of Inuboye-zaki was flashing on our port bow, and we were on our course, nearly due north, for Oginohama, where we arrived the following forenoon.

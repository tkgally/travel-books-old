The wooden scabbard might be of the finest lacquer protected with a metal end (<i>kojiri</i>), upon which the goldsmiths’ best work had been expended. The silk braid (<i>sageo</i>) on the handle, as well as the <i>tasuki</i>, used when in action to tie back the sleeves and tied in a war-knot (<i>tachi-musubime</i>) on the scabbard afterward, would be the product of the best looms. There would be sword-bags (<i>tachi-bukuro</i>) of silk, and sword-racks (<i>katana-kake</i>) of carved wood or lacquer. But it was on the <i>kashira</i>, <i>menuki</i>, <i>tsuba</i>, and <i>fuchi</i> (which is an oval ring fitted round the hilt next to the <i>tsuba</i>) of the two swords that the metal-worker lavished his best work in gold, silver, and bronze. And the owner of a pair of good blades might have many complete hilts for each, as the parts are all interchangeable; and a bamboo rivet through the hilt and the <i>tang</i> is all that is required to hold them together. This rivet can be pushed out, and the blade, the ring, the guard, and metal collar round the blade detached. All this the custodian shewed us, pushing out the bamboo rivets and replacing them, with a special little tool; wiping off the oil-of-cloves with which the blades are covered, and polishing them with powder from a silk pounce-bag tied to a short stick (<i>ūchiko</i>), before handing the swords to us for our inspection. The long swords (<i>katana</i>) measure twenty-eight inches from guard to point, the back of the blades are three-eighths of an inch thick, and the hilt nine inches long. The short swords (<i>wakizashi</i>) are, respectively, nineteen inches, a quarter of an inch, and six and a half inches. The long sword has a recess in the scabbard for the <i>kōgai</i>, a sort of skewer, said to have been used to leave in the body of a dead enemy, which makes a very good paper-knife. The scabbard of the short sword has a similar recess to hold the small knife called <i>kozuka</i>. The exhibits included <i>himo-gatana</i> and <i>aikuchi</i>,—daggers with blades nine inches long. There was also a bronze figure of Confucius to be seen in the Tenjin museum; and many large bronze and stone figures of animals in the grounds.

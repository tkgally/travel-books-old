The ex-Premier has been calmly judged, and deliberately condemned by the highest authorities, as well as snapped at after his fall by many men of lesser ability. There is no need for me to judge him over again. My business throughout is more with his opponents, who have “laboured with such really courageous fidelity for the interests of the Methodist Church in Tonga,” “and with whom the Conference in Sydney so deeply sympathizes, on account of the trials and unjust accusations to which they have been exposed.”

[[stream]] The persecution of the Wesleyans begins in earnest in March, 1885. The law of the six is a regulation of the Tongan Parliament passed in 1885. “In order to prevent the disturbances which spring from the ecclesiastical arrangements of the present time, the King and the Tongan Parliament enact as follows: <b>(97)</b> It is forbidden to a religious body to preach a sermon in a town where there are fewer than six adults in all, men and women natives of the town. Anyone breaking this law shall on conviction be fined 20 dollars.”
[[stream]] The following letters are selected from the correspondence between Wesleyan missionaries and the Premier: “I am sorry to trouble you about business at this time, but my men have been forbidden by Nafetelai to use <b>(98)</b> the tank of our preacher’s house.
[[stream]] “I beg to call your attention, as Premier, to the extraordinary occurrence this morning at the Fai-kava All who are Wesleyans were told to go, after having taken their food, which was not <b>(99)</b> given back to them. Such action is certainly not characteristic of the Wesleyan Church, and I hope it will not be sanctioned by the rulers in the Free Church. . . . There is no need to say with what pain I heard a crier proclaiming that the Wesleyans were not to share in showing respect to the memory of their prince. That he would have approved such an act I do not believe. As for the order—the Wesleyans are not <b>(100)</b> to stir abroad except at night, and then again sending them away from the Fai-kava—these things show the spirit that actuates the Free Church, and will, with the exclusion from the funeral, stamp it as a <b>(101)</b> persecuting Church. . . .

I am also informed that the Wesleyans in Homuka have been banished <b>(104)</b> from the Islands with their minister. Their embarkation was to take place to-day. . . . The church, too, <b>(105)</b> has been taken from us.

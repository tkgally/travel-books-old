[[cont]]latter led the chair coolies to remark aside, “The Living Road is on the chair and is substantial, <i>i.e.</i>, heavy.” We stirred up several larks and stopped to drink delicious cool water at the Dragon Spring. In China it is unwise to drink even beautiful running water unless you <i>see</i> it is at the fountain head, for various reasons. In the great valley we were traversing, trees are plentiful. The Government has issued a proclamation advising people to plant trees, and promising official rank to all who plant ten thousand trees. Near Yakeo-l’ang on a tree, was hung a notice in poetry, which is here translated in prose for the “gentle reader.”

<verse>
“Yellow Heaven and Green Earth:
I have a squalling brat at home.
Will the gentleman passing by please read this verse?
And let me sleep till early dawn?”
</verse>

This request is seen all over Central China posted up on bridges, walls and hills. The idea that a reader’s baby will catch the “squalls” by one perusal is novel, if not altruistic. From this I gather that crying babies are the same all over the world.

The sun like a ball of fire dropped into the west, and by the light of burning forests on the mountain sides we entered Willow Woods, Yanglin.

“He also states that the high moral tone among the Japanese officers and men contributed to their success, and that they were saturated with a patriotism imbibed with their mothers’ milk. He also spoke highly of the simple life led by the highest ranking Japanese officers in contrast to that of the Russians.

“General Kuropatkin might have gone much further in his summary of causes which led to his universal failure to win battles. One of the greatest factors conducive to Japanese success was the excellent moral training of both officers and men and their temperate habits. Very few of the Japanese officers or men drank anything besides their native drink, <i>saké,</i> which is low in alcoholic potentiality, and none of them were hampered with lewd women either in garrison or field. On the other hand the Russians were excessive in the use of the very strongest alcoholic drinks and openly courted an immoral life. It is generally known that during most important and serious engagements officers of the highest rank found time to pay court to their mistresses who were permitted to accompany them to the field and were even provided with tents in which to live.

WE have now arrived at that period of Japanese history ‘when knighthood was in flower,’ and feudal lords ruled supreme. Since the legendary days of Jimmu Tenno the Japanese people has been a race of warriors and accustomed to deadly conflict on untold fields of sanguinary battle. For two thousand years the military spirit has been inbred into their very marrow and taught by heroic mothers from the moment they were able to lisp the sacred name of Mikado. For ages the object of life was to die for Dai-Nippon and to join the heavenly host of warriors whose martial and protective spirits hovered over their sacred soil. The spirit of national justice, a sense of right and wrong, was beginning to crystallize among the people and Kiyomori began to realize that he had far exceeded his prerogative as Kwambaku and ruler.

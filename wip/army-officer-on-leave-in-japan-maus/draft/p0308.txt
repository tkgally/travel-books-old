The most noted representative of the latter was Michizane, 890 <sc>a.d.</sc>, who became noted as the adviser and councillor to the Mikados Uda and Daigo. He adopted literature as a profession and became a brilliant scholar in Chinese classics and learning. Kwambaku Tokihara, the leading member of the Fujiwara family, became jealous of his prominence, so sent him to Dazaifu in the island of Kyushu as viceroy, which appointment was regarded as a political banishment. He died there in 903 <sc>a.d.</sc>, and was subsequently canonized under the name of Tenjin by the Shintos. Michizane is held sacred to-day by the nation as the patron saint of the literary guild.

“Japan had now become a feudal camp and the government was controlled by any chieftain who could wield the greatest power. We have now arrived at that most interesting period of Japanese history, namely the creation of the shogunate, which occurred in 1190. After the downfall of the Fujiwaras, the military class became an important part of the population and those who had an aptitude for arms gradually became distinct from the agricultural classes under the profession of the samurai. The struggles between the Tairas and Fujiwaras after the downfall of the latter, were transferred to the Minamotos and kept the country in a state of warfare for more than a hundred years. During this period gallant leaders sprang up from both these families and thus for a century or more the country was alternately ruled by members of these two clans.

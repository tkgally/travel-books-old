It was a quaint attempt to beguile and to fascinate. It was studied national art, followed solemnly, worked out religiously by the little mimic. Had she been performing the sacred rites to her dead ancestors, she could not have been more serious, more conscientious in her effort. Not a side glance, nor the shadow of a smile, betrayed a thought beyond the lesson. The coquetry of the fan drill showed the same stately dignity, almost stern in its exactness. Each twirl and twist, each flutter and turn, had weighty value, and must be made with thumb and fingers at the proper angle, with hands adjusted to a code of fan etiquette which was only known to the high bred. Any omission in the attitudinizing stamped the performer as a wretched bungler. The teacher's quick rap of the wand on the stool meant another fleet dash of light feet across the stage, and the lesson ended with low bows and airy flutters.

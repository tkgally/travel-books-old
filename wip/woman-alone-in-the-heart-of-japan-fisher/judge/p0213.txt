# Chapter VIII
[[subtitle]] An Inland Trip

## <i>Preparations for the Trip</i>

I<sc>f</sc> few tourists see Ikao, whose leafy groves and flowering fields are girt about by rugged mountains, seldom does the trotter burrow farther inland and penetrate to the wild mountain fastness, the centre of the boiling sulphur springs, seat of invalidism, and Mecca of the sufferer. It had long been the goal of my travels, a dream and ambition which were difficult of accomplishment, since obstacles there were many, hardships numerous, and companions none. But with the rising difficulties came increased desire. “Interesting! Wildly so, if you can stand the terrible sights, but I could not endure them myself,” said the experienced friend.

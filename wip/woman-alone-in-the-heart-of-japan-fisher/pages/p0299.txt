But I had not come for show-rooms, and only when I caught a stiff laboratory smell of alcohol did I feel sure that we were approaching anything like hospital wards. The laboratory was a storehouse for pans, jars, cases of organs internal and external, malformed, putrid, semi-gone, retained for examination and study. Specimens there were, enough to satisfy any lover of the monstrous, in this ghastly chamber of horrors. Every ailment in the catalogue of miseries has its sample.

The quiet corridors were restful, and the little nurses flitting about like gentle doves, in white uniforms and high French caps, with the red cross, were a happy relief to the gruesome den. The hospital staff, of three hundred women, had passed a training of three years. They live and mess in annexes of the hospital. They earn the small sum of eight yen a month for their services, and must pay five for board, so that their actual income is three yen, or one dollar and fifty cents per month.

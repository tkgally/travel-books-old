[[cont]]They crouch around the brass hebachi, that melancholy little kettle of ashes where flickering charcoal warms the outspread fingers. Later, they bring in the wares and draw the sliding wall of the little box which serves as home and store. One step up, they draw another panel, set with little panes of paper, and they spread rugs on the spotless matting, and the family goes fast asleep resting on little wooden pillows which would give us cramps for a week. Such is the life of the merchant, the average, well-to-do middle class. These are the midgets whose every phase of life is Lilliputian. Meagre and bare as it looks, it is the making of the brave soldier on the battle-field.

The little people of bows and smiles see no reason for our aggressive speech, pushing ways, abrupt manners. They have time to be polite. To them life means more leisure and less money. They linger long over a sale, and seem to care little if they make one. They love their treasures and know their worth, and the best are hidden away.

The water, running in from the hillside, so terribly hot and so impregnated with sulphur, passes rapidly down-stream, and thus is constantly changed, and the mineral destroys all germs, so that no contagion is feared from the community bath. The follies of youth, the madness of intermarriage, the sin of wild oats, are frightfully in evidence at the baths. Arms and legs are raw with ugly sores. Knees and armpits are eaten away by vile disease, the flesh is putrid and laid bare. These most afflicted parts are swathed in folds of soft cotton before and after entering the bath, as even the brave Japanese could not submit the open flesh and the exposed nerves to this awful heat.

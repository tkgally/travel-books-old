## <i>Miss Hewes</i>

To Miss Hewes, of Oxford, were owed the foreign contributions. In her around-the-world tour, she lectured extensively throughout the island, and taught English in the University. Her tactless assertion against the <i>“American-English”</i> was hard on the teachers, but let them watch their words and guard their lips, ere they too speedily repudiate the criticism, which certainly had a germ of truth. Extremely faulty English, among those who should be professional models, is a sad reflection on our public schools, and we are often tempted to ask, “Have the schools ceased to teach English?” When the teachers make the very usual errors of “like” for “as,” when they say “this much” and “those kind,” they condemn themselves, and they should correct the faults ere they bubble over with wrath and bewail the general charge. The criticism was sweeping and severe, and it worked havoc for American teachers among the Japanese, who want the best and who are easily influenced.

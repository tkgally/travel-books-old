I proceeded to my usual method of indicating famine, by placing one hand on my stomach and the other on my mouth. Here was a case where ordinary “signs and omens” failed. They took the feat as dramatic attitudinizing, and doubled up with mirth. Then I tried high art, and cackled like a hen, I thought. They did not recognize the barn-yard note, and fell into more merriment. I felt very much like a continuous vaudeville, but made another venture, this time in the realm of objective art, and drew a hen, as I supposed, with eggs, as these seemed simple, natural food in the country. But they did not recognize the bird, and indulged in more convulsions. Certainly the professional clown never extracted more spontaneous applause from his admiring audience.

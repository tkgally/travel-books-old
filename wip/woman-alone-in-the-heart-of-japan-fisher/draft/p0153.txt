Eight miles of stiff mountain road ran beside the racing river, or deep in the heart of the woods. There was hard scrambling for pushman and pullman, but no moan or murmur escaped them. They passed each rough spot with a laugh of triumph. They were reënforced with many sandals, and the road was paved with these relics of the runners, as they pulled on one new pair after another, and left the worn one in shreds by the road. The men knew every foot of the way, and flashed back a sunny smile for every appreciative word of the patron.

We ran through acres of bamboo grass, where young shoots striped in green and white threw a spring carpet in the azalea woods, whose bright flowers made rainbows in the air from trunks that were thirty feet high. It was the ideal June day of the poet, when every tree is new-gowned and the birds chant their pæan of praise.

## <i>The Lake Biwa Canal</i>

One commencement day, a college graduate of Tokio set all other graduates a worthy example, as he refused to sweep the fields of oratory with the usual flowery platitudes, and dealt with matters practical, that his erudition might be a blessing to his land. His essay, for the College of Engineering, gave birth to the Lake Biwa Canal, opened in 1890, as an invaluable highway for men and matter. The authorities saw the worth of his idea, and appointed the essayist to execute the scheme. The engineer’s right arm became paralyzed while drawing his plans, and he finished them with the left hand. It was a gigantic feat to carry the water up to Kioto by a canal seven miles long, which included three tunnels of a total length of two and a half miles through the very heart of the mountain.

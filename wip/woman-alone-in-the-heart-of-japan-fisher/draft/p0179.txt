On the wall hung the big drum, which had beaten many a tom-tom to departed ancestors. Opposite were shoguns’ emblems, beautiful swords with ivory sheaths. There were lunch-boxes of lacquer wrought in gold, layer after layer, connected by cords. I felt like a throned god when the little man placed me a mat on a lacquer lunch-box. He hardly spoke of his stately souvenirs. He loved them all and knew their worth. He had ransacked the country far north for such trophies, and he loved them as if they were his children and a part of his life. Tenderly he unwrapped the trifles, as we squatted in the dim light. There were teapots chased with shoguns’ crests, brocaded purses, inlaid pipes, wonderful sword-guards, ivories yellow with age, intricate figures carved in a solid piece, wrought in microscopic patterns, so delicate that only a glass revealed the perfection of their workmanship.

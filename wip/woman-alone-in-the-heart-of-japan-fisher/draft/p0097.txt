[[cont]]from either side sprang a giant, naked, except for a purple loin cloth and a fringe of stiff, silk spikes, which bristled like quills when the battle raged. His hair, gathered to a war-lock at the top, was tied with a cord above which it fluttered an inch, like the ruffled feathers of a fighting cock.

On the stage, each antagonist planted his hands firmly on his knees, stretched to a base like the Colossus of Rhodes, lifted in turn each leg to the highest pitch, and slammed it down with a thud. The extraordinary gesture seemed a weighty threat. Then the men faced each other, squatting on their heels, and glowering into each other’s eyes, like a couple of game roosters. After intense seconds of bated breath and desperate scowls, one flew at the other in fury, and, if the other was in no mood for war, the battle was off, and they retired to a corner to spit and drink, to blow the nose and mop themselves down with tissue-paper.

Bravely they bowled us beside the rushing waters, where the famed array of Buddhas never adds up twice the same. “There are certainly two thousand,” exclaimed the novice. But at least there were two hundred gods grinning by the roadside with folded hands and placid smile and look of supernal wisdom, as if to say of passing tourists, “What fools these mortals be!” Moss and lichens drape their saintly forms, and, though the head of a deity may have rolled in the mud, his staunch figure retains its stately pose. Years ago, the patriarch of the gods rode down-stream in a washout, whirling among the boiling eddies, but he landed right side up at the next village, where to-day he wears a red bib, and is worshipped for his triumphal journey by the passing peasants.

Then came the trainers, with the sacred monkey who did not like holy processions, and hid his face in the folds of his master’s gown, as if blushing for all of his descendants.

Though the Shinto priests may not be followers of Darwin, the tricky quadruped is an important feature in Nikko’s history. His fame is wide throughout Japan, and, though he does not run wild in the woods, nor hurl sticks on the coming tourist, as many anticipate, he is a holy symbol, carved in three attitudes on the sacred stable dedicated to the great Ieyasu, where he piously shuts his ears and eyes and mouth to evil.

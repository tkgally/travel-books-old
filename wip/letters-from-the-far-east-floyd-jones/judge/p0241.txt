And should they fail to grease the palms of his subordinates, some of their weak points will be brought to light, and in place of paying a moderate sum it may cost them many thousands of <i>taels</i> to escape with their heads. The Chief Inspector of Trade at Canton holds his appointment for three years. At the time of retiring he visits Pekin on the accustomed visit to the Emperor, and he rarely gets off with less than 100,000 taels (about $142,000), and, besides it, he has probably put away a large fortune for future use, all of which has been made out of a salary of $2,500. I would not repeat these stories had I not heard the same thing from different sources in various parts of China, and from parties whom I have reason to credit.

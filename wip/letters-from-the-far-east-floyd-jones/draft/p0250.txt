[[cont]]Major-General Cameron, commanding the British troops in China, turned out his marines and a share of his sailors, and escorted the General to the race-course, where he inspected a body of the Shanghai volunteers. The blue-jackets looked and marched well, and were highly complimented. This act of courtesy struck me as being well placed, and a good promoter of friendly feeling between the two nations, who have so much in common; besides, it gave the captain an opportunity of showing the Chinese that, in addition to his naval guns, he was prepared to land a respectable force, and do some fighting on shore.

The two hundred in the cabin, were chiefly composed of returning Californian bankers, merchants, and tradesmen, whose fictitious successes or genuine means seemed to justify the expense of the apocryphal luxury of a first-class passage. Aged matrons, youthful expectant mothers, and a swarming host of tokens of conjugal affection—children of all ages—secured to the well-wisher of his race the daily prospect of all the domestic relations from fond connubiality to conjugal satiety. There was a runaway couple from the West, who were provokingly tender on every public occasion, and several experienced pairs, who did not await a private opportunity to exhibit their mutual discontent.

Ten days at sea will disclose more of intimate character than a life-time on land.

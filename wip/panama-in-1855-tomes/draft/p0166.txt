On a clear noonday, looking from the high-walled fortifications of Panama, southward down a broad avenue of the gulf, formed by green islands on either side, the view closes upon Taboga, some three leagues away; its pyramidal summits look purple in the distance, and their outlines marked on the blue sky, show through the clear air as sharply traced as a drawing. The smart little steamer <i>Taboga</i> will whisk you away there within the hour; a ship’s cutter, with four stout tarpaulin-Jacks, will pull you there in double that time; a bungo, with a fair wind and the ebb tide in its favor, may roll there in half a day; and a canoe, with a quartette of paddlers, in nature’s sable suit, with much screeching, hard paddling, great expense of oil and sweat, and unlimited pulls at the aguardiente, will reach there somewhile in the course of time.

The construction of the road was undertaken upon too narrow a basis, as to expenditure of money and labor. Stimulated by the hope of immediate gain, from the gold discovery, which was attracting the world to California, and flooding the Isthmus with an emigration which poured in torrents from the Atlantic to the Pacific, the commercial originators of the enterprise thought only of the tribute they might extort from the hurrying crowds of eager adventurers, or the gold they might glean from the rich harvest of the diggings of the Sacramento. The great object was to secure an immediate benefit from what was believed to be a temporary occasion. The practicability of a road was soon proved by the surveys of competent engineers, and estimates made of the cost. The former pleased Wall Street, for it showed the speculation feasible; the latter were received with dissatisfaction, for they threw doubt upon its paying. Accordingly, engineer after engineer was dismissed, until one was found whose arithmetic accorded with the close calculations of avaricious trade.

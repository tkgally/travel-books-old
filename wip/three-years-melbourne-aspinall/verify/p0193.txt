I must confess to having taken rather a fancy to the poor Chinamen. They seemed to be just as good-tempered as Englishmen are in general; indeed, if I may declare the truth, I thought that they betrayed more equanimity and meekness of disposition than do many of my own countrymen. They are patient and industrious, though not energetic, and they prefer settling down on those “diggings” which have been deserted by Europeans. The only crime with which I ever heard them charged was that of stealing—especially from poultry yards.

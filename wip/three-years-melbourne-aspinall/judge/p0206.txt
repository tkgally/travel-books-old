I was more than ever struck with John Chinaman’s perfect good-temper and meekness. Whenever the passengers wanted anything done, they ordered John to do it. “Open the door, John!” said one woman; “Put down that blind!” said another; “Shut that window!” said a third; and each time he obeyed promptly. At last, by way of trying John’s patience to the uttermost extent, a frolicsome young man, who sat behind him, began to pull his pig-tail, and taking out a pen-knife pretended that he was going to cut it off. Now a Chinaman loves his pig-tail better than his life; it is a badge of honour and respectability, and if he returned to his country without it he would be tabooed and scouted.

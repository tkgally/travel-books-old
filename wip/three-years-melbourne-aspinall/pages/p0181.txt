[[cont]]These wild flowers are delicately small, so that they do not attract the eye in riding through the forest; at least, such was the case in those parts of the country which I happened to see. The yellow wattle-flower is, however, an exception to this rule, as it presents a very gay appearance, and also fills the air with its delicious perfume. The most beautiful flower which I saw in Australia was the <i>Clianthus Dampiera</i>, or Captain Sturt’s desert pea,—not in the least like a pea, it being much more like a drooping cactus, only smaller. It hangs in clusters, and its colour is bright red, with a dark purple eye. This flower grew most luxuriantly in the gardens about Kyneton, as did every other native or British flower; this neighbourhood rejoices in a rich black soil—the only good soil I happened to see in Australia.

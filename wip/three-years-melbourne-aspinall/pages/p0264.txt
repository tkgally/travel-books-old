There is little daylight in these icy latitudes, and the sky is dull and leaden, so that it is sometimes difficult, even for those who are well enough to read, to beguile the weary hours by a book. For the comfort of sea-sick sufferers, I may state that during the greater part of the time in which “the Horn” is being rounded they will be confined to their berths, and therefore will not feel the cold.

The passengers, however, keep up their spirits wonderfully through it all, knowing that it will not last long, and that soon it will be hot enough. Exactly thirty-three days after we left Melbourne, I find recorded in my journal that “it was quite a spring day,”—just the sort of day on which at home one suddenly discovers gay little groups of crocuses peering out of their winter quarters.

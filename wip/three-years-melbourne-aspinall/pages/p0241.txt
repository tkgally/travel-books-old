Still, talent, with energy, will generally make its way; and those who feel, as did Sheridan before he made his great speech, that they <i>have it in them and it shall come out</i>, would doubtless succeed in any of these professions.

With regard to the medical profession, I certainly think that such men as <i>Sir James Clarke, Dr. Jenner, Mr. Stone,</i> and <i>Mr. Bowman,</i> would be acquisitions in Melbourne; but then I do not think that they would get so well remunerated for their labours as in London. It struck me very much that the medical men in the colony had rather a hard life of it, and were not proportionably well paid, as in England, taking into account the enormous house-rent in Melbourne, and the expense of keeping there any kind of vehicle.

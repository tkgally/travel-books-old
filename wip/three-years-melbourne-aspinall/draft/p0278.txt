On the day of our arrival two of them came and stood by me in silence for some time, with their eyes mournfully fixed upon the Welsh mountains. “Well, it can’t be helped!” said one at length, as she turned round and fondly gazed at the sea which we were leaving behind us. “What can’t be helped?” I asked. “Oh, it can’t be helped,” she answered, “that we have to leave the dear ship and the Captain, and everybody that we like!”

I must not omit to inform my readers that we had a doctor. All ships have a doctor, but they have not all one like ours, who combined so many of the qualities desirable in a ship-surgeon.

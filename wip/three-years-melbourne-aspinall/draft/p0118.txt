The bed-rooms were small as compared with the sitting-rooms, but cool and lofty. The kitchen was just about large enough to contain an American stove, tables, and two good-tempered Irish servants. English servants accustomed to English comforts would not, I am sure, have stayed in it, for, small as it was, there were three doors to it, which admitted frightful draughts and gusts of wind in the cold weather. Out of this kitchen was a little room for servants. Nearly all houses that were built a few years since have the same kind of miserable little kitchen, with a room for servants leading out of it,—sometimes this room of extremely small dimensions.

The same man told me that he was the best whisky-drinker thereabouts, and that he drank nothing else, as it made him popular. Now, how whisky-drinking could add to his popularity I am unable to conceive.

On the way, we stopped at a very neat little farm-house, at the edge of an almost circular cornfield. Nearly the whole crop had been trampled down and destroyed, and the owner informed us it had all been done by grizzly bears. These animals are very fond of sucking the ripe ears of corn, and will tread down acres in a single night. Of all beasts that walk the earth a grizzly is the most formidable, and it is a difficult matter to get even professional hunters to pursue them. The immense masses of muscle and fat over their vital organs render them exceedingly difficult to kill outright, and a wounded one is a terrible opponent. They can run as fast as a horse, and their prodigious strength enables them to crush a bull as easily as if he were a squirrel. However, they seldom attack man, unless they are wounded or have their cubs with them. They are then very dangerous and savage.

By means of spears and pistols, the miserable brutes (I refer to the cattle) are so lacerated that they are soon exhausted by loss of blood and the frantic pace at which they have tried to elude their pursuers. When one falls, the Indians try to urge it on, by goading, to another race; but when they fail in this, they stand over the fallen beast and torment it until death ends its miseries. The squaws are then turned out to get the carcases into camp and cut them up. There are sometimes frightful contests among them for choice portions of the animal; and a couple of them may frequently be seen hacking at each other with knives over a tender sirloin. When the last animal is killed, the Indians return to their lodges; their bloodthirstiness whetted to such an edge that the only wonder is that they can be restrained from massacring the entire post.

The Indian Department still does the most extraordinary things. Very lately, two chiefs, named respectively, Santanta and Big Tree, both vile Indian murderers, were captured and imprisoned, instead of being hanged. After some correspondence between the Governor of Texas and the Indian Department, in which the latter almost begged that the prisoners should be released, Santanta and Big Tree were restored to their miserable tribes.

It seems—and I ought to have mentioned it before—that a very exciting scene had taken place in the Republican Valley, not far from Omaha, a few days before we arrived there. The Pawnees had prepared for a buffalo-hunt. Their enemies, the Sioux, being aware of this, stripped their ponies and distributed them round an adjoining hill, to lead the Pawnees to believe them to be buffaloes. As soon as the Pawnee ‘<i>braves</i>’ left their camp the Sioux immediately attacked it, and succeeded in killing about fifty squaws before the warriors returned. When they did return they fought bravely for about ten hours; but they were at last obliged to retreat, and fled across the river, having lost over a hundred men. The Sioux loss amounted to about thirty. The soldiers from Fort M’Pherson arrived after the fight, and drove the Sioux back to their ‘reservation.’

The Pawnees were at Elm Creek Station when we arrived, and I never saw more miserable-looking objects.

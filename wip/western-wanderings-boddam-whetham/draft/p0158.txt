Having crossed the bridge over the main stream, we presently arrived at a most picturesque ford. Oaks, maples, and cottonwood trees, overarched the broad shallow stream, and in the background the lower fall of the Yosemite was descending in a sheet of mist and spray, behind a dark middle distance of firs and pines. As we approached the Falls a change of temperature was perceptible, and a heavy shower of spray soon brought us to a stand-still. When seen from such close quarters they are certainly very grand. Overhanging mountains of solid granite hemmed us in, huge boulders of sharp, angular rocks lay scattered about, and ferns, flowers, and grasses grew in profusion all around. The great mass of falling water, apparently grown so wide now we were near it, seemed more like a snowy avalanche than a liquid stream; and we lingered there for some hours, gazing on this wonderful scene of wood and and water, and even then we were loth to leave. But our appetites finally prevailed, and we returned to the hotel for luncheon.

For dress they delight in gaily-coloured, well-made mats, or rude kilts of cocoanut or pandanus leaves. Their taste for decoration is shown in the necklaces and belts made of innumerable rings sometimes wonderfully minute, cut out of cocoanut-shell, highly polished and sewn on cord made of the cocoanut fibre. To the necklaces a large disc of burnished tortoise-shell is often attached and the native “masher” is then complete. Men wear the hair long, and in some of the westward islands tied up in chignons of such size that, but for the thin beard and moustache, it is impossible to avoid mistaking them for women. The women, on the other hand, often let their hair hang loosely down. This long, straight hair is characteristic of the Polynesian. Of the Papuan race, with its crisp, wiry hair, I saw no sign on any of the lagoon islands.

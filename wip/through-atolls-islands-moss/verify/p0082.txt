Copra is largely sought by Hamburg merchants on whose trade the much-talked-of German interests in the Pacific are based. No German mission, no German exploring expedition, no German sacrifice of any kind for the benefit of the natives has accompanied their trade in copra. On commerce alone can Germany rest her claim. Commerce sounds well, but call it business and the odour is less sweet. Visions of natives duped, of base Bolivian dollars made island currency and of hard grinding in all directions, rise at the name. This is surely no fitting foundation for a great nation’s claims, nor justification for high-handed measures such as have been adopted by Germany towards the kindly, English-loving people of Samoa.

The influence of England, of America, and of France has been bought with the blood of martyrs, the work of missionaries and explorers and the friendly and constant intercourse of their people with the islanders of the Pacific. Of these three nations England, in the extent and strength of her influence, is supreme. English is the only language which the natives know besides their own.

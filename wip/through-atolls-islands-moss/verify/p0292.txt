The Assembly passed laws organizing various departments, and did much useful work. It sanctioned an expenditure for the ensuing year of 25,866<i>l.</i> of which sum “H.M. Privy Purse” absorbed 1500<i>l.</i>, “H.M. Private Secretary,” 200<i>l.</i>, and “H.M. Ministers and Staff,” 2500<i>l.</i> Ten native chiefs, as “Governors of Provinces,” received 300<i>l.</i> each, and ten natives as “Lieut.-Governors,” 50<i>l.</i> each. For the “Chief Justice and Chancellor of the Kingdom,” 750<i>l.</i> was appropriated, and for a “Puisne Judge,” 500<i>l.</i> A temporary loan of 5000<i>l.</i> was authorized to meet present emergencies, and the Assembly was dismissed in due form.

The Assembly had done much good and useful work, but two fatal mistakes were made. The Customs Act gave to the Government a permanent independent revenue, and another Act authorized them to raise armed forces and to discipline the same without requiring annual renewal. In effect, a Mutiny Act in perpetuity. The Constitution contained eloquent declarations about human rights, but the Assembly had deprived itself of all safeguards for their preservation.

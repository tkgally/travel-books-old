Runaway sailors began to settle among them, often men of fair character escaping from tyranny and ill-treatment on board and doing much good among the natives who were in manners, morals, and knowledge, far inferior to these Europeans. The good which the natives derived from whalers far outweighed the evil, and to the intercourse of whalemen is due the breaking down of savage barriers more than to any other cause or people.

Escaped convicts from Australia soon made their appearance among the natives. They were a desperate and dangerous class from whom as a rule no good could come. Happily there were exceptions, men of better character expatriated for poaching and minor offences and escaping at the hazard of their lives from the cruel brutality of a convict <i>régime</i>. Happily, too, the worst of these men were short-lived. They killed each other, died of their own debauchery, or were killed by the natives, the usual end of such men and well illustrated by the history of the mutineers of the <i>Bounty</i>.

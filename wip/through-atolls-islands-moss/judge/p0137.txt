Their general detestation of the practice is so marked as to suggest the thought that the Maoris and Raratongans may have grown to it by contact with the Papuan races with whom they came into conflict.

Passing over many hundred miles of ocean covered with islands occupied by races of mixed Polynesian and Papuan blood, with perhaps a strong dash of the Hindoo, one suddenly falls upon the pure Maori in the little lagoon island of Nukuor, situate in the centre of the Caroline Archipelago. The Nukuor language is Maori of the oldest and purest kind, easily seen despite the constant change in the application of words which is characteristic of uncivilized nations. I regretted that our stay at Nukuor was too short to enable me to make any inquiry as to their traditions, but learned that they too had never been cannibals.

It is notorious that the Manihiki women are always seeking to leave the island, and that they have to be closely watched lest they should escape, when trading-vessels call there. Who can wonder that this should be? And if those who have the heart and the brains and the power do not find a remedy, who will further wonder if the reaction should grow stronger and the last state of these poor people be worse than the first, worse than the original state from which the devotion of many a missionary has so far raised them? In these distant lagoon islands where the population is so sparse and European missionaries with their families cannot dwell, the responsibility of those who send the native teachers to guide and practically rule these people is a responsibility of no light kind, for upon the native teachers, and upon their training and character, the future must depend.

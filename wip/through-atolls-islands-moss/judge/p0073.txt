The earliest records of Spanish conquest in the Pacific are connected with the pearl islands in the Bay of Panama. In 1517, only four years after De Balboa first sighted the great sea, Ponce de Leon caused timber to be carried across the isthmus and built a small craft with which to make the conquest of the pearl islands. The natives were cruelly treated and forced to give up 800 ounces of pearls found in their possession. They were further ordered to pay a yearly tribute of the same quantity. Of course this payment was impossible, as those found in their possession were the accumulated treasure of many years. They must have been as reckless as their descendants in opening the oyster, for the Spaniards afterwards discovered that many of the pearls were badly discoloured through the shells being opened by fire, a discovery which excited their just indignation at “the wicked carelessness” of the savages of those islands.

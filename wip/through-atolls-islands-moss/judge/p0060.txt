The Milli atoll, about thirty miles long and twelve to fifteen broad, cannot be seen from a ship’s deck in the clearest weather at a greater distance than ten to twelve miles. Even then it is only the tops of the tall cocoanut-trees on the islets that are visible. The land is about twelve feet above the sea, as a rule, but in some parts a few feet higher. Approaching Milli, the long line of breakers is discerned as the ocean beats with fury upon the outer edge of the massive reef. A narrow brown line gradually coming into view, marks the belt of shallow water, a couple of hundred yards wide, covering the surface of the reef between the breakers and the white beaches of pure coral sand which border the little islets on the reef. The houses of the natives, picturesque and cool, soon peep out from the thick green cocoanut groves.

The platform, and the creek embankments, are as clearly useless for defence as these vaults would be for dungeons or buccaneer’s treasure-chambers, as they have been respectively styled in old narratives. The opinion started on hearsay I fancy by Dumont D’Urville, that these buildings were put up by the buccaneers has been repeated in various works but will not stand investigation. No civilized people could have wasted time and labour in raising such a structure, but that some powerful savage chief should have conceived the idea of increasing his glory and perpetuating his name by a temple to his gods, and that his people should have eagerly abetted him, is quite intelligible. Far more inexplicable are the miles of careful embankment round the islets, forming into veritable canals the ocean-streamlets that meander through them. That the so-called vaults may have been designed for holding sacrifices, human or otherwise, and the passages through the walls intended to admit them, is also a reasonable surmise. One can even imagine them as receptacles for fuel, or in other ways connected with savage rites, but certainly not as hiding-places for a buccaneer’s plunder.

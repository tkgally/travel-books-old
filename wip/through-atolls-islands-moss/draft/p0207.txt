If the power rested with me, he should be made a bishop straightway—only, by the bye, he belongs to a church strongly anti-episcopal. His case was perfectly clear. The Maoris are mad for racing, and surely it is better that their sports be carried on at home under the eye of a venerated pastor, than that they should fall into the hands of a drinking, gambling class, to learn evil and be fleeced to the last farthing.

At Lukunor, in the Carolines, we found at anchor the American mission-vessel <i>Morning Star</i>. It was Sunday and they sent us an invitation to attend the afternoon service. An American lady who had come in the vessel on a visit from Boston, played the harmonium, and we had a capital sermon from the Rev. Robert Logan. The <i>Morning Star</i> sailed next day, leaving Mr. Logan to visit the adjacent islands in native canoes, and to find his way back to his chief station Ruk, 120 miles distant, in a small half-decked boat of six or seven tons.

The owner received us most hospitably and we had a comfortable night’s rest, none the less pleasant that the wind howling outside made the house delightfully cool, and effectually banished mosquitos.

At daylight, after a cup of excellent coffee and a breakfast of the renowned tinned meat of our own New Zealand, we started gaily and with every prospect of a splendid day. A run of a few miles, with a favourable wind, soon carried us into Metalaneum Harbour and past the Missionary Station which looked picturesque and homely among the fine trees on the beautiful hill-slope on which it stands. At nine o’clock we were running among a number of little low verdant islets, scattered for some miles over the shallow waters of the coral reef and distant about a mile from the high volcanic shore. The islets were thickly covered with trees, and, threading our way through them we came to a space, about 250 feet square, so regular that it looked at once as if formed by the hand of man.

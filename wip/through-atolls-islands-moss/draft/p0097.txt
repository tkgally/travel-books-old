The ships engaged in this trade were from India or Sydney and the profits were enormous. The sandal-wood cost on an average 10<i>l.</i> to 12<i>l.</i> per ton, and was sold for 60<i>l.</i> to 70<i>l.</i> in China. Fortunes were made by a few shipowners in this trade and the earnings of the men were large, but it was probably the least beneficial to the natives of any in which they engaged. Chiefs would dispute the right of ownership in those by whom the wood had been cut and sold. Or they would endeavour to murder and rob for the sake of plunder, as savages will mostly do. Hence war to the knife was common with those engaged in the sandal-wood trade and none but old ships, only fit to carry timber, were risked in the adventure. This trade lasted beyond the middle of the present century, since when sandal-wood has become more scarce and the quantity obtained comparatively small.

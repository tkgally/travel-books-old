The captain warned the natives to keep off, and took supplies from them in his boats half way from the shore. But they managed to steal some sacks from the boats one day, and that was how the small-pox got into Ponapè. The houses are small and poor and only of one room, so that the disease must have proved a terrible scourge among the people.

The Ponapè canoes are very good, owing to the fine timber which enables the natives to use solid tree-trunks, instead of being obliged to build the hull with small planks fastened by twisted cocoanut fibre as in the atolls and other less favoured islands. As we enter the harbour of Jamestown, the first station established by the American Mission under the Rev. W. Doane is a prominent object in the view. The buildings are extensive and stand on a green-clad promontory, but are now in charge of a native teacher, as Mr. Doane is stationed at Metalaneum Harbour on the other side of the island, about twenty miles from Jamestown.

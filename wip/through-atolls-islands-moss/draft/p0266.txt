An apprentice in the missionary brig <i>Camden</i>, when John Williams was killed at Erromanga, in 1839, he has a vivid recollection of the sad affair. On board were Mr. Williams, Mr. Harris an intending missionary, and a Mr. Cunningham. Williams understood natives, did not like their manner at Erromanga, and thought it would be prudent not to go ashore at once. Harris was impatient, and Williams gave way. The three passengers landed with the captain, and Harris walked in advance up a little stream. Presently he ran back, followed by a number of natives who knocked him down and clubbed him. The other two and the captain were also seen making for the boat. Cunningham and the captain got in and escaped, but Mr. Williams fell on the shingly beach and was slain.

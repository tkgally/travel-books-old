On Sunday these natives used frequent prayers and hymns in a manner so simple and unpretending as to prove them sincere. They were a pleasant, well-behaved and cheerful people. Nor were they in the least an annoyance though moving about in all parts of the vessel with a freedom that would have been very shocking to cabin passengers travelling with their own countrymen and countrywomen, under the same conditions, in any other part of the world.

These Rotumah, Penrhyn, and Namarik people are fair types of island natives in their several ways. But we had also on board a native from Ocean Island, taken up at Majuro to be landed at Pleasant Island whence he hoped to get back to his own little home.

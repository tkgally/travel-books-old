And, thinking over Ellis’s words, I am bound to say that, if the choice had to be made, I would ten thousand times rather start to-morrow as a missionary among these savages than do the work which I have seen clergymen, with delicately-bred wives and families, nobly face in the squalor and foul air of the East of London.

I should like to have more room for the shrewd and suggestive comments of this rough trader. Let us turn from him and his pleasant Manihiki people to another type of the races which inhabit the Pacific, to a people still heathen, and to chiefs, happily becoming rare, on whose word life and death still hang. Among these potentates, who is so worthy of note as King Jibberik, a very great man in his way and undisputed and despotic monarch of the best part of the Majuro Lagoon in the Marshall Islands. At least he was so till last year, for he then became—although he does not clearly understand it yet—a subject of the German Empire.

<i>Mem.</i> 2.—At the same meeting it was decided, after a long discussion, that the Society be called “The British Subjects’ Mutual Protection Society.”

<i>Mem.</i> 3.—On the next day those present at the meeting (fourteen) signed this pledge: “We, the undersigned settlers in Fiji, hereby pledge ourselves to mutual assistance, in our determination to depose the present Government with a view to the future annexation of Fiji to Great Britain, and the immediate reduction of the unreasonable expenses of the present administration.”

Here follow the signatures, now increased from fourteen to forty-six, not including Ba, Nadi, and Nadroga.

<i>Mem.</i> 4.—A Committee was appointed to canvass in Levuka for persons willing to subscribe the above pledge. The committee, consisting of White, Keyse, Beatson, Wecker, Townsend, Baily, E. S. Smith, and Fullerton.

<i>Note.</i>—It is now so evident, in spite of the opinion of Mr Thurston, that the country, almost to a man, is desirous of annexation and retrenchment, that we hope <i>local Committees</i> will be formed, who will work heart and hand with the Society at its headquarters.

<i>Mem.</i> 5.—T. W. White was appointed President, H. J. Beatson, Vice-President, and James Fullerton, Secretary of the Society.

When we left New Zealand (August, 1886) nothing was known of the mutual declaration signed at Berlin on the 6th April by Sir Edward Malet on behalf of England and by Count Herbert Bismarck on behalf of Germany, but I saw a copy at Jaluit, attached to the Report of the German New Guinea Company. Since my return I have also noticed a congratulatory article by Mr. C. Kinloch Cooke in the <i>Nineteenth Century</i> of November, 1886. Mr. Cooke suggests a similar mutual declaration between England and France as to their respective spheres in the rest of the Pacific. It is to be hoped that our Government will make itself acquainted with the true state of affairs before moving in that direction, or they may be led into making some serious sacrifice—surrendering, for example, Suwarrow Island, which gives us an alternative connecting link with the future canal at Panama or Nicaragua.

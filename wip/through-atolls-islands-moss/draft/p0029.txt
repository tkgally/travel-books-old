All had left who could and no settler now thinks of making a home in that beautiful and fertile group. Nor is any likely to do so while the present un-English and degrading system of government is suffered to exist.

In New Zealand and in Fiji I had gained some knowledge of the Southern and Central Pacific, but the islands and myriad islets of the outer lagoon world were a sealed book still. They are not easily visited, the only communication being by casual trading-vessels each with its own distinct line of traffic. Gladly availing myself of an offer from the well-known Auckland firm of Henderson and Macfarlane, I took passage in the schooner <i>Buster</i> which they were sending to make, for the first time, a complete round of their old stations and to find proper sites for establishing new. There was the further advantage that in a trading-vessel the natives would be seen as they are, and not as they get themselves up for the passing visit of a man-of-war, a High Commissioner, or an inspecting Missionary.

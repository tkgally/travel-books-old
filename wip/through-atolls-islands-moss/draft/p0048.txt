From Christmas Island we again turned south and called at Swain’s Island, a small coral gem not three miles long and about one mile broad. Swain’s is a little distant from the Union group and was taken up about twenty-five years ago by Mr. Eli Jennings, an American, who settled upon the islet with his wife the daughter of a Samoan chief. He planted cocoanuts and made as much oil as want of capital and the cumbrous processes of the period allowed. By-and-by the system of drying the nut for exportation was introduced into the islands by the German firm of Godeffroi and Co. The great difficulty of getting casks and the extra labour in making and handling the oil were thus avoided. Mr. Jennings increased his plantations and worked with new energy till his death about ten years ago. His sons followed in his footsteps

The population is returned in the missionary census as 5070 resident, with 503 absentees all married men, and temporarily at other islands. About one-half of the population consists of adult men and women, the other half of children. The decomposed coral soil is shallow but very productive, and Niuè yams are renowned for their quality. The fruits and coffee, the cotton, sugar-cane and arrowroot are all of the best description, while the climate is cool, dry, and admirable.

We spent three pleasant days at Niuè, the most pleasant of the voyage. The island has the advantage of a resident missionary and his family and the effect is apparent in the manners and habits of the people. If all missionaries were like the Rev. Frank Lawes of Niuè, large-minded, sympathetic and educated men, the native teachers who are trained and sent forth to smaller islands would be very different to many of whose strange pranks I shall have to speak by-and-by.

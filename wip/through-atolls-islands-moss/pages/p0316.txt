The French blockade of Hamburg and the commercial derangement of the time, involved Godeffrois in difficulty. The collapse of their Pacific trade was imminent. They obtained, it was said, from a great London financial firm an advance of 60,000<i>l</i>., on the security of their Pacific property. This enabled them to keep the numerous agencies supplied with stores and money till a new company could be formed to take up the large business they had built with so much energy and care. The company was formed, the “Deutsche Handels- und Plantagen-Gesellschaft zum Süd-See Inseln,” to which I have before referred as the “Long Handle Co.,” by which designation it is, in the Pacific, universally known. The business established by Godeffrois is still carried on by this company, who own the fine cocoanut plantations, and have agencies and stores everywhere.

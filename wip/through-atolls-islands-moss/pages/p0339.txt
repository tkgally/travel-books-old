# Appendix A.
[[subtitle]] Showing How Land Titles Were Registered in Fiji in 1862.

T<sc>he</sc> following documents show the mode in which titles to land were originally acquired and registered in Fiji:—

Ko i au na Vuni Valus, au sa vakadonuia na vanua ni Kai Papalagi i Viti na Vanua sa vakadonuia i na vale ni Konisela Peretania. Au sa vola oqo e Levuka, Ovalau.

[[right]] 2 <i>Apirila</i>, 1862.

[[right]] E. C<sc>akobau</sc>,

[[right]] X

[[right]] V<sc>uni</sc> V<sc>alu</sc>.

V<sc>akadinadina</sc>,

W<sc>ill</sc>. T. P<sc>ritchard</sc>, <i>H.B.M. Consul.</i>

C<sc>has</sc>. W<sc>ise</sc>, <i>Consular Interpreter.</i>

Witnesses to mark of Cakobau,

[[right]] J<sc>ames</sc> C<sc>alvert</sc>,

[[right]] J. M. B<sc>rower</sc>, <i>U.S. Consul.</i>

[[center]] [<i>Translation.</i>]

I, the Vuni Valu, do confirm the lands of the foreigners in Fiji—the lands that are confirmed in the office of the British Consul.

L<sc>evuka</sc> O<sc>valau</sc>,

2 <i>April</i>, 1862.

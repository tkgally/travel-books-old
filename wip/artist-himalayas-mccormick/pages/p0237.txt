[[cont]]Gurkhas had built for him. He said that he expected to be able to catch us up at Askole, and in the expectation of his doing so we went on our way, leaving him a small tent and provisions. However, it was a fortnight before he recovered. This was a very unfortunate incident, and it was extremely difficult to know what to do—whether to stay or go on. But camping there would have meant the loss of very valuable time, and expense, and yet going on seemed selfish. It is one of those things bound to happen in such expeditions, and one must reckon on them and take one's chance, just as one must look for tumbles, the risk of a crevasse or a broken rope on the ice. Bruce, I think, understood the situation, and bore it as he did everything else. But we all deeply regretted leaving him, though he had his faithful and affectionate Gurkhas to look after his wants.

Of late, people who know very little have called him “a man of one book.” In remembering his travels they have forgotten his “Wood-cutters of the Netherlands,” a most learned and valuable contribution to the history of engraving, to say nothing of other equally important works.[[fnref *]] But to those who went with him into the Himalayas he certainly stands more as the man of deeds than the man of words. His endurance of toil, his tact in the management of many races of men, his unfailing readiness of resource, and his absolute foresight, mark him as one among a thousand. That he made the expedition a success we all know, but few know the difficulties with which he contended, nor the happy, bright temper with which he combated them.

[[cont]]like old Gothic stone-carved saints from the niches of some grey cathedral, their long loose blankets hanging festooned to the ground from their uplifted arms. A rare and fantastic group they made, standing on the dirty soft snow, the great dark ridges behind rising up into the clouds which eddied and flowed about their hidden summits.

At last I asked them what they were pleased to require.

“Ah, Sahib, the road is bad and we have no food.”

I remarked that they were a pack of liars, and that they had cooked food at Haigutum to last them ten days, by which time we should be at Askole, on the other side, where we would provide them with plenty. Their spokesman’s Hindustani was about as good as mine, so we perfectly understood each other. I gave him a little English extra, and he gave me a little Yashkun, but his main cry was, that the road was bad and we should be lost, and all cried it in chorus.

I told them to go away, and woke Conway up. He blessed me for waking him, and at his “Go, go,” they went, most of the younger members laughing.

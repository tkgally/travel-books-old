Conway and the rest, not having yet tried the security of their “Whymper” tents, took shelter in an old, dust-littered shelter-house, but Jack and I, conscious of a strong Cabul tent over us, remained and watched the hills and valleys blotted out, and when the rain was upon us we lay and listened to the downpour. The storm, which was heavy, passed away during the night, and a bright morning saw us on our way upwards to the Rajdiangan Pass. Most of us made straight up the mountain by the old track, but the coolies and servants kept to the well-made mule-road. As we climbed higher and higher, the view opened, and its beauty well repaid our troublesome march. Sometimes I saw beautiful bits of colour, such as trees in blossom, against the deep blue sky, and then came a dark grove of pines sunk in the shadow of the high hills with the heads of flowers seen against the dark purple of its depths.

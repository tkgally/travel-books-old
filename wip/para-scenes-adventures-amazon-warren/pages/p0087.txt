Among the passers by we noticed a man of wonderful corpulency jogging slowly through the street, while with one hand he was wiping away the thick drops of perspiration that had gathered on his massive brow. “That man,” said a gentleman present, “has had <i>three wives.</i>” “Three wives!” ejaculated a merry Scotchman at our elbow, “by heavens! he looks as if he had <i>eaten them all.</i>”

Many of the houses in the city still bear marks of the late disturbances. That of Mr. Norris, an intelligent and hospitable American merchant, is perhaps the most notable in this respect. Being a very lofty building, it was used as a kind of fort, and garrisoned by the president’s guard. Some of the upper window-blinds were completely riddled with bullets, and in the garden, Mr. N. informed me, that he found a quantity of balls, of from half a pound to a pound in weight. These were probably thrown from the vessels then lying in the harbor.

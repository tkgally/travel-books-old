On account of the revolutionary spirit of the people, a large military force of regular troops is distributed throughout the province. The number in the city alone cannot be less than eight hundred or a thousand. At all the important posts of the city, such as the palace, custom-house, and arsenal, guards are stationed, who may be seen walking about listlessly during the day, with huge muskets on their shoulders, or stretched out before the doorway itself, in a state of half intoxication and repose. On a certain evening, it is said, that as an inebriated Yankee sailor was perambulating the streets of the city, serenading the inhabitants as he reeled along, he was suddenly hailed by one of the custom-house guards, as he was making a short tack to carry himself past that establishment, with “Quem vai la?” (who goes there?) to which question the customary reply is, “Amigo” (a friend).

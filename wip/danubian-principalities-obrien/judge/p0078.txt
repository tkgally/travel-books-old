# Chapter VIII.

W<sc>hen</sc> an occasional English tourist arrives at Bucharest, it is more generally the effect of accident than design; and notwithstanding the attractive name of the city, and the fascinating flavour of the Dimbovitza, he jolts off to the banks of the Danube, as soon as the soreness occasioned by his previous ride has left his bones. Some years ago a man arrived one morning to the door of the British Consul-General. He was clad in the dress of a Transylvanian shepherd.

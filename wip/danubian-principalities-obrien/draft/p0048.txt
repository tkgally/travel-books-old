# Chapter V.

I<sc>t</sc> was nine o’clock in the evening when the passports were returned to us, and the horses set free. But the conductor said it was then too late to start, for the night was dark, and the way bad, and moreover the Cossacks along the road, who are generally a very giddy, thoughtless set of fellows, might in the obscurity mistake us for a body of invading Turks, and not discover their error till after they had cut our throats and rifled our portmanteau.

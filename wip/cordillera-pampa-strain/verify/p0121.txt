The Judicial power in Chili consists of two Superior Courts, the Supreme Court of Justice, and that of Appeals. They each consist of a President, five Ministers, a Fiscal, two <i>Relatores</i>, and a Secretary.

F<sc>oreign</sc> A<sc>ffairs</sc>.—Chili is at present at peace, and is generally in amicable relations with all nations. The most important negotiations on foot during 1848, were those tending to a long projected meeting of Plenipotentiaries of South American States, to determine upon a confederation between them, and establish a convention for purposes of commerce and navigation. Many years had elapsed since the American Congress at which the United States were represented, had met at <i>Panama</i>, and many of the causes which made it desirable at that time, had been removed or modified, and when, owing to the exertions of the States bordering on the Pacific, it was finally re-convened, only five Plenipotentiaries appeared in Lima, representing the Republics of Chili, Peru, Bolivia, Equador, and New-Grenada.

In 1848 the Representatives of these Republics signed a treaty of Confederation, one of Navigation and Commerce, as well as two pacts termed Convention of Mails and Consular Convention. The ratifications, which were subject to the decisions of the respective Governments, were to have been exchanged in Lima in August, 1849.

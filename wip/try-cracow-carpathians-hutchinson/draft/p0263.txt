Adjoining this building we were astonished to see an enormous oblong mound of earth, supported by brick walls; it was twelve feet high, seventy yards long, and ten broad. Our guide told us that this was completely filled with the bodies of children who had died uncircumcised before they were eight days old. There is but a handful of earth sprinkled between each coffin. What hundreds of thousands of infants must be lying there!

No one can visit Prague without being struck with the beauty of its old buildings. To the lover of antiquity it is especially interesting, and the “Grosser Ring,” which contains the splendid Rath-haus and Theinkirche, is one of the gems of this ancient city.

The old Watch Tower, at the end of the bridge, is another never-to-be-forgotten edifice. It was here that a Jesuit priest, in the Thirty Years’ War, let down the portcullis, and, with only four others to help him, saved the town from falling into the hands of the Swedes.

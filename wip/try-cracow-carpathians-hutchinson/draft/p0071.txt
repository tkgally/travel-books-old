The old Cathedral, close to the Schloss, is far from imposing in its external appearance. Founded in the year 966, burnt down 1306, and rebuilt 1320—1359, the jumble of styles both within and without is more curious than pleasing. But it has been well called the “Polish Westminster Abbey,” containing as it does the ashes of Poland’s greatest kings and heroes.

In all the spoliations of Cracow the Cathedral has never been touched, and it is full of the richest treasures. Over the lofty door by which you enter the building is suspended the thigh bone of a mammoth, besides other gigantic antediluvian remains.

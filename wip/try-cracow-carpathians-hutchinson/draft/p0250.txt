It labours, however, under the same disadvantage as do nearly all old fortifications, namely, that there is some height near, from which modern artillery can shell the town. In this case, a hill called the “Holy Mountain,” between three and four miles off, is the weak point, and, as usual, the Austrians say they are <i>going</i> to fortify it. We drove there in the morning, through the plain outside the town, passing several outlying forts; the last part of the hill must be climbed on foot, and from the summit we had a most complete view of the town and surrounding country.

Here was a very imposing religious establishment, monastery and chapel, with a public-house close by and numerous wooden stalls, where rosaries, crucifixes, and other sacred relics could be purchased by the faithful.

The great attraction is the sacred picture of the Virgin, which we were told had originally been brought down from heaven by an angel, and given to a worthy man who built the church. <i>Now</i>, somehow or other, it is frescoed on the stone-work inside, and however much you may cut away the stone, the picture remains the same. No one, however, is allowed to make the experiment for himself.

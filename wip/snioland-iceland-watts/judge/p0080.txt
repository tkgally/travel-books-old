W—— had been fishing, and had caught two kinds of fish—one the natives call “blœker,” apparently a kind of char, and the other “rauther,” which resembled a sea-trout. As the weather now seemed likely to clear, we prepared for a trip to Katlugia; but our anticipations were not realized, for the next day looked anything but hopeful. So instead of starting for Katlugia, <i>en route</i> for Myrdals Jökulls, as we had proposed, S—— and I, accompanied by the farmer from Little Hethi (the next farm to Hethi), named Brenyolver, set out for Gœsir Tindr, as I wished to have another look at it. We rode up the hill to the north, and, after about an hour, we left our horses in a little valley where sheep were feeding, and proceeded. We were soon, however, enveloped in fog, and could see nothing of the formation of the depression before described. It is surprising, if these depressions are ancient craters, that there is not more lava visible around them; for, as it is, lava only here occurs in fragmentary forms.

In many places there are rich iron ores; but all the specimens I have seen have been the Titanic iron, which is of little commercial value. There has been a great deal said about the surturbrand of the north: its value can be but local, although such value would be high. I do not know whether it occurs in workable quantities or not.

One source of hard cash, and by no means a small one, would be the encouragement of tourist visitors during the summer months. Of the sporting facilities of the island I am scarcely qualified to speak; but I know that its lakes, and many of its rivers, abound in fish, and that the shooting is not to be despised.

To the admirer of natural phenomena, Iceland offers exceptional interest, and it is to the pecuniary interest of the inhabitants that these objects be made more generally known, and that fresh features of attraction to the traveller be disclosed from unexplored parts of the country.

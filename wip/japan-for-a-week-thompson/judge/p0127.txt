She stays at one of the factory dormitories, and has to pay 7½ sen a day, thus practically keeping only 4½ sen or 1¼d. a day. For this pittance she has to work twelve full hours day or night, as the factory is worked in two shifts, and the change from day to night is effected weekly.

There are 1400 girls kept in dormitories. The space of each room is 24 by 24 feet, in which forty-four or forty-five girls are packed day and night in two equal shifts. In winter all windows are closed.

Readers may imagine how filthy and suffocating these dirty and low-ceilinged rooms become. As to the food, it must be the coarsest sort, since a day's meals cost less than 7½ sen. One pound of bread costs 7 sen in Japan. Thus these girls are fed on something less than one pound of bread a day!

The highest wages paid in Tokyo are 1s. a day, and thousands of women operatives get not more than sixpence. There are no laws regulating the hours of labour, and factory operatives usually work from seven in the morning to six at night, with a few minutes at noon for their meal.

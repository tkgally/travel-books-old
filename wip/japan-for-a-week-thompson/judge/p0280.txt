[[cont]]its vernacular is uniform and well established, and it has discarded most of the clumsy syntax of more primitive languages, such as Russian, its only future rival, and German, its Teutonic sister tongue.

German, though still the language of scholarship, makes no advance in the world of affairs, and the fact will astonish no one who has fruitfully understood Mark Twain’s famous appendix in <i>A Tramp Abroad</i> on “that Awful German Language.” It is said that Germans in the States make haste to learn English and soon speak nothing else, even amongst themselves. It is the same all over the East. German commerce expands, but most of it is conducted in English, or, as on the Bagdad railway, in French. The Orientals are leisurely people, but even they have not time for the complexities of the German sentences.

English, on the other hand, seems destined to become <i>the</i> medium of intercommunication over the whole of the vast world now starting into quick life on the shores of the Pacific, from New Zealand and Australia to Alaska. It is compulsory in all Japanese schools of upper grades. In China it is the official language for scientific and technical education, and its study is compulsory in all provincial scientific and technical high schools.

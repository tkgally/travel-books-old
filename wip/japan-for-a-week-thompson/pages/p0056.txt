There is fascination in ugliness, no doubt; and I confess that I found it difficult while in Moscow to unrivet my gaze from this dire and excecationary ecclesiological gew-gaw. It haunted me. It appalled and terrorised my imagination. It sat upon my spirits like some awful nightmare.

This—a house of God? This monumental charivari of designed discord and ordered disorder, with its gloomy labyrinth of painted dungeons—this a dwelling for the God who painted the sunset, the rose, and the butterfly? What blasphemy!

There can be no benevolence in the religion that found expression in this monstrosity. Here is no suggestion of thanksgiving for Divine Love, but very manifest fear of Satanic malignancy. This violation of beauty and grace proclaims not piety, but terror.

More prepossessing, but not less emblematic, is the adjoining Kremlin, the shining crown of “White Mother Moscow,” the Mecca of the Greek Church.

The conditions of working-class life under the rapidly-growing factory system are even worse. Kiyoshi Kawakami, writing in the <i>Orient</i>, says that children under ten—children of scarcely five or six years—are largely employed in factories, and rapidly disfigured and demoralised by injuriously-excessive hours of labour. Women often work fourteen, fifteen, sixteen, and even eighteen hours a day!

The latest legislation forbids night-work for children under fourteen; those under twelve will not be permitted to start work, but <i>those between ten and twelve who are already engaged when the law comes into force may continue</i>; there is to be a postponement of two years’ time before the enforcement of the law to hear the opinions of business men”; and the employment of children under twelve may be sanctioned “in accordance with the provisions of a separate ordinance.”

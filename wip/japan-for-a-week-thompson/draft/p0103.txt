# The Temples of Old Nikko

FOR twenty miles before reaching Nikko the high road stretches through an avenue of gigantic cedars, sombre and cold as a tunnel. For twenty miles the stately cryptomerias lift their pillars to an invisible sky between walls of serried foliage. Thus was the majestic way designed many centuries ago as an approach of fitting solemnity for the great Shogun’s funeral procession to the shrine of the soul of Japan.

Through the dusk of these miles of vaulted verdure the pilgrims from all parts of the country tramp unceasingly to pay their devotions at the tomb of the great Iyeyasu.

In white pilgrims’ gowns with wide sleeves and immense hats; in grey cotton kimonos thriftily tucked into the waistbelt, revealing elderly bandy legs in white cotton drawers; in straw-thatched rain coats and thatched straw mushroom hats; in wonderfully embroidered kimonos and papilionaceous shot silk obis, the multifarious pilgrims come.

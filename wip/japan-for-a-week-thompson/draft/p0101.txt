Outside, a wonderful miniature garden, a miniature lake, with miniature torrents and waterfalls, miniature crags and rocks crowned with a miniature dragon, and, of course, a plantation of the ubiquitous Japanese dwarf trees.

The preciosity of this doll’s-house landscape had created a perfect illusion, till three spotlessly white storks majestically stepped on the edge of the miniature lake. They were the only real things in the picture, and utterly destroyed its reality.

Having selected a section of the vast room for our entertainment, we were secluded by a shifting of the noiseless paper partitions, cushions were placed on the floor for our accommodation, giggling mousmés produced tea and multi-coloured rice-cakes, and, like a row of extra-sized Buddhas, we knelt on our cushions to partake of refreshment face to face with three attendant little maids.

The mousmés kneel in the most obsequious postures, but regard our efforts to look happy under the influence of pins and needles and cramp in the calf with giggles which belie the respectfulness of their attitudes.

The charge for the tea was one shilling per head. It was cheap, but I think the mousmés got more fun for the money.

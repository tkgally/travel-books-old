[[cont]]dark and uniform; around us were mountains and peaks of every form and hue, from the bright green turf and white barren limestone, to the jagged and angry-looking granite. The clear blue atmosphere above, the sun shining without a cloud, but the heat tempered by a cooling breeze from the snow, made one’s enjoyment of this lovely scene thoroughly perfect.

Guided by the murmuring of the stream (the sound of which we never lost for a moment) we at length reached the spot from which it burst from the ground, dashing down a steep declivity in a headlong torrent to the river below, forming a magnificent cascade. It was about thirty feet broad as it issued from the mountain, and became a respectable stream of twelve feet wide and three feet deep as it traversed the valley; the water as cold as ice. One can scarcely understand the phenomenon, as there was no cavern of any kind; but just as if the earth here had turned into the gigantic rose of some monster watering-pot, the water spurted out in fifty different places at once, and then joining together in one huge volume, it swept and leapt over ferns, mosses, and boulders till it subsided in the stream at our feet.

We remained for some time watching and admiring the pretty picture before us, quite unique of its kind.

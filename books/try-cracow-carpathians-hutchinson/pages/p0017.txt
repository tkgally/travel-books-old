Once more, on the 1st of July 1871, we start for an unknown land, armed with our usual amount of luggage, thirty pounds’ weight each. A bundle of wraps, lady’s handbag, fishing rod and tackle, and Mr. Solomon’s well-known travelling barometer. Alas! another article has to be added this time, more’s the pity!—a passport!—but we shy at the French visa, 8<i>s.</i> each, and no end of bother, and therefore discard the route by Calais or Boulogne, and choose the London passage to Ostend, by which the boats are larger and far preferable to those from Dover, the sea very little longer, and the expense very much less.

So after many barometrical observations and calculations, as to moon, &c. behold us once more outward bound! pacing the deck of the stout old tubby paddle, the “Holland,” as she steams away from St. Katharine’s wharf, with weather middling, and wind gusty, blowing in fitful starts from every quarter; and, we, poor creatures! anticipating the worst.

We often watched the dance, and if well done, there is something very pretty and graceful about it.

Slowly and solemnly it begins, with a promenade, which gradually increases in speed, until the pace is that of the fastest galop, the couples breaking away from one another and dancing back to back, and the next moment whirling round together, the gentleman with both hands round his partner’s waist. Again they separate, the lady retreating and advancing as if she could not quite make up her mind what to do; her partner pursues, and she as rapidly makes off in the opposite direction until caught, when they spin round again together for a few turns and divide as before, to shuffle about <i>vis-à-vis</i> to one another in the most ridiculous manner.

There is endless variety, for no two couples are ever engaged in the same part of the dance.

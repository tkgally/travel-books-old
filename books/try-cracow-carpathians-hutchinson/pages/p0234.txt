Here we halted for a couple of hours to refresh ourselves, as well as man and beasts. We ordered our dinner, and heard with delight that we could have some mutton. As we had not seen any for nearly six weeks, we looked upon it as quite a treat; but, when set before us, it proved as tough as an old boiled boot, and was almost uneatable.

We rummaged about the kitchen for further provisions, but were unsuccessful. Our landlady was very busy over a sack of land-crabs, or rather lobsters, which abound in all the streams near here. She was obliged to be very careful in the way she took hold of them, for fear of being bitten: seizing them by the back with one hand, she pulled off the tips of their tails with the other, and then chucked them alive into the boiling water bubbling on her stove. It was a comfort to think that the poor things must die directly.

The Sclavish peasants were constantly running in and out, placing their filled pipes in the fire, then, shutting the stove-door for a minute or two, they pulled them out and began to smoke.

Our course still continued over broken-up rocks, and in less than half-an-hour we stood on the top of the Schlagendorf, nearly 10,000 feet above the sea.

A short stick had been placed in a small cairn of stones covered with ice; a melancholy monument of the desolation around, and also of the gloom which damped our ardour as we sat down to rest, for a nasty cold cloud had just surrounded us like the folds of a great wet sheet, and completely shut out everything. We could see nothing beyond twenty yards, and within that distance there were stones and fog, and fog and stones!

Patience had seldom sat on so high a monument, or one more cold or windy, or where breathing was more difficult. Under the shelter of a friendly rock we planted ourselves and hoped for the best. Ever and anon our flagging spirits rose as a gleam of sunshine darted past and revealed a glimpse into the depths below; but all in vain!

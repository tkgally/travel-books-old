The best plan is to ask for the hotels most frequented by the English, and at these one is more likely to find the little specialities in which we delight. We were shown into a large handsome room fronting the square, the outer walls five feet thick and the inner ones four feet. The beds were to all appearance perfectly clean, and we looked forward to a good night’s rest after our long and fatiguing day of travel.

Our interview, however, with the chambermaid was not quite satisfactory, and might have been a warning as to what was to follow. We viewed our one small basin and water jug with dismay, and begged for some addition to this department.

“Impossible!” was her reply, “we never put more than one jug or basin in one room. No one that comes to Cracow ever requires more.”

“Could we have a hip-bath or foot-bath?”

“Oh dear no! Not such a thing in the hotel. There is a very good public bath close by, where anyone may go,” and we must do likewise.

Be it so!—when at Cracow, bathe as the Cracovians do, and so to bed and to sleep.

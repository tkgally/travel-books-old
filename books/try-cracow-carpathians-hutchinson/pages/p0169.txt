We were now making our way along the southern side of the Tatra; our faces, for the first time, turned homewards.

This valley is a very broad table land. Mountain after mountain, without a break (that is to say, without intervening hills), rise like a wall on one side, while the distant view on the other is bounded by smaller hills of the great Carpathian range, the southern slopes of which lead down to the heated plains of Hungary. The hay-harvest has begun, this 29th day of July. The people look prosperous and busy, and huge carts, drawn by three handsome horses, or by enormous white oxen, fifteen hands high, and five feet from tip to tip of their horns, are all engaged in dragging home their fragrant loads. These white or cream-coloured oxen are splendid-looking creatures, with their large, soft, brown eyes; and when six or eight are yoked to one cart, it forms, indeed, a perfect picture.

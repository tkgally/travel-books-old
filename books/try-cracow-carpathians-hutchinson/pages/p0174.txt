The peasants come from far and wide to fill their wine casks with it; the dregs of the wine assist in making it a very refreshing drink; but labourers in the field generally take the water pure, even while working in the heat of the sun. It is perfectly colourless, and without smell. When placed on the dinner-table, as it was every day in glass bottles, it looked like soda-water just uncorked.

Among other ingredients it contains small quantities of carbonate of soda, sulphur, and oxide of iron; but derives its principal value from the large proportion of carbonic acid. After rain, the properties of the spring are stronger, as the heavy dampness of the atmosphere prevents the escape of the gas.

We remained at Hispar village two days, preparing for our march through the uninhabitable regions of snow and ice, which now lay before us. This was the last habitation of man we should see for many days.

We employed ourselves during our stay in surveying and sketching, and selecting the men to accompany us. The villagers naturally showed great reluctance to leave their homes and green fields for the untrodden snows of the mountains; but with the power of written orders from their Rajah and the Kashmir Durbar, the male population of the village was brought before us and we selected the most suitable, the women bewailing the fate of their own particular friends.

It was the only sad incident on our expedition.

He looked an outcast, far away from the habitations of man in the midst of overpowering immensities. What a subject, I thought, for a picture! For the death of Cain perhaps.

On leaving him we crossed the rope bridge, and kept along the bank to the mouth of the river, instead of crossing the high ridge. At the corner we had to climb up the mighty glacier-worn rock face that descended straight into the river: this could only be managed by clambering up great fissures, in which steps had been artificially made here and there by placing loose stones in the cracks. But the smooth rock face had to be traversed, and at one place it was very dangerous. A corner round which we had to turn was quite smooth.

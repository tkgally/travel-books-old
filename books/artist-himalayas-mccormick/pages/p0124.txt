A snow-storm was gathering over it, and made a grand picture, which I painted, sitting on the ledge of our camping ground. If I had fallen I should not have stopped rolling for two or three thousand feet, but there was nowhere else to sit, and the fire, over which Zurbriggen was making some soup, shared the seat, so, with the driving smoke and the ashes blowing about one, sketching was not all a pleasure at an altitude of 13,000 feet. Snow commenced to fall around us; through it we could hear the <i>Ram Chukor</i> crying far below, as the three of us crawled into our little tent, and made ourselves as comfortable as the situation would permit.

At 2.30 the next morning Zurbriggen woke us up saying it was time to start, and good man that he is, he had a hot tin of Kock’s meat peptone ready. So, waking up the two coolies, who were sleeping under the rock wrapped in their thick blankets, we packed the tent and sent them down.

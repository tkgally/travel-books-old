Conway, who was eager to reach the <i>col</i>, untied himself and went on with Harkbir. Soon I was alone in the barren waste, where no sound broke the stillness of the vast solitude, for there was no life of any sort, bird or beast or vegetation; only the long sweep of snow, bounded on each side by walls of ice with dark patches of steep rock, that emphasised the whiteness to an intense brilliance which the eye could not look on without the aid of dark glasses.

At last I reached the top where the coolies were now all gathered together, eating or sleeping. Conway was working hard at his plane table, the Gurkhas had spread a waterproof sheet out, and were melting snow for drinking water in the rays of the sun, and Rahim Ali was preparing lunch with the same <i>sang froid</i> he would show in the bangla at Abbottabad.

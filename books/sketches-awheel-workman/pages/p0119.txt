At Alicante African Spain may be said to begin. From there to Murcia the landscape, the atmosphere, the vegetation, all have a distinctly north African character. Elche, situated among thousands of date palms rising to the height of forty to sixty feet, closely resembles an oasis in the desert. Still although it has its palms and flat roofs and square towers, it lacks the mud walls and graceful burnoused figures that lend an Oriental charm to oases like Vieille Biskra. If the tourist has never been in any of the countries of north Africa nor seen an oasis in the Sahara, let him by no means omit to visit Elche, but let him not stop here but continue on to Murcia by the carriage road, for it is on this drive that African Spain bursts upon him in all its enthralling beauty.

It is one of the dreams that some time when Japan is rich the reaches will be dredged and Yedo made a second London and the greatest seaport of the Eastern world. But public income is small, and the railroad leads in all directions from Yokohama, where the harbor is reinforced with a stout breakwater and equipped with splendid docks. Meanwhile, Tokyo does the best it can to improve itself internally. The railroads leading to the north, south, and inland do not start from a common center, but from widely separated terminals. To remedy this an ambitious scheme of an all-embracing viaduct has been planned and much of it built, but lack of funds has held up the completion. The scheme includes also a great central station, and that, too, will come some day when the government shakes off its load of war. Recently the viaduct has been put to some use as an elevated trolley line.

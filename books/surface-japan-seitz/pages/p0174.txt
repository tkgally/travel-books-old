In making machinery the Japanese have a similar difficulty in arriving at the real thing. Some essential to perfect working is either overlooked or left out in the desire to save or from sheer inability to discover it. Japanese visitors to America always impress their hosts with the closeness of their inquiries and by taking multitudinous notes. But to go home and disentangle the mass of information, not always clearly comprehended, is quite another matter. There is plenty of testimony to prove Japanese mechanical ineffectiveness. In the cotton-mills it is difficult to have the plant kept in good order or to produce with foreign rapidity. The cheap labor is an offset to a degree—but only an offset.

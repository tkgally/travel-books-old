The young men join the garrisons in the great cities and soon find their way to the tea-houses and the Yoshiwara, breeding extravagance and bad habits, which they take back to the villages after their period of service, much to the displeasure of the elders. Japanese villages are controlled very largely by public sentiment as enunciated by these village elders, grave old gentlemen, who discuss and concur. When they have concurred the result is the law of the village and the crystallized opinion of the community. The central government takes great care to sound this form of opinion, and when it once gets a consensus is pretty sure to be sustained if it follows the trend that prevails. So underneath the monarchy, despite the limited franchise, the force of public opinion is always at work and it is always heeded.

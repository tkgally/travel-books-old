The Japanese standard is no such thing as that, but it concentrates government worship to the exclusion of much that might be well taught and practised. A nation in the main for its entire existence, military and agricultural, has much to discard and remodel before it competes with another civilization far less high-minded politically and perhaps less noble in thought, but efficient industrially and financially to the last degree.

[[break]]

Even education brings its burdens. The colleges at Tokyo and Kyoto are crushed with students. They crowd the higher institutions in hungry-minded thousands, creating a problem as they graduate. What are they all to do with their widened minds? The small villages offer no field, except perhaps to the doctors. Professional men are little needed outside of the cities, and there the want is best supplied from the ranks of those educated abroad. Teaching is the poorest paid of all occupations.

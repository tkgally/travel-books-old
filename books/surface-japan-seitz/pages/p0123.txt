Meat is still little eaten in Japan, but “Beef Store” is an occasional sign in the cities. Fish and rice continue to be the staple articles of diet. But good vegetables are plenty. Eggplant is largely raised and sweet potatoes are found on every farm. The Japanese sweet potato is purple in its outer color and full of strings. Onions, beans, radishes, and the like are on sale in every stall, while the fruit shops compare with the average in New York. The choicest peaches are grown; the trees are kept small and the fruit ripens incased in little paper bags. The pears look like russet apples, are woody and have no flavor, but are full of juice that is expressed and carbonated into a champagne cider that would be popular in America. The Japanese watermelon has a thin rind and the red meat fills it closely. It reaches about half the size of the Georgia variety, but has all the qualities of juice and sweetness that go with the best Southern specimens.

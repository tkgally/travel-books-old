A great fleet must be maintained to guard the little islands from the envy and animosity of the world, while the whole nation is under a perpetual strain to uphold the prestige gained in the conflict—a prestige that cannot be converted into anything that will give more rice and better fare to the 49,000,000 out of her 50,000,000 people who must live from hand to mouth, feeding themselves only by forcing two crops a year from the reluctant soil or snatching fish and seaweed from the selfish sea in a land where every grain of rice must be counted and fish and seaweed dried.

Thus in trying to be modern and competitive without natural resources the burden placed upon the industrious inhabitants is very great. Poetry, cherry-blossoms, and chrysanthemums are at a sad discount in conflict with the natural advantages and organized resources of the Western world. That so much has been done is marvelous; that much more will be done is a natural expectancy; but the load is enormous

The shadow of the day of departure now began to fall, and this was fixed for early morning on Sunday, the 21st of September. Meanwhile, Sir Robert had arranged a dinner party for me, although almost all the world were absent, and after dinner there was a vast amount of entertainment among all the company with the marvels of the phonograph. “What next will be invented?” has been a perpetual interrogatory, and I never forget a strange phrase used by an old home gardener when informed of some (to him) new horror, that “by-and-by we should be getting too cunnin’ for God A’mighty.” And there are certain of a higher rank, too, that labour under much the same misgiving.

On the Saturday afternoon we were regaled on the lawn with a band of music, conducted by a Portuguese bandmaster, whom I, of course, invited to a Portuguese conversation. This band is one especially belonging to Sir Robert, and is exclusively fostered by him. It plays every Saturday, and the lawn is an harmonious scene of social meeting.

That evening we had a trio dinner of adieu, Sir Robert, Mr. Ludlow, and I. The chair which Sir Robert had sent for from Tung Ching had arrived, my guide and servant had already departed, and late at night I gratefully bid my hospitable host good-bye.

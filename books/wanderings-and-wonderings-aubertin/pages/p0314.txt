But having deliberated for a while, wiser counsels prevailed. I was sufficiently charged with memories of Fujikawa, and resolved to return direct to Yokohama. This I did by the morning train of the 15th of May, falling in (not out) with Mr. and Mrs. Brooke at one of the short stations. Thus, including the deviation, which to some whom I know I would almost recommend, the seventh day found me at “The Grand” again.

It was on my return to Yoko that I decided about leaving Japan in consequence of a letter I had received, that opened me a chance of a visit to Peking. But I had now determined to see the Tenriugawa river on my way down to Kobe, and therefore I arranged to leave Yoko on the morning of the 22nd of May. Bidding good-bye to my friends, therefore, I sent my own servant on to Kobe direct, and started with Sosuke Yamamota by the 9.15 morning train to Tokio, in order to take the train from the Yueno station to Takasaki, and to continue thence by jinrikisha to Ikao. This I did, arriving late at Ikao—just too late to join a dinner-table—after having passed through the usual style of Japanese scenery among the mountains.

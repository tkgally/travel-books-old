Hitherto, the great basis of the safety and tranquillity of the empire, has been the patriarchal system which, as was noticed in a former chapter, has continued to be followed to this time, in China, of all the individuals of succeeding generations, living under the oldest surviving heads of families. The prudence and experience of the latter in directing the concerns of their offspring, tend to avert from them the evil consequences of events which might provoke discontent and disaffection; and their mistrust of innovation, induces them to set the example of resignation to the lot of life in which they happen to be placed. The natural sentiments of respect to age, united with affection to kindred, early taking root, and strengthened by a daily sense of services received, often bind the mind more effectually, tho with gentle ties, than the force of compulsory laws.

The art of printing, practised probably at a very early period of the empire, has contributed to the preservation of it in a state nearly uniform to this time.

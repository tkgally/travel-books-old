We now commenced the ascent, carrying everything upon our backs, for it was impossible to think of hauling the sleigh over the rough surface of the glacier at this point; for the sand which the ice contained, even if the glacier had been smooth, would have soon worn the runners of a sleigh through by the friction. I had hoped that the surface of the glacier would become clean and smooth after a mile or so, as the part Mr. J. Milne and I had traversed near Grœnafjall in 1871; but I was doomed to disappointment. After an hour’s hard work of climbing with our heavy loads over the uneven surface, and dragging the unwieldy sleigh and the snow-shoes (which latter, although of no weight, were the most cumbersome part of our load), we were still surrounded by difficult and bewildering aiguilles, and hummocks of sand and ice, which seemed to increase around us.

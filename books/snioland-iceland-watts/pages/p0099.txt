He was in a state of complete incapacity, and all that I could get from him, in answer to my exhortations to go on, was “<i>Mikid slimt fyrir gamlan Islanding</i>,” “Very bad for old Iceland man,” which he reiterated in a plaintive voice, and at length began to cry. I took his load from him, and left him to himself. We camped about a quarter of a mile from the point whence I had determined to attack the Jökull, upon the edge of the plateau I have before described. Here we built up four walls of stones, and roofed them with the sheet of canvas we had brought for that purpose. While building the hut, a hail-storm burst upon us, the spherical little pieces of ice, as large as a good-sized pea, pelting us unmercifully. During the course of our operations our drunken servant came up, and lying down, at once went to sleep.

The principal monuments of Iceland are literary ones. In the first place, the Icelanders have preserved to us the mythology of the Scandinavian nations, under the form of the books popularly known as the older and the younger Eddas, the poetic or the prose.

The older Edda is a collection of such poetic fragments of the ancient mythology of Scandinavia as was still current in Iceland in the twelfth century. The younger Edda is a prose and later work which traditionally bears the name of Gurdi, the historical. It is a compilation which amplifies and, in some places, fills up gaps in the earlier Edda.

There is also the form of composition known as Sagas by the Icelanders, which are mostly legendary histories of affairs which took place in Norway and Iceland from the ninth to the thirteenth centuries. It should be mentioned that there is mingled with the prose of these works a great deal of verse, exceedingly intricate in its character and metaphorical in diction, to the extent of often proving a puzzle for learned Icelanders themselves.

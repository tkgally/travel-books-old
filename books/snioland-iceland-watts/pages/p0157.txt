When we all roused up from our sleep, the snow was thickly falling; and as Biartney looked out he remarked, in scarcely a cheerful tone, “<i>Allt thoka og mikil drifa,</i>” “All fog, much fine snow.”

We held a council of war over our breakfast. The men were unanimous in their decision to turn back, nor could I (much as I should like to have done so), with anything like an easy conscience, have tried to persuade them to remain where we were or to go on. We had scarcely three days’ provision left; the wind was SSE., where it might stay for a fortnight, as I had often known to be the case; it was hopeless to expect fine weather; the fine snow which was falling shewed there was a great deal more to come, for a heavy fall always begins with fine driving snow, and a passing storm with large flakes, and I did not like the prismatic ring round the sun two nights previous; we were two good days’ travel from the commencement of the glacier; our compass was useless, and with the present weather we might be a great deal longer trying to find our way down; I therefore determined to return.

<i>July 10th</i>.—A wet day, and Hecla was entirely obscured by clouds, well deserving her name, which means “cloak.” Having suffered for some days with acute inflammation of the right eyelid, I was compelled to remain in tent and doctor it.

<i>July 11th</i>.—The weather being somewhat improved, I bandaged up my eye, and we set out for the mountain. We reached the little farm of Gastolœker about midday, passing over several lava-streams and tracts of sand and ash: here we stopped to drink coffee, and then proceeded, with the farmer as our guide, to ascend the mountain. We crossed the river Sola, which takes its name from the Sola (i. e. <i>Solanforso</i>). Before the eruption of 1848, the waters of this river abounded with fish. The farmer said none had since been caught; so I suppose they all then perished. We ascended on horseback to the termination of an arm of the lava-stream of 1848, a height of 900 feet.

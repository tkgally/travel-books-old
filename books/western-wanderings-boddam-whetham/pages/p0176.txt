The stage-drivers doubtless are, as a rule, very intelligent, and may be relied on; but some of them have really such excessive regard for the truth that they use it with almost penurious frugality.

From Stockton to San Francisco, through the Sacramento and San Joaquin Valleys, and over the Coast Range, is an uninteresting journey. We were very glad when we reached Oakland, which is to San Francisco what Brooklyn is to New York. Here the train ran out into the bay, for a distance of more than two miles, on a substantial pier. We were then transferred to the ferry-boat, which took us three miles across the water to the City of San Francisco. The sudden change from heat to cold was rather severe. A perfect hurricane was blowing in through the Golden Gate. A gentleman informed us, with tears in his eyes—whether from sorrow or from the coldness of the wind I do not know—that it was always the same in the summer months. The wind in the bay, he said, was nothing to the breeze on shore, and that the consequent dust was the eyesore of ‘the Golden City of the West.’

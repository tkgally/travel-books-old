Some of the new settlements seem thriving, but I know not from what source their support is derived; as <i>lager-bier</i> saloons, whisky-shops, and what they call hotels, are often the only houses to be seen in the village. And this is what is termed the ‘march of intellect’ across the continent!

Groups of ‘noble savages’ are now to be met with at most of the stations; but those who expect to see in them the heroes, or their descendants, of Fennimore Cooper’s novels, will be woefully disappointed. The traditional Indian is a romance, and his ‘wild turkey’ appearance is pretty in a picture, though not in the reality—at all events, not near a railway station. The ‘squaws’ have certainly settled a point much discussed lately, relating to extravagance in dress; and a work entitled ‘How to Dress on Nothing a Year like a Pawnee, by a Pawnee,’ might be a success, but would hardly be approved of by Worth or Madame Elise. There is a quaint Indian proverb which says: ‘The smiles of a pretty woman are the tears of the purse;’ but where either the pretty woman or the purse is to come from is matter of conjecture. What the Indian woman would do with a purse if she had one I cannot tell.

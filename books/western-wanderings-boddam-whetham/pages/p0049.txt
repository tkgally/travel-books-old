This and another spot, from which somebody once jumped into the river, were evidently of much deeper interest to the mind of our Jehu than the glorious Falls themselves. We were inveigled into a house in front of the Horse-shoe Fall by the assurance of the proprietor that the view from the top was magnificent, and that no charge was made; but the view did not seem to us finer from above than from below. Incautiously entering another room, we discovered a shop full of photographs, Indian fans, &c. (made in Birmingham, probably), and offered for sale by some fascinating young ladies, who could not allow us to leave without a <i>souvenir</i> of Niagara.

We were afterwards induced to change our clothes for waterproof ones, and to descend with a guide beneath, or rather behind the Fall. I am not sure that this part of the programme repaid us for getting very hot and wet; but the proprietor when he had pocketed our dollars seemed to think it all right—so we had to look comfortable and happy. A slight shade passed over his countenance when he found he could not induce us to be photographed ‘in connection with the Falls,’ as he expressed it.

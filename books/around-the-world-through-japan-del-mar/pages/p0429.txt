The manners of the ignorant and dull rustics leave something to be desired. For one thing, it is only in Japan that I found the country-people neglecting to return a passing salutation addressed to them in their own language. It is, perhaps, not surprising that a people so recently released from feudal serfdom should in some ways resemble the slaves emancipated about the same time in the United States; but the curious mixture of servility, bumptiousness, and truculency in the bearing of the enfranchised negroes finds its counterpart in the Japanese peasantry; and the position of the negro in the Southern States, where in theory and law he is the equal of the white man, but in practice is kept in an inferior station, is duplicated in Japan in the relation of the peasant to the <i>samurai</i>.

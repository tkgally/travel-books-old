We first had to learn how to pronounce the native words as written, and for this purpose the five vowels are pronounced as in French and are merely lengthened by being doubled, <i>eu</i> like <i>eu</i> in French; <i>ui</i> like <i>oeu</i> in French; <i>ei</i> or <i>ij</i> like <i>ei</i> in the English veil; <i>oe</i> like <i>oo</i> in cuckoo; and <i>au</i> or <i>ou</i> like <i>ow</i> in how. Then we committed to memory the numerals,—<i>satoe</i> meaning one or the indefinite particle, and so on; and a few words like <i>sapada</i> (<i>spada</i>), waiter or “boy”; <i>ajer panas</i>, warm water; <i>ka</i>, to, go to, or go to the——; <i>poeloe</i> (or <i>nusa</i>), island; <i>goenoeng</i>, mountain; <i>planki</i> or <i>djoelie</i>, palanquin; <i>dessa</i>, village; <i>kawa</i>, crater; <i>telaga</i> (or <i>sitoe</i>), lake; and so on.

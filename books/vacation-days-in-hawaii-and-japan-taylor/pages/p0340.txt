# Kobe, Osaka, and Nara

[[subtitle]] A Geisha dance—Losses by flood—To Kobe—Nunobiki Waterfalls—Iwamoto, the bamboo worker—Osaka—Wrestling match—Satsuma—Cotton fields—A first-class compartment—Nara—Avenue of lanterns—Tame deer—Temple of Kasuga-no-Miya—Daibutsu—Naraningyo—Return to Kyoto—Sobei Kinkozan—Young ladies' school—Kyoto Castle—Imperial Palace.

Q<sc>uite</sc> gayly we enter our jinrikishas and set out for the tea house, in which, as we are informed by our guide, a first-class Geisha dance is to be held. On our way to this entertainment we pass a street called Zezeura Street, which is, we learn, monopolized by the demi-monde. We see the frail young people sitting at the small windows, some of them looking miserable enough. This is a dismal street. The lamp on each house is so placed as to cast a bright light upon the inmate, and I pity the poor creatures, who seem to have no means of escaping their wretched mode of existence.

Arriving at the inn, we are ushered into a large room on the second floor, where, to our surprise, chairs are provided for us. The room is bright and attractive with candles and lanterns.

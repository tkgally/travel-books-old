# On the Road—Akakura—Naoetsu—Niigata.

[[subtitle]] Akakura—Hot springs—Eating with chopsticks—A warm bath—Blackened teeth—Naoetsu—Equalization of labor—Umbrellas—Katamachi—The new railroad—Aomigawa—Kashiwazaki—An inn receipt—Souvenirs—Jinrikisha rates—A “corner” in jinrikishas—Tashiro—Miyamoto—Yoita—Floods and freshets—Prayers for clear weather—A Japanese steamer.

W<sc>e</sc> leave Nagano this morning at 9.30 by train for Akakura, en route for Naoetsu. How beautiful, from the car window, is the view of the lofty mountains, with the wide plains at their feet! At Toyono we have a clear sight of Tojakushi-san and Ken-no-mine mountains, and last in the grand triad comes Myoko-zan, with its elevation of eight thousand one hundred and eighty feet. These mountains overlook a beautiful valley.

The grade is now upward until we reach Kashiwabara Station, at an altitude of two thousand two hundred feet. We do not stop again until we arrive at Akakura, when we leave the train, and after refreshing ourselves with a cup of tea at a neighboring inn we leave our baggage in the care of the landlady, and take jinrikishas for the celebrated Hot Springs, four miles distant.

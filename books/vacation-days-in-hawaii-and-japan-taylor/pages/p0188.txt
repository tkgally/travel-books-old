Upon either side of the gateway are wooden images of the “Wind Devil,” which is supposed to rule the winds, and the “Thunder Devil,” who controls the storms.

These images, called Mio, are placed here to guard the sacred temple. We visit also the stable in which is the sacred white pony, kept for the use of the god. The workmanship of this bronze portal is of wonderful beauty. Here we have a fine concentrated view of this whole group of temples, and the splendor of this consecrated spot.

A short walk from the temple brings us to the Red Bridge, or Mi Hashi, which is worthy of note. It spans the Daiya gawa, and may be viewed from another and public bridge, a few feet lower down the stream. From this last we look upon the scene before us in wonder and admiration.

In the background are lofty mountains clad in verdure, towering thousands of feet heavenward, while at their base the beautiful waters of the Daiya gawa flow in rapid and tempestuous course. In the foreground is the Mi Hashi, the sacred bridge, formerly closed to all persons but the Shogun, excepting twice a year, when it was opened to pilgrims.

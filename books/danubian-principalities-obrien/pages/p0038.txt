It might then give shelter to about fifty or sixty vessels of from one hundred and fifty to two hundred tons burthen. The facilities for restoring the mole are very great, as hewn stones of all sizes are on the spot.

Having examined Kustendji I determined on returning to the Danube by the road to Rassova, so as to be able to compare the advantages of the two lines.

Leaving Kustendji, I proceeded southwards for a mile to where the line of embankment, which crosses the country from near Rassova to this place, called Trajan’s Wall, ceases, and this is the spot where it is pretended the Danube formerly fell into the sea. I could not see one vestige of a water-course to justify such a supposition.

Before any light could dawn upon the blundering obscurity which enveloped the plans of the different diplomatists, the fifteen days allowed by the Porte expired, and Turks and Russians were at once engaged in deadly strife on the Danube. On Sunday, the 23rd day of October, the delay allowed by the Sultan expired. On that very day orders were given to the Russian flotilla to advance up the Danube. The cannon on board the steamer and gun-boats were ready shotted, and the vessels were cleared for action. The fort of Issactche was the first formidable point which the flotilla had to pass. On the left bank opposite to Issactche, General Luders hastily threw up some works behind which he placed his mortars.

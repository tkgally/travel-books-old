The Englishwoman’s success was decided after the first scene. The Russian officers, who crowded the pit, applauded uproariously, and brought our countrywoman three times curtseying to the foot-lights. She had a good voice, of considerable compass; but, above all, she showed herself a thorough musician, who had been properly educated for her profession. She sang the composer’s music faithfully and correctly, a thing which no one had ever heard before at the Opera of Bucharest.

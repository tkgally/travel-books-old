They carried their greatcoats in a round leather case on the top of their knapsacks, which were made of cow-hide. I observed that they did not wear highlows like our soldiers, but Wellington boots. The uniform worn by these men I have since learned to be that of nearly all the Russian infantry of the line. When the Russian soldier returns to his quarters, he instantly puts aside his helmet, coatee, cross-belts, and trousers, and turns out in his drawers, which reach below the knee, till they are met by the Wellington boot, and he wears a flat foraging cap of dark cloth, and a fawn-coloured great coat, which is gathered in at the waist and comes down to his ankles. It is in this dress that he performs all fatigue duty. I am sorry to say that the bright clean appearance of the Russian soldier when on parade is confined to the surface, for his shirt, drawers, and other under-garments are generally in an alarming state of dirt.

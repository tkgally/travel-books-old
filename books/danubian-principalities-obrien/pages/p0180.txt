At Szolnok the Vienna railway begins, but between that town and Arad there is no road whatever. The carriage in which we travelled from Arad had no springs, and the jolting as we galloped along over the broken ground was so great that at times I was afraid of having a limb fractured or put out of joint. When we arrived at Szolnok, the day after our departure from Arad, I could scarcely move, I was in such pain from the jolting.

Between Szolnok and Pesth, a gend’arme came into the train, and took the passports of all the passengers, and gave us receipts in return. When within about an hour’s distance from Presbourg, a police-officer entered the carriage where I was, and asked me for my passport. I handed him the receipt which his colleague had given me between Szolnock and Pesth.

His speech was answered by a cheer, and he at once marched to the palace, which he surrounded with his troops, whilst he planted his loaded cannon at the gates. In the early part of the evening an order had been sent to the commander of a Greek war steamer lying at the Piræus, who was in the plot, to have his steam up at about midnight, to be ready to convey King Otho to Trieste the instant His Majesty arrived on board. The conspirators naturally concluded that the King, terrified by the revolt of his troops, would at once fly from Greece. In this, however, they were mistaken. His Majesty is a man of undaunted courage, and he was determined that if he could not remain on his throne, that he would at least die in its defence. Thus the main object of the conspirators failed.

Modern Greek, which only a few years ago was studied solely by some Fanariot families, is now spoken with purity by millions of the Greek race. There is not a town or village throughout Greece that has not a college or a school paid by the nation. I remember visiting, not long ago, a little village about twelve miles from Nauplia, on the sea-shore. The inhabitants were very poor—some of the poorest in all Greece—but they had a school. The wretched people toiled at the meagre soil from morning till night to keep the wolf from the door; and though their children might have aided them in their labour, yet they thought it their first duty to send their little ones to school. I visited this school, where I found some thirty children assembled. They were supplied with slates and books by the <i>commune</i>, but writing-paper was an expensive luxury which was only given to the more advanced pupils.

And close to where the soldier lies dead, his comrades are busy with their camp-kettles cooking their morning meal or are going through the routine of their duties. Nothing around you is in harmony with feelings of mourning or regret. And so you continue your way over the ground till you have satisfied your curiosity as to the state of the living and the number of the dead, and then you too look anxiously after your morning meal, and as you are sipping your coffee and smoking your pipe, you speculate calmly upon the chances of the war.

How different is this from the feeling of depressing awe with which you look on death in cities, in the sick chamber, in the midst of quiet, daily avocations. In the house where there is death, you walk noiselessly and hold your breath, for perhaps you hear stifled sobs. It may be, a child, that is weeping beside her dying father, or a mother’s heart that is breaking, for the boy who was her pride and hope, lies dead.

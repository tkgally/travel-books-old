Old Theresa, Jules’ bonne, would be so glad to see him; the old woman always cried with joy when he came back from college. She had prepared Jules’ room. It was quite droll to see the fidget she was in all day long, about this room. It could never be nice enough for her Jules. She had hung a picture of <i>Notre Dame de la Garde</i> at the head of his bed, and every morning she put fresh flowers in his window. The father talked a great deal about “Marie.” She had become a <i>grande demoiselle</i>, and prettier than ever. Every Sunday since Jules went to college, she came to the château with her mamma, to ask for news about him. They would go and pay her a long visit when they got back to Cannes. I observed that Jules’ voice was growing weaker and weaker, and at length it was the father alone who spoke. And Jules would sigh whenever his father spoke of little Marie.

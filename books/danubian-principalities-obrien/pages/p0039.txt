The road then runs for about two hours parallel to Trajan’s Wall, and is one continued though gentle ascent. We then arrived at a point commanding a view of the valley of Tchernavoda with Bourlack in the hollow. We descended for about a quarter of an hour to the little Tartar village of Alacap, and from thence our road lay along the face of the hills forming the southern boundary of the valley of Tchernavoda to beyond the town of Carasou, where crossing over two considerable ridges we came upon a small marshy lake below Idris, down which we continued our road till we reached the bank of the Danube half an hour below Rassova, after nine and a half hours’ hard riding. This latter road holds out no appearance of anything like a line for a canal, nor offers as a mere road any of the advantages of the line from Tchernavoda.

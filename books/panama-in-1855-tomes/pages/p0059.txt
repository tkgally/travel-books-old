We now get upon the railroad track, which leads us into the main street. A meagre row of houses facing the water made up of the railroad office, a store or two, some half dozen lodging and drinking establishments, and the “Lone Star,” bounds the so-called street on one side, and the railroad track, upon its embankment of a few feet above the level of the shore, bounds the other.

There is another and only one other street, which you reach by crossing a wooden bridge, that a sober man can only safely traverse by dint of deliberate care in the day-time, and a drunken man never, and which stretches over a large sheet of water that ebbs and flows in the very centre of the so-called city. This second street begins at the coral beach at the northern end of the island, and runs southward until it terminates in a swamp.

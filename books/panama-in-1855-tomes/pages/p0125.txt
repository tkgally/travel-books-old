His boyish, well-combed head, and delicate features, indicate little of the daring spirit of the man; but there is a close, resolute, pressure of the thin lips, a commanding glance of the eye, a sinewy wiryness of the limbs, and an activity of movement, which are in character with his bold determination and lively energies. His guard of forty are not very impressive in appearance. A military martinet might object to such a loose assortment of bravadoes, of all colors, heights, and varieties of dress. Negroes, Mulattoes, and white men mingle indiscriminately in the ranks, who, however effective in service, would not make a much fairer show on parade than Falstaff’s Ragged Regiment.

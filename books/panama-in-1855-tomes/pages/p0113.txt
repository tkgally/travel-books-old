John L. Stephens, the traveler, however, must be excepted from those who, purely with mercenary motives, early engaged in the Panama Railroad enterprise. The grandeur of the union of the two oceans, the opening of a high road to the intercourse of all the world, the binding together of distant nations, and the consequent progress of civilization, warmed his imagination, and yielding up home, its comforts and its literary ease, in which he so much delighted, and sacrificing health and life in his devotion to the cause, he looked beyond the mere temptation of present gain, to the brighter prospect of furthering the interests of humanity.

With a parsimony which has turned out not to be by any means the best economy, the railroad was commenced as a temporary expedient, and constructed as hastily and cheaply as possible, that it might receive its share of the golden shower which was just then fertilizing American enterprise.

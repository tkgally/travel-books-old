We have now the Chagres on the left, and pass through the forest, which rarely permits a view of the dark waters of the river, except here and there, where the cleared space of a settlement on its banks, as for example the village of Gorgona, along which the railroad track is laid, gives passage to the sun, and discloses the stream sparkling in its light. Leaving Gorgona and its group of thatched huts, with their moist palm-leaf eaves, distilling clouds of vapor in the hot sun, its groves of oranges and bananas ripening in the noonday heat, and its yellow-faced inhabitants swinging in their grass hammocks, or indolently lolling in the shade, we plunge again into the forest, and thence in a moment out upon the open savanna, where we reach Matachin.

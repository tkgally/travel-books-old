Yankee agents and Yankee lumber, with a fig for the picturesque, carry the day hollow against palm-trees and orange-groves.

There is the little French <i>restaurateur</i>, Monsieur Jacques, in white apron and velvet capote, suggestive of <i>eau sucrée</i> and innocent dominoes, busy over the endless job of putting up and arranging his bijou of a café in that clump of cocoa-nut trees, which shuts him out from the yellow beach. Mons. Jacques is always in a fume; but in spite of his fuss and <i>mille tonnerres</i>, it will be months yet before the Café de Taboga rivals its predecessor in Mons. Jacques's good keeping at Bordeaux.

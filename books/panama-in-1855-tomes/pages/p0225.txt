I had exhausted Panama, and was tired of looking up the street, where the view seemed fixed within the outlines of the houses like a picture in a dingy frame with a prospect, made up of a bit of blue sky showing scantily above a roof and of a dash of green sea hemmed in between two white walls; with there a tint of yellow light coming from behind the swinging sign of the “American Hotel”—which hung before the hot sun, like a patch upon an inflamed eye—and with here the rising land with its vegetation clouded in a <i>chiaro oscuro</i> of smoke. Down the street was the same spire of the gray old church, with a bush growing out of its ruined belfry. Along the overhanging balconies were the same lounging women in loose drapery, the perpetual monkeys dangling from the balustrades, the fighting-cocks drooping in the sun, and the parrots hiding their heads under their green, stagnant wings.

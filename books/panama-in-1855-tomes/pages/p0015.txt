# Panama in 1855.

# Chapter I.

“WOULD <sc>you like to go to</sc> P<sc>anama</sc>?” was the question propounded to me by a friend on one of the bitterest of the cold days of a New York January. While thawing my frozen fingers with the breath which steamed out of my mouth, and fell condensed in a rime of frost upon the beard, I warmed up at once at the proposition, and my imagination, like a migratory bird, fluttered away from its forced hibernation in my “cold, uncomfortable body,” shivering in an atmosphere which scorned double-milled pilot cloth with the coolest contempt, and sunk the thermometer into a degree of littleness to which 0 (zero) would have been an unexpected relief, and the freezing-point a hopeless aspiration.

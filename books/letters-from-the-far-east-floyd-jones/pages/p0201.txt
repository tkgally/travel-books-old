I stop writing for the present, but will resume again after leaving Penang, where we are due some time to-morrow afternoon.

<i>P. & O. Steamer</i> “<i>Hydaspes,</i>” <i>March 3rd, 1886.</i>—The approach to the harbor of Penang was an agreeable one, as we skirted along the shores of Penang Island for many miles. High hills, almost mountains, come directly down to the water's edge—heavily wooded, save where the farmer or planter has made a clearing. As we approached the anchorage we passed numerous fish wiers, made by planting stakes in a square form. I did not learn the method of getting said fish into these inclosures, but presume there is one. We anchored about 4 <sc>p. m.</sc> on the 2d, and, finding there would be a delay of five hours, a party of us started for the shore in one of the native boats, called sampan—a triangular-[[corr shapd|shaped]] affair. The stern is broader than the bow, and the former terminates in a couple of pointed and upward curved gunwales—the rower standing and facing the bow.

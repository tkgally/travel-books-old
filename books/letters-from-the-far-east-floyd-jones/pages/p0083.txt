A drive of two and a half miles, through the dusty, populous streets of the old town, brought us to this spacious and comfortable club, where I am most comfortably placed—for all of which I feel grateful to my kind friend, Mr. Pickering Clark, one of Bombay’s most useful citizens, and to whom that city largely owes her well-conducted and excellent tramway system. I’ve thoroughly inspected its stables, shops, and offices, and for good order, thorough construction, and neatness of appearance they compare well with similar systems in any section of the globe. Mr. Clark is ably seconded in his work by Mr. George A. Kittredge, another American gentleman and a twenty-five years’ resident of Bombay.

To give you an idea of the care bestowed by this company upon its horses, I can add that when the sun has great power, not an animal is hitched in—, without having a light cork hood, attached to the head stall and raised above the animal’s head: The result is that they are rarely overcome by heat and sunstroke, and while I am told the loss of animals from over-fatigue and other causes, and on street railroads, in New York City, is about 10 per cent, here it does not exceed 7.

# VI.

[[subtitle]] Aden to Bombay.

[[right]] <sc>p. and o. stmr. “nepaul,” indian ocean.</sc>

[[right]] (L<sc>at.</sc> 17° <sc>n.,</sc> L<sc>ong.</sc> 63° <sc>e., “line of no variation.</sc>”)

[[right]] <i>Sunday, December 20th, 1885.</i>

IT is not often that a Long Island man crosses the line of “no variation,” in the Indian Ocean—hence I gave it a prominent place in the heading of this letter. It is probably superfluous to tell your readers that this line is where the magnetic meridian and the true meridian coincide—or, in other words, that the needle of the compass points to the true north and there is no variation either to the right or left, as is usually the case.

We were a day late in reaching Aden, but that did not prevent our making the usual stop of six hours—time sufficient to see everything of interest. We were on shore by 2 <sc>p. m.</sc> of the 16th—landing at the post office pier.

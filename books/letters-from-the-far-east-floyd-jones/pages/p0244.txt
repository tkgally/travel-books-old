A trip up this great river has added materially to my information and opened my eyes to the numbers and extent of their river defences, for at numerous points along its course apparently strong earthworks have been erected, and they seem to have shown good judgment in selecting the best sites for its defence, often, however, omitting the important consideration of protecting themselves from an attack in the rear, and forgetting that an enemy is not likely to approach from the direction you are best prepared to resist him. The earthworks below Nan-Kin furnish an example of this character, and the same may be said of some others. At many of these military stations we have observed their men idling about, but at scarcely any did we see them engaged in drilling or performing any of the duties of a soldier. Their dress is precisely the same as any civilian Chinese (colors dark and light blue), and apparently without any marks to identify them as soldiers. There was an exception to this at one or two of the military works we passed, and at these the dark blue sack or coat had its skirts edged with a broad band of red, with a red badge upon the breast containing the number of the regiment, and I am led to believe that this is the chief distinguishing facing of most of their troops; it also extends to the retainers of many of the mandarins, as I've observed

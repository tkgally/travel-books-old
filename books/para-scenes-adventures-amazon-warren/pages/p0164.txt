Having said this he took up his gun, and in a moment the bird was floating lifeless down the stream. Putting our canoe in motion, we soon overtook it, and picked it up from the water. Instantly the snort of a disappointed alligator, only a couple of rods from our canoe, told us of the risk we had run of losing our feathered prize.

“These alligators,” said my companion, “are hungry fellows, and remarkably sharp-sighted. Not many weeks since, as I was taking a hunting trip one morning down the stream from Cajueiro, I shot a snow-white egret, which fell into the water within a few yards of my canoe. My dog, who was in the boat, plunged instantly into the stream, and began to swim with great rapidity towards the floating bird. I kept my eyes on him with intense anxiety, fearing lest he might be seen by some one of the ferocious monsters of the stream. My apprehensions were but too well founded. No sooner had the dog reached the bird than the wide jaws of an alligator appeared above the surface, and, with a suppressed but awful howl, the dog and bird sank to rise no more. For a moment the water around was crimsoned with blood, but the rapid current soon erased all traces of the catastrophe.”

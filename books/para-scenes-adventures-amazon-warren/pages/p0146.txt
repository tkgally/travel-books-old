The true friendship of our inferiors is far better and more desirable than the selfish and hypocritical concern of those who are far above us.”

“A fig for your sentimentalism!” said J.; “don’t you remember that famous parrot of Senhor P——’s, in the city? What a feathered prodigy he is! Why, I’ve heard him jabber off Portuguese by the hour, and converse much more fluently than either of us are able to do at this moment.”

“Oh, yes, I remember the bird well; he is a very large specimen, and was brought down from the Rio Negro, I believe. I heard him repeat one day several verses of poetry, and was astonished beyond measure: he is a perfect ornithological miracle, and would make his fortune by visiting foreign parts. His voice is softer than that of any other I ever saw, and his laughter is as melodious as that of a young girl.”

“I have heard marvellous accounts of the longevity of parrots,” continued J. “One is mentioned by Le Vaillant, the distinguished French naturalist, as having lived in a state of domesticity for nearly ninety years.

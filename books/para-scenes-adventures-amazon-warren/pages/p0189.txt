In passing along, we several times tripped over tangled vines lying under our feet, and scratched our limbs quite seriously against the sharp thorns with which the bushes were amply provided!

In the interior of this almost impenetrable thicket was the citadel of the beautiful ibis; and here every bush and tree had on it from five to twenty nests. These nests were about a foot and a half in diameter, and were perfectly flat. They were composed of dry twigs and fibrous roots, twined together and covered with leaves. The ibises, being disturbed by our approach, rose up in immense numbers around us, and filled the air with their cries. Although naturally very timid, yet they cannot be forced away from their rookery, and at this time they continued flying in circles but a short distance over our heads. I have no doubt that this rookery alone included at least a thousand feathered inhabitants. The plumage of the full grown ibis is of the richest crimson, with the exception of the extremities of the primary wing feathers, which are tipped with bluish-black, and form a pleasing contrast with the general scarlet hue of the bird.

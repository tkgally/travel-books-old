Without meeting with any incidents by the way, except of the sporting kind, we at length arrived at the “mills.” W. seemed glad to see us again; but oh! how he did laugh when J. related to him my snake adventure.

“Well!” said he, “that will do for John to tell his friends when he <i>gets home</i>, or will do to serve up to the marines on the passage! And,” continued he, addressing me, “allow me to insinuate in the most gentle manner in the world, that if you should ever publish an account of this singular story, it would be well for you to say, by way of embellishment, that when you fired into the snake, he became seriously indisposed; whereupon a whole <i>flock of ducks flew out of his mouth!</i>”

At three o’clock in the afternoon I left Maguary for the city. I was accompanied on this particular trip by a Scotch gentleman, who had resided at Para for many years. He was a remarkably intelligent person, and an excellent companion. His fund of anecdotes and marvellous stories seemed inexhaustible, and I experienced much pleasure in listening to their narration from his own lips.

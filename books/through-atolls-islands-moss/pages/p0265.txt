My eldest boy is pretty good at the forge, in his way, but there is less work than in the old whaling days. My other children have also to be provided for, and so in my old age I am obliged to turn trader for their sakes. What harm,” he added, “so that it be honestly done? And yet somehow I feel that the missionaries will no longer regard me with the same kindly feeling.”

“Of course,” he continued, “that won’t affect me. I shall do the best I can for my family, but still I shall be sorry for the change.” Which change of feeling, strange as it may seem, this quiet, well-informed, kindly old man looked forward to as a matter of course, and in the nature of things to be expected.

A very different man was Captain Rodd, the gentleman who came on board with Mr. Begg. A short, spare, wiry, voluble personage, over sixty years of age, full of life and action, and hard as iron.

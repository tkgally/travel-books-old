The lagoon island of Penrhyn also suffered greatly. The people are a fine manly race of the true Maori type, speak the Maori tongue and are as ready as Maoris to fight when required. Dangerous to attack, they were deceived by false promises into taking passage for a neighbouring island. Nearly all the people on Penrhyn took advantage of the opportunity and when thus decoyed on board were battened down and taken to Callao. The rest of the inhabitants were away getting <i>bêche de mer</i> on the reefs, and returning to the island found none but the old and helpless to greet them. For many a day they remained ignorant as to what the fate of their relatives had been.

Fakaafu, Nukunono, and Nukufetau have the same tale to tell. When the natives were likely to resist, they were decoyed. When too weak to resist, they were seized and torn away. Some of the Nukunono natives were at Wallis Island working for the Roman Catholic Mission, and returned to find their homes deserted and their relations gone.

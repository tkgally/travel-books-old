Germany has only in recent years entered the field as a Pacific Power. An account of the origin and growth of her influence cannot fail to be interesting. For the origin we need only go back to 1857, when the well-known Hamburg merchants, Johan Cæsar Godeffroi and Company, extended their business to the South Pacific. They were an old wealthy firm, and owned many ships trading to the East Indies and China on the one hand, and to the West Coast of South America on the other. At Cochin they had a large trade in cocoanut oil, and in South America a large trade in saltpetre, cochineal, and other rich products.

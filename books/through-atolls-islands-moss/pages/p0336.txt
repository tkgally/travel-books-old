As with the Tongans, so with all the native races in the Pacific. Conscious of their own weakness, they look to England, and place their trust in her support. They know that, despite many blunders and occasional individual wrong, her aims are high and her people mean to be considerate and just. It is a noble position and one of which a great country may well be proud. Every true Englishman, home-bred or colonial, will pray that this proud position England may ever merit and maintain.

Here I had ended my sketch of the Old Pacific and the New. Since it was written events have happened in Samoa which can only be referred to with pain and deep regret. Picking a quarrel on the most frivolous pretexts, the German squadron rendezvoused at Samoa. A large armed force was landed, and the King (Malietoa), a staunch upholder of English influence, and openly supported by the High Commissioner and British Consul, was dethroned and deported to the German possessions in Eastern Africa.

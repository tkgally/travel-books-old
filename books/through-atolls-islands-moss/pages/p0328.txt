One of her Majesty’s ships called soon after at Apiang, a neighbouring island, where dwelt Mr. Randolph, a friend of the murdered man, and who had formally taken his dying deposition. The murderer declared himself an American, and our captain, after mature consideration, decided that he had no right to interfere. Randolph, grievously hurt, declared that if the murderer put foot on the deck of his schooner he should certainly shoot him, but added, “I suppose, captain, you will then arrest me.” “Afraid I must,” was the prompt reply, “for there is no question of your being an Englishman.” This story is eminently characteristic of the present position.

Some four or five years ago the <i>Orwell</i>, an Auckland vessel, was wrecked at the island of Peru, in the Kingsmills. She had a cargo valued at 3000<i>l.</i>, including three tons of tobacco, five tons of rice, a great quantity of biscuit, sugar, preserved meats, and other articles.

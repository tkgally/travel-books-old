He so far gave way that while the custom of his forefathers required the sacrifice of all, he was content with only a part of the number. The customs of Fiji were terribly bloodthirsty and cruel in those days.

It is interesting to read to-day the account given by Commodore Wilkes of his visit to Fiji nearly fifty years ago. He speaks with admiration of the great canoes, some a hundred feet long, and of their magnificent appearance “with immense sails of white mats, and pennants streaming from their yards.” His interviews with Cakobau’s father, Tanoa, were frequent, and he tells how Tanoa came on board “almost naked, having only a small <i>maro</i> passed round his loins with long ends to it, and a large turban of white tappa, his face bedaubed with oil and ivory black, as well as his long beard and moustaches the natural hue of which was quite grey.” The commodore found it necessary to warn Tanoa against further attacks on the few scattered white people in the group, and to give him good advice generally.

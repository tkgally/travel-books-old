Travelling is entirely by boat or canoe, the mass of thickly-strewn boulders making land travel too difficult for ordinary purposes. The natives are divided into five tribes, of whom three have joined the American Mission established at the island. The other two are still heathens of the comparatively mild character that marks heathenism in these days. The Ponapè people are skilful workers, and Ponapè sleeping-mats are known over the whole Pacific. They are of peculiar make. Pandanus leaves are doubled and stitched together like thatch with one carefully-folded leaf sewn over the other so that they roll up easily, and are not only thick but soft and elastic.

The natives have abundance of turtle on their beaches, and pearl shell is found but not so worked as to yield any quantity. Tortoise shell is among their exports. The vegetable ivory nut is abundant, the sugar-cane very fine, and good coffee is produced but only on a small scale.

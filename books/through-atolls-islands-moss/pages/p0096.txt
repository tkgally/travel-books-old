Or, as more frequently happened, how the white man had made presents to his chief or king who ordered him to go with the white man wherever he might choose to take him. But in no case did I find the least ill-feeling exhibited. They were treated well on board and spoke of the kindness shown to them on the plantations where they worked. They had been sent back with wages which to them represented great wealth, were proud of their English, and as ready to “belong Peretané” as the rest of the people in these seas.

The sandal-wood trader was another important personage who played no small part in the Pacific in his day. Old, dilapidated ships, well manned and well armed, were employed for this dangerous work. Peter Dillon gives an excellent description of sandal-wood trading, in the rare and interesting account of his own experiences in the early part of the century, prior to his discovery of the remains of La Perouse.

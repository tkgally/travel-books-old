I wish,” he continued, “they could find some way of keeping their brains going. But they have enough to eat at any rate, plenty of fresh air, and no rent to pay. Now, in London, where I come from,” added he, “there was many a thousand couldn’t say as much. Look at this,” and George held up to me one of the <i>Illustrated London News</i>, which I had given him on the voyage. The illustration to which he pointed was styled, “All that is left,” and depicted a poor little half-clad Irish girl in Mayo, sitting amid the wrecks of the family furniture, before the door of a hut from which the family had just been evicted. “Talk of Manihiki ways,” cried indignant George, “isn’t that enough to make a man’s heart bleed? You may tell me it’s the law, but the chiefs of the biggest savages I know, wouldn’t be chiefs long if they made laws like that. They may club or spear their people, but no one would stand their turning out and starving women and children, like that poor little thing there.”

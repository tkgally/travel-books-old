On another of the islets of the Jaluit lagoon, about a mile from the metropolis, a gentleman acting for an American Company at Honolulu has also established himself. He has only lately been joined by his wife but the hand of woman is manifest in the taste which one seeks in vain among bachelor houses in the lagoon world. That these islets, with all their natural drawbacks, are susceptible of such improvement is encouraging to those who look forward to the changes which increased facilities of communication must bring, and to the higher social life which the presence of refined women able to cope with the inevitable seclusion, cannot fail to create.

Have I succeeded in giving a clear conception of the curious little islets which form a Pacific atoll? I would fain hope so, but feel the very great difficulty of fixing in the reader’s mind a picture so foreign to ordinary experience and so different to the conception I had myself formed. But, it may be asked, how do so many natives subsist on these islets and what resources have they to warrant a hope of future advance? The answer is easy.

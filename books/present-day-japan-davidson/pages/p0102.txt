[[cont]]but they are numerous, and the men and maids of the inn go off their heads when they come. They sleep about twenty in a room, and talk all night, and you may clap your hands till you flay them for all the attention you will get. Mountain pilgrimages, especially that to the summit of Fuji, are only summer diversions—I should say devotions—but spring and summer seem to do equally well for the others. I would give anything to go on a pilgrimage myself, Buddhist or Shinto, with impartial piety, at any time of the year. Just imagine the delight of a walking tour in Japanese dress (tucked up off the ground, of course), Japanese footgear, and Japanese lack of luggage, through Japanese scenery, though, alas!—for every rose has its thorn—on Japanese food. No, I do not wonder one bit at the popularity of this form of devotion.

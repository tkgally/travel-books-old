Inside there is a beautiful stone staircase and much fine floriated iron-work, of which a curious example is seen in the Torre dell’ Orologio close by, where two bronze warriors strike the hour with iron maces upon the bell. Beneath them is sculptured a fine colossal head of Orlando, the hero of Italian and Dalmatian romance; he is represented in plate armour. Public edicts used to be proclaimed in a little space railed off at his feet, and close by is a square pillar of masonry which supported till lately the standard of the Republic, hoisted on a tall flagstaff, as they are in the Piazza in Venice. Then there are bronze well covers that remind one of Venice, and sculptured marble fountains with more than a touch of the East upon them—a touch which tells of that close trade with the court of Adrianople for which the strict Catholics of Ragusa had to get a special permission from the Synod of Basle in 1433.

And then we waited till the baggage came up, when the man swore Zitza and its monastery was in every direction we pointed to, and finally in none, while the whole set of them loudly declared they must and would stop at the khan where we stood or go on to Ioannina. We informed them that to Zitza we would go, and off we all started, except Captain S., who stopped to impress the man a little more powerfully than before with heavenly blessings. Of course the pack-saddle immediately turned round under him and deposited him on the ground; but this we were all used to, as it happened twenty times a day, and upon any unusual movement, ‘I’m off!’ ‘Oh, and I too!’ used to be heard on all sides.

Up the long, winding, stony, baking valley we now retraced our steps, feeling considerably hotter, very much hungrier, and not a little disgusted, as we saw that by this long delay we should not get to Ioannina that evening; moreover, the mutinous voices of the men did not sound comfortable.

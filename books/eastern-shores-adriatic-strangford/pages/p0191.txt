We then mounted into a huge long clumsy boat, with very high sides and no seats in it of any kind, while the bottom was filled with puddles of water. We climbed up on a tiny poop, about as large as a good-sized desk, on which two of us could only just squeeze, and tried to shelter ourselves from the then burning sun (it was now 7.30 <sc>a.m.</sc>) under an umbrella covered with my shawl. Fortunately our eight rowers carried us down the river in less than three hours, but they seemed very long hours, for nothing could be much more disagreeable. The change from the exhilarating fresh mountain air to the stifling heat of the low level to which we had now sunk, was very great and trying; it had been hotter in the last few days in Montenegro than had been known for very many years; but it was cool on the plain of Tsetinje to the heat of the Tsernoyevitch.

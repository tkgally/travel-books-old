Another dilates with much dogmatism on the most incredible muddle in the way of statistics I ever read, given on the authority of ‘two clever men’—one clever man, indeed, could hardly have invented them—and he winds up with the convincing statement that there are ‘only ten thousand Turks in Samos.’ Ten thousand, indeed; why there are not ten score! But the whole world may see, and profit by the sight, that nobody knew or cared to say that Samos has been for thirty years past a semi-detached province in the same category as Moldavia and Wallachia, with its own prince and its own constitutional assembly; that, during the period between 1851 and 1858, after attention had been called to it by a revolt consequent on the delinquencies of a Fanariote absentee prince, it was wisely and excellently governed by a Wallachian—must I say the one Wallachian?—of integrity and patriotism, M.

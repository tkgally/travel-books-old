I spent most of my day in sketching, though I was continually tempted away for the amusement of watching the sittings of the Senate. They appeared to sit, at intervals, throughout the day: they are judges as well as lawgivers, and it was most patriarchal and primitive to see them sitting in the open street, or under the tree, or in the meadow with the peasant or peasants, whose cases they were trying, standing in the midst of them, telling their tale. Then came the discussion, sometimes a very noisy one, usually followed by a decision from Mirko, given occasionally in a voice of thunder, and, in one instance, by an instantaneous and summary infliction of punishment. This was in the case of a woman who objected to live peaceably with her husband, ‘because he was so ugly and wore such bad clothes!’ I asked the Prince what would be done to a woman who was unfaithful to her husband: he looked surprised and said simply, ‘They never are; if they had been, in former days they would have been put to death: I should imprison them for life.’

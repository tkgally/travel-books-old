I had heard at Ragusa that every plant of the Malvoisie grape, for which that city was so famous, was destroyed by the disease: and they have now to seek for grafts from some other place to which plants had been carried years ago, in order to get back their own <i>spécialité</i>. The disease attacked all the white grape vines everywhere in Dalmatia, and besides destroying the Malvoisie, it also destroyed all the <i>vugava</i> vines of the island of Brazza (the wine from which was like Frontignac, and was very celebrated), and the <i>maraschina</i> vines about Sebenico. This latter is an excellent wine or liqueur: it must not be confounded with the <i>maraschino</i>, for which Zara is so famous, which is made from black cherries. Miles and miles of vineyards along the coast have not, for some three or four years, yielded a grape; and they were then anxiously examining the vines to forecast the probable reappearance or not of the disease. As yet it had only been seen in the convent garden. There are also some very good red wines made in Dalmatia; the manufacture is rude, and they want a better class of cellar for the ripening of the wine, but some of it is full bodied and well flavoured.

Here also the awkward squads of recruits are drilled morning and evening, and here the veiled ladies disport themselves in groups among the tombstones. The view from this hill is by far the best of the city and the lake, and very lovely it is.

The bazaars are very poor, though somewhat extensive: they appeared to me to be fully stocked, but with the commonest materials—cheap cottons and cloths, miserable glass and clumsy pottery, were the chief articles, although in the few shops that contained gold embroidery there were specimens of a very superior class, at certainly a third less than the prices of Constantinople or Damascus: indeed, I never saw better embroidery anywhere. Both the town and the inhabitants are in a state of such decadence and poverty that even the Turks make no display, since there is no one worth impressing by luxuries or wealth; and for the purchase of common clothing, it is said that the peasants now chiefly go to Avlona.

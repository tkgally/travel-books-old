It was a strange interlude in the midst of the heathen surroundings. They sang two verses:—

<verse>
“How good is the Lord we adore,
Our faithful, unchangeable friend,
Whose love is as great as His power,
And knows neither measure nor end.

‘Tis Jesus, the First and the Last,
Whose Spirit shall guide us safe home,
We praise Him for all that is past,
And trust Him for all that shall come.”

Then I looked at them closely and wondered which of them would be the first to be massacred, for they were <i>en route</i> to sections of the country from which came rumours of disquiet and rebellion. But for the friendship of the two Yangtze Viceroys, they must have left their bodies unburied on the soil of Western China during the Boxers’ orgy of blood. These tranquil mandarins should receive some recognition and expression of appreciation from the governments to which these favoured citizens belong.

When we had finished the Early Rice, which tried to finish us, the river was fully ten miles wide. Rising among the Pillars of Heaven, far off on the edges of that mystical and awe-inspiring country of Thibet, the civilising Yangtze winds around three thousand miles through this land of the blue gown, then falls into the sea by the little village of Wusung.

On the “Florida” there was a good cooking stove, and it was possible to enjoy a very fair table. But the “New York” was only seven feet wide; and as two-thirds of the boat were taken up by the machinery and boiler, the small space amidships did not give sufficient room for myself and crew, and I had to tow a large dug-out alongside. In this canoe I carried some

[[cont]]of my men, with their mats and cooking-pots, two or three goats, some fowls, and last, but not least important, my cooking apparatus—a small earthenware native bowl in which my cook kept his fire and over which every dish was cooked. My cook was a native boy, named Mochindu, to whom I had imparted, to the best of my ability, the few culinary recipes which I had gathered during my travels. But his position as cook on board my boat was not an enviable one, as he was exposed to all weathers, and sometimes had to turn out a dish under the most trying circumstances.

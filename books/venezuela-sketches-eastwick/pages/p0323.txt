23. <i>Smooth-stained Cotton.</i>—This name has reference to the appearance of the seeds, which exhibit towards the base a large smooth stain. Its cotton is fine and white.

24. <i>Coarse Cotton.</i>—Called in Trinidad Hairy Cotton. Grows seven feet high and four broad, and gives only one harvest, from February to May. Remains on the tree a long time after it is matured, and is very easily cleaned, but does not yield more than two and a half ounces per tree.

25. <i>Red Downy Cotton of Siam.</i>—The cotton which it produces is of an Isabel colour, very strong and elastic.

26. <i>Muslin Cotton.</i>—In all its four varieties it is so difficult to clean that the operation has to be performed with the fingers, at the cost of sixteen hours' labour for every pound. The red variety and the Raminez Cotton (so called from the islands of that name off Cayenne) requires still more time. It only gives one crop a year, small in quantity, and not of good quality.

27. <i>Red-leaved Cotton.</i>—The tender bark of the shoots, and the leaf-veins, are flesh-coloured; a great many of the leaves and the outsides of the calices and of the capsules assume the same colour, when the cotton reaches maturity. Those which do not, become stained with red. It only yields one crop a year, from February to March, producing one ounce and three drachms per tree. Its cotton is as white and fine as the Indian cotton, but it cannot be cleaned by machine, and one pound demands at least thirteen hours of hand labour. Height, seven feet; diameter, eight feet.

28. <i>Monjas Cotton.</i>—That of Tranquebar has the leaves lobed, the lobes being lanceolate and pointed, and the side lobes jagged. That of Cambaya has the leaves divided into three elliptic and parabolic lobes, those at the side being cut into, sometimes so deeply as to make it appear that the leaf has five lobes.

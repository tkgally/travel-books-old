The High Federal Court examines causes against the Ministers of State, Diplomatic officers, and the high functionaries of the different States, declares where laws clash which is to prevail, and discharges other important duties. It consists of five members, who are chosen by the majority of the States.

The States, which form the Union, reciprocally acknowledge the rights of one another to self-government, declare themselves equal as political units, and retain in their plenitude all sovereign rights not expressly delegated by the Constitution. In one particular the English Government might, perhaps, borrow an idea from the Constitution of Venezuela. The Venezuelan Ministers have the right to speak in either Chamber of the Legislature, and are obliged to attend when called upon to give information.

The scheme of the Venezuelan Republic, further particulars of which will be found in Appendix C, looks well on paper, but unfortunately does not work well. The States are governed in the same way as the Union, and form an <i>imperium in imperio.</i> They attend but little to the orders of the general government, and have appropriated its revenue to their own purposes. Under a very determined and popular President they might be reduced to obedience, but at present they are quite beyond control.

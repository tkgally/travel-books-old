Black hair and moustache, a clear olive complexion, and regular features, do not of themselves imply anything specially attractive; but the expression of Falcon’s fine dark eyes is singularly pleasing. Without aiming at a pun, I might say that they are the eyes of a dove rather than of a falcon. Their too great softness is, however, corrected by the firmness and decision of his mouth; and, to sum up, one may say that Falcon’s physiognomy announces him to be manly, courageous, and most humane.

While the president was exchanging recognitions with the crowd around him, my friend Don Fernando whispered to me: “There’s the man who may truly say, ‘Le gouvernement c’est moi,’ for he it is who keeps the present party in power, or rather preserves Venezuela from downright anarchy. You know, congress has decreed to him the title of ‘Grand Mariscal’ of the republic, just as Bolivar was styled the ‘Liberator,’ and Paez the ‘Illustrious Citizen.’ Well! Bolivar perished in exile, and almost in want of the necessaries of life. Paez has long been a fugitive. It remains to be seen what will be the fate of the Grand Mariscal.”

After the president had greeted his friends, and had been told who I was, he stepped up to me very affably, and inquired if I spoke Spanish.

Melons and cucumbers, pumpkins and gourds, lie about, piled like shot in an ordnance yard. The people eat cucumbers as the English peasant would an apple, evidently considering them as fruit; and it is curious to see how often the little donkey, laden with these cool green vegetables, is stopped by eager customers.

The Khan was a great dusty, freshly-painted place, with a handsome <i>café</i> opposite, before which sat crowds of richly-dressed Turkish merchants, Osmanlee officers, and Greeks in European dress, smoking splendid narghillys with rich tubes, costly amber mouth-pieces and magnificent cut glass

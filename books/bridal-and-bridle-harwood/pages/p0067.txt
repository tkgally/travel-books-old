Near Ruma the people insisted that we were Russians, to which we gravely assented. There was a clear blue sky as we left Ruma, with peculiarly still, thick, white, heavy clouds, listless and immoveable, such as we never see in England.—A very tropical sky indeed it was, and looked scorchingly hot. The road wound through immeasurable fields of bright gaudy flowers—a wide plain enamelled with waving azure leaves and green stems. At Golnbinitz the postmaster told us, that only about three travellers passed annually. No wonder we found such difficulty in obtaining horses on the road.

At length from a steep hill we caught sight at once of Semlin—of the broad and noble river, and of the Ottoman fortress and Servian city of Belgrade, on the opposite bank.

From habit, the Tartar becomes so absolute a centaur, that it is as difficult to divorce him from his saddle as from his wife—perhaps more so.

We did not find the road equal to an English highway, certainly; and the passage of a small river seemed likely to be as fatal to us, as that of Inachus to the Argive king. The opposite bank was steep and slippery; the mud, as well as the water, deep; and the strong grey horses vainly strove and toiled to drag the zelica on dry land.

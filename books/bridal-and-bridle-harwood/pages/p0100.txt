Our people lay down anywhere on the ground outside the stable-door. At about half-past six, Iovon awoke us; we made a hurried toilet, drank a hasty cup of tea, and were soon ready, and once more in the saddle.

To eat was impossible—the intense heat and fatigue took away the power. This was fortunate in one respect, as eggs do not abound at Grojsko; and as for meat, you might as well ask for beefsteaks in a Brahmin's house, or a Banyan hospital.

We rode on to Kolar, where we dined on a piece of soddened kid in water, a lettuce, and some cherries.

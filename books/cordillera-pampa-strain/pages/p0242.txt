# Chapter XV.

[[subtitle]] San Luis.

<i>March</i> 17.—Breakfasted about 11 <sc>a. m.</sc>, and finding the heat too great for a comfortable walk, I remained in the <i>fonda</i> until evening, when I called at the government house to have my passport “<i>viseed.</i>” The troops stationed at the door had neither the brigandish air of those of Mendoza, nor were they so uncivil and exacting. I passed in freely without a <i>devisa</i>, to which no allusion was made during my stay, and I even saw a man with a full beard, a strong evidence of the liberality of the authorities. The officials, the most important of whom was the Minister, or Secretary of State of the province, were courteous. Having performed this duty, I took a walk through the city to observe it and the people, who were generally seated at their doors enjoying the cool air of the evening. It is regularly laid out in squares, the houses of one story, in some cases tiled and whitewashed, but more generally thatched, and the natural colour of the <i>adobe</i>. As in Mendoza, gardens are generally attached to the houses, which being surrounded by a sombre clay wall, gives to the streets a dry and arid appearance, not out of character with the temperature of the place. The floors of the houses are of pounded clay, or half-burnt tiles, productive alike of coolness and dirt.

Many winding roads lead from the still lee sands to the windward, heavy seas eternally dashing on the dark greenish-brown coral shore-rocks that line the <i>Licoo</i>[[fnref 1]]—the opposite side of the island. The walk across is a very charming one. It passes numbers of pandanus trees,

[[image: photograph of bread-fruit hanging among large lobed leaves]]
[[caption]] Bread-fruit.

[[cont]]whose roots, growing out of the main trunk three or four feet above the ground, and sometimes shooting down from the branches, look like sticks supporting the tree. Over the pathway the bread-fruit hangs down so invitingly that I reach up and pull one off the tree, intending to take it on board the steamer and have it cooked for dinner; but it is too heavy to carry in the hot weather, and I am glad to lay it down.

[[fn 1]] <i>Licoo</i> is the rocky, windward side of an island, inaccessible to boats. It is also applied to any inaccessible part.

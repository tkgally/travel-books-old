This was the land which Macarthur shrewdly took up, calling it Camden, after the man who had given him his first solid encouragement. He bought a ship, which he named the <i>Argo</i>, and sailed for Sydney with the sheep he had acquired from the King’s stud at Kew, and many fruits and plants to experiment with in the new land.

It was on this Camden estate that, after many more vicissitudes and some years of parliamentary activity, he settled down for the rest of his life. He built the fine house which now stands here, but died at the age of sixty-seven, in a small cottage hard by, before it was finished. But he had lived to see his labours in the cause of Australian wool-growing bearing rich fruit, and his plantations of vines and olives growing lustily. He was a man of hasty temper, but of tremendous energy and patriotism, far-seeing, resourceful, and sanguine. He is justly called “the Father of the Colony,” for Australia owes an immense debt to him.

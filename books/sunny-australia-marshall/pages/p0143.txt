## XVIII

My last experience, before sailing for Western Australia on my homeward way, was also one of the pleasantest. It was a long motor trip to the northern rivers of New South Wales, with Mr. Adrian Knox as host and Capt. Wemyss, of H.M.S. <i>Cambrian</i>, and myself as guests. We had all three come out together, and the skeleton of this trip had been laid months before in the smoking-room of the <i>Osterley</i>. It had been my first experience of the infinite amount of trouble your Australian friends will take to put you in the way of things; but I had not been prepared for an arrangement by which everything was done for me during a ten days’ journey, with the best of company thrown in.

I remember so well the pictures I had formed in my mind of motoring for days through the bush, as we talked it over on ship-board with the maps in front of us. I had been in the bush in New South Wales twenty years before, in a district fairly well settled, but I did not remember any roads to speak of; and I had ridden in those days many miles over bush tracks in Queensland.

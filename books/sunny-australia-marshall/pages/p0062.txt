Most of the guests of the New South Wales Government on this trip were seeing the Australian bush for the first time. We started in the late afternoon, and had sight of the wooded shores and various indentations of the harbour and the beautiful Parramatta river before dusk fell. But that is not the Bush, although it is called so.

We woke up to it. We had arrived some time in the night at Werris Creek, and when I opened my eyes we were at a standstill on a siding.

Here was the Bush—the wide, flat landscape of all shades of brown and purple shot with vivid spring green, the bright sun, the heavenly freshness of the morning air, filled with indescribable scents from the soil and the vegetation, the brooding stillness, broken only by the fluting of the Australian magpie, most musical of all bird sounds. I never felt happier than at that moment. The fascination of it came upon me with a rush, and I was suddenly twenty years younger.

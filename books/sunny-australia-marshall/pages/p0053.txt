Australia seems to have developed a new ideal for a State Governor of late years, and it must be a somewhat arduous one to live up to. In the case of New South Wales and Victoria, where the Governor-General has his official residences, the State Governor has been, since the federation, somewhat eased of the burden of lavish entertaining, although he does his abundant share: but apart from his official work, which is heavier than might be supposed, and the innumerable public engagements he has to fill, he is expected to take a keen interest in the various interests of his State and in its development; and he usually responds nobly to the expectation. Lord Chelmsford, who had just exchanged the Governorship of Queensland for that of New South Wales when I reached Australia, told me about certain developments of agriculture and certain parts of the country which were then being opened up in a way which showed his keen interest in the subject and his complete mastery of it.

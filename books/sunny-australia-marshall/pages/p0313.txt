But look what the men of theory have done! It was Professor Lowry, of the Roseworthy College, in South Australia, who initiated the system of growing wheat under superphosphates. It took him a long time to get his theories put into practice. The old rule-of-thumb farmers would have none of them, and it was only when the pupils whom he had trained began to go on to the land that they made way. And what is the result? Thousands of acres of land, which were previously thought useless for any practical purpose whatever, are now growing fine crops, and supporting a comparatively large population.

## V

If Australia owes any one a statue it is to Mr. Farrer, who died not long since. He was an Englishman of good family, a schoolmaster, I think, in Sydney, but I have not been able to find anything about him in print, and do not even know his Christian name. He retired, and on a small farm of his own conducted experiments in wheat breeding which have had far-reaching effects all over the continent.

Even in the Australian spring, which begins in September, when everything is springing with quick life, you miss those myriad tints of delicate green. And you miss the birds, so clamorous in a dewy English garden or orchard.

The house, built on one floor, with double walls to keep out the summer heat, and broad, shaded verandahs, looked over a wide bay with the blue hills beyond. Inside it you were in an English house, although you were five weeks’ journey from England. And that is typical of Australian homes; not even the great differences in climate have brought about any change. Australians are the most conservative of the British races in this respect, and in a surprising number of others. Their very clothes remain the same, although in the hotter regions they wear fewer of them. Except in the big cities, houses are mostly of one story, and the broad verandahs are used both by day and night during the summer. To that extent only has the British race adapted itself to the altered conditions.

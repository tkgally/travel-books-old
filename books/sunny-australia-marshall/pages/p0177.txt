[[cont]]meet the retail tradesman in the more exclusive clubs, nor elsewhere amongst the upper classes of metropolitan society. But in the country towns he is amongst those at the top of the social tree. You may meet on shipboard a man who was apprenticed in his youth to a trade in a small English country town. He is returning to a country in which he has built up just such a trade as he was trained for. He has made a fortune. He is probably a magistrate in the district in which he lives, possibly a member of the Upper House of Legislature. And he, too, can talk about the land, and has interests in it. He, least of all men, would go back to carry on his work in the old country, and to the subserviency which is still expected from the retail trader. He lives a much wider, freer life. His sons have their horses to ride, and can have their farms if they please. They are not shut up to the minor pleasures of an English country town, with little money in their pockets, and little hope of a career except in their fathers’ shops or in clerkships or shops elsewhere. The whole great continent of Australia lies open for them, and no manner of work carries with it any social stigma.

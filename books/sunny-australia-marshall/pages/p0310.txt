## III

In February—not before, for fear of danger to the growing crops—they put a fire through the now dead timber and brushwood which covers the ground. With a “good burn” nothing is left but wood ash, which fertilizes the ground, but sometimes there has to be a “pick up” after the fire has run through.

Now the ground is ready for ploughing. It is still full of stumps, but the Australian plough makes nothing of these. It is an affair of discs or shares up to sixteen in a row, and is drawn by as many as ten horses if it is to plough up that number of furrows. If one of the ploughshares kicks against a stump it is automatically tilted up and comes down again into the soil on the other side. The kick it gives to the stump loosens it a little, and the crop pushing up through the ground does the rest.

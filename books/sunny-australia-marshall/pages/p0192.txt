You must get used to galvanized iron in this country, for rain water has to be saved, and there is no other kind of roof that will do it so well. The only thing to be said in its favour from the picturesque point of view is that in single-storied buildings with a low pitch and a wide span it is most easy to get used to. But the wide space over which the buildings are spread and the beautiful trees save the situation. There are giant evergreen bunyas, graceful eucalypts, like towering birches, a few English oaks, native silky oaks with their deep-yellow flower tresses, full of honey, and the wonderful jacaranda, which is one huge, spreading mass of bloom the colour of wistaria. It is not difficult to make any place beautiful in this country, in spite of galvanized iron.

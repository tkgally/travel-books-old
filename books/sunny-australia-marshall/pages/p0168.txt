Perhaps I should, but I doubt it. Our descendants may go faster, by sea or through the air, and ships may be bigger. But as to the rest, we must pretty well have reached the limit of comfort on a sea-voyage. Except for sea-sickness, and not being able to get away from the ship—that is to say, except for the fact that you are at sea—you live just as you would at a good hotel; and the liveliest credulity as to the wonders that a scientific age has still to bring forth fails to hit upon any that will improve that, except in the matter of making the good hotel still better, which is being done every year.

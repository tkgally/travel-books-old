How was this? The only explanation I could think of was that they must have got later news, and learned that Stanley had changed his plans as to which of the two roads he would take. Here was a dilemma! My intention all along was to elude these people; meet Stanley first, avoid them again on the return, and secure a journalistic victory worth having. Now, however, there was plainly nothing for it but to follow after them and find out why they had taken this route.

We traveled hard till dusk, when our ears were greeted by the blare of a bugle ahead, and soon we were in sight of their camp at Usigwa. It was a military camp, and in the center of it were two fine large tents and several smaller ones, surmounted by German flags. One of the big tents belonged to Baron von Gravenreuth, the German officer commanding the expedition, and second in command to Capt. Wissmann; the other belonged to Mr. Vizetelly of the <i>Herald</i>.

The Baron and his brother officers were making their toilets preparatory to the evening meal. They hurried out of their tents, and crowded together in eager surprise at the apparition of a dusty European and five natives trudging into camp. The Baron spoke very good English.

# Chapter XII.

[[subtitle]] Arabs and Slaves.

ASIDE from my quest of Stanley, part of my commission to Africa was to see what I could of the slave-trade. I endeavored to investigate and report upon it from as many sides as was possible within the scope of actual observation.

All African travelers, and even many missionaries, agree, that once the slave reaches his destination in Zanzibar, Pemba, or Arabia, his or her condition is often, though not always, improved. The violence and bloodshed of the raids in the interior, and the hardships and misery of the long march to the coast, are the revolting features of the slave-trade. It was the author’s privilege to see something of both the darker and the brighter sides, as well as to take a glance at the subject from the standpoint of an old Arab slaver.

One Sunday I accepted an invitation to accompany a party of Europeans to an Arab shamba on a day’s outing. A shamba is a country estate, or plantation. Spending a Sunday at an Arab shamba is a favorite form of recreation with the European residents of Zanzibar. The one to which we had been invited was situated about ten miles northeast of Zanzibar, near the shore.

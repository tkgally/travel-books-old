One evening the conversation turned on the subject of people keeping or breaking their promises. This is the sort of theme that a man of Stanley’s character enjoys. His face assumed a look of calm relish, and he at once dropped into a bantering vein of quiet sarcasm at the expense of nearly everybody present, as well as a few people who were not with us in the flesh.

“It’s curious,” said Stanley, “but hardly anybody ever keeps promises but me. In an African expedition it would make all the difference in the world if people could be depended on to keep their promises; and the results of their failure to do so are often most disastrous. Now, take the case of the Pasha (Emin) there, and Jephson. When I left them, on our second visit to the Albert Nyanza, to return for the rear column, they both promised very readily, and without hesitation, that they would come away in a couple of months or so, and retire to Fort Bodo to meet me there—but they didn’t come.

“MY friends,” said the Major, as we crossed the Ai-oi Bridge, the morning following our arrival in Kobe, “although you may not be aware of the fact, we are nevertheless in one of Japan’s ancient capitals.”

As our visit to Kobe was necessarily brief, we had taken an early breakfast and started for Hyogo, which joins Kobe on the southwest, and may at present be regarded as a continuation of that great seaport town, which lies at the western gate of the Inland Sea.

It was in 1180, shortly after the destruction of the palace in Kioto by fire, that the powerful regent Kiyomori removed the capital to Hyogo for a period of six months, after which it was transferred back to Kioto, where it remained for over 600 years, and until changed to Tokio in 1868.

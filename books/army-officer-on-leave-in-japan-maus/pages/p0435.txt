“WHEN Buddhism,” said the Judge, in continuing his discourse on the native religions, the following evening, “drifted across seas from Korea, the Land of the Morning Calm, with its rich argosies of learning, culture, and arts, it found benighted Japan struggling against barbarism, dense ignorance, and internal dissensions. Although there was the semblance of a government, with headquarters in the Province of Yamato, no cohesion existed between it and the unruly tribes which constituted the nascent nation, nor indeed was there any permanency to the capital itself, which until 710 <sc>a. d.</sc> was migratory in character.

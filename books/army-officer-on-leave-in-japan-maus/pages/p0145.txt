LONG before the last golden ray of the setting sun had disappeared below the western horizon, Formosa had faded away into the southern seas and while the gallant old ship was bravely ploughing her way to the north, the passengers again settled down to the tranquil life of a quiet sea.

Sixty miles north of Formosa we passed the rocky island of Hoo Pin Su, while to the east, lying fifty miles away, began the chain of the Loochoo islands which for many centuries have belonged to Japan, and were the initial point where Saint Francis Xavier began his missionary work with the Japanese in 1545.

We had now been out from Mariveles sixty hours, had reeled off seven hundred and fifty miles of the one thousand two hundred and fifty from Corregidor to Nagasaki and expected to reach port about three o’clock on the second afternoon.

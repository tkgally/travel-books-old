Chamberlain quotes also a book called <i>The Practical Use of Conversation for Police Authorities</i>, which furnishes the following admirably suggestive dialogue between a representative of the force and an English sailor:

<blockquote>
“What countryman are you?
I am a sailor belonged to the Golden Eagle, the English man of war.
Why do you strike Jinrikisha-man?
He told me impolitely.
What does he told you impolitely?
He insulted me, saying loudly, ‘the Sailor, the Sailor,’ when I am passing here.
Do you striking this man for that?
Yes.
But do not strike him for it is forbided.
I strike him no more.”
</blockquote>

It is perhaps owing to the occurrence of such dialogues that the Japanese Government has issued an edict prescribing the deportment of the people to foreign visitors on their shores.

This curious document lays down the following rules of behaviour:

<blockquote>
“Loungers shall not crowd around foreigners. Merchants must not charge them excessive prices. People must refrain from throwing stones at the dogs which accompany strangers, who are to be treated with courtesy
</blockquote>

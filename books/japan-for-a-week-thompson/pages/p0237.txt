The most fascinating personality on the ship proved also the most elusive. This was Admiral Sah, a slim, sleek, sly Oriental man, smooth as oil, uncannily suave, and inscrutable as the Sphinx. There was something mysteriously attractive and memorable in his sensitively ascetic and delicately alert countenance, his lithe grace of step, and his feline softness of motion.

He became to me a haunting obsession. It seemed that I never looked up from my book without finding him gliding by, with furtive tread, and obsequious bow, a slow, wise smile, and purr of lowly apology.

On many such occasions I tried conversation. But though the Chinese gentleman was always beautifully polite, he always contrived to parry the “leading” questions and to wriggle away without saying anything.

“You have been to Europe before?” I asked on the first of these occasions.

“Yes, I have lived in London,” he answered, with the urbanity of a Louis XV <i>abbé.</i>

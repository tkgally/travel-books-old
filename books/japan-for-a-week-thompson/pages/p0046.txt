The Tsar’s own peace address, summoning the Hague Conference, was “censored” out of the Russian Press.

Everything that is printed must be submitted to the Censor, music as well as literature, visiting cards, announcements of funerals and weddings—everything.

A novelist who had dared to jest about the decoration of the seats in the Tsarkoe-Selo had his book suppressed on the ground that “the design of these seats had been honoured by the supreme approbation of the Tsar.”

There doubtless are revolutionary forces at work in the Empire; but my information represents them as much weaker in numbers, and more divided in aim, than we are commonly led to believe.

I met one man who assured me that “all the educated classes were socialistic”; but when I questioned him as to the details of their Socialism, I found it evaporate into aspirations that would be enthusiastically backed by Mr. Balfour.

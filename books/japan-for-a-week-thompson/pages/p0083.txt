The sonorous charm of the grasshopper’s never-ending song took on a note of sadness when I looked down on the lights still burning in the picturesque workshops of the poetic writers’ raptures.

The wrinkled, mummified, old, old faces and bent backs of the old people in the streets had begun, too, to prey upon my imagination. There appeared to be no middle-aged people in Japan. From the chattering, light-hearted, sturdy young rickshaw boys and giggling waitresses of the hotel, Japanese humanity seemed to drop in one sudden <i>débacle</i> to senility and decrepitude.

The workers here evidently cannot become “too old at forty,” for none of them seem to attain to that age; those that have passed twenty, especially the women, look about a hundred.

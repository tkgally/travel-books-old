# The Inland Sea

THE two days’ sail from Kobe to Nagasaki is probably the most glorious water trip the world affords. I had thought the Caledonian Canal fine, and Lock Katrine exquisite, but hereafter when I want a phrase to express the superlative in seascapes I shall quote the Inland Sea of Japan.

The Inland Sea divides the main island of the Empire from the two next largest. It is a hundred and twenty miles wide in some places, and in others no more than a mile. It is thickly studded with small islands of irregular coast-lines, deeply indented with gulfs, bays, and inlets.

There was a day, a mellow October day, when the sweet drowsiness that daily followed lunch was suddenly broken by some wakeful passenger’s cries of admiration, and two minutes later every deck chair was empty, the usually deserted bows of the ship were thronged with excited people, and every camera on board was clicking impressions of an enchanted archipelago.

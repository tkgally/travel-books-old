The tradespeople with whom we had dealings were invariably polite, patient, and obliging to a degree unprecedented in my European experience. It is true they always asked more for their wares than they expected to get, but their haggling was done very gently, very good-humouredly, and with an air of going through an inevitable formality, rather than with any apparent intention to take advantage.

The Cornish lady of whom I have made mention would ask: “How much do you want for this?”

And the bowing, smiling Oriental would reply: “Sicketty yen, if you please. It is a fine day, yes, I am sure, ha! ha!”

The Cornish lady, scorning the meteorological reference, and sticking to business, would retort sharply: “Give you thirty.”

“Tharty yen? Yes, yes, that cannot be. It is very pretty goods—yes, yes, ha, ha, very pretty. What you give?”

“I said I would give thirty yen. Didn’t you understand?”

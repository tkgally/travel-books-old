The man’s earnestness and confidence amused me at the moment; but it struck me afterwards as a serious symptom, too. It was typical of many signs and portents.

If the wide-stretched British frontier has any vulnerable spot, at home or overseas, that spot will, before many years, be discovered and attacked. There may be no war. The “incident” may be as quiet as the pegging back of Russian influence in the Balkan States after the Japanese war. But for that vulnerable spot Britain will have to pay the price. That is sure as the tide, sure as sunrise, sure as death.

It is the inevitableness of the clash and conflict of interests that makes the situation so tragic. The German aims are, according to the modern standard of international morals, entirely legitimate and unexceptionable. The Germans are only proposing to do what the British have done in the past; and their claims can only be answered by submission, or by opposition of strength to strength.

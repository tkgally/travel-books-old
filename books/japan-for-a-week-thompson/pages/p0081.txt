Everything looks clean, fertile, prosperous, and strange. That is the wonder of it. The trees are bent and twisted into shapes all unfamiliar. The temples are shaped by ideals foreign to the architects of Europe. The villages are bizarre beyond any comparison I can think of. Trees, rocks, mountains, and even skies, show an irregularity of form and softness of outline that I can only describe by the one word—Japanese. That is the pith of the marvel we found in this southern part of the country: Japan was Japanese.

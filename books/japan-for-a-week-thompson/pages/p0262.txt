“I know,” I answered. “But what is the fare?”

“Well,” he said, still laughing, “five cents.”

I returned to Wai Lee and Ah Lie. They were still protesting, though with less confidence. I referred them to the policeman.

Changing their tone they furnished me with touching statistics as to the number of their wives and families. I said: “You should have mentioned these painful but irrelevant facts before you quenched my simple faith in human nature by a five-mile drive through pelting rain. Gentlemen both, you have your legal fare, and wild horses of Tartary would not drag another cash out of me. Farewell. Be virtuous and you will have better luck.”

With this scathing shaft of irony I shook a quart or two of rain out of my hat, and proceeded with stately dignity to parade the great Queen’s Road.

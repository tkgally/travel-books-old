<verse>
“And very often we have heard
How men are killed and undone
By overturns of carriages,
By thieves and fires in London.
We know what risks all landsmen run,
From noblemen to tailors,
Then, Bill, let us thank Providence
That you and I are sailors!”
</verse>

Indeed, I felt much more fear than I had ever done at sea when, soon after my voyage home, I crossed the Bay of Carnarvon in a small sailing-boat. It was blowing “a stiff breeze,” and the boat was manned by amateur navigators. How (as one of our poets on board was wont to say, in describing a moment of intense excitement)—

<verse>
“My heart went out within me,
Like a spark dropped in the sea!”
</verse>

and how I expected every moment, as I clung to the windward side, also <i>to drop in the sea!</i>

I shall never forget my drive home at sunset from the scene of the pic-nic. Since my accident I had grown timid, and was thankful to obtain a seat in the steadiest and most jog-trot conveyance of the whole <i>cortége.</i> I sat facing the cavalcade, lost in admiration of the picture before me. Lovely ladies, well mounted, all bathed in one flood of fire—the roseate light enhancing their beauty—and horsemen, were vieing with each other in equestrian feats; leaping over every fence or fallen tree with as much ease and grace as I ever saw displayed by the most accomplished hunter or huntress at home. Altogether, the picture before me carried me back in imagination to tales of <i>merrie England in olden times</i>—and certainly struck me more than did anything approaching to pageantry I ever saw in Melbourne.

# Chapter V.

[[subtitle]] The Boiling Lake of Dominica.

[[subtitle]] A Wild Cat.—Tree-ferns.—Mountain Palms.—A Rare Humming-bird.—The Valley of Desolation.—Misled by a Bottle.—Boiling Springs.—Hot Streams.—Sulphur Baths.—The Solfatara.—Building the Ajoupa.—Cooking Breakfast in a Boiling Spring.

<verse>
Dominica’s fire-cleft summits
Rise from bluest of blue oceans;
Dominica’s palms and plantains
Feel the trade-wind’s mighty motions,
Swaying with impetuous stress
The West Indian wilderness.
. . . . . . . .

Dominica’s crater-caldron
Seethes against its lava-beaches;
Boils in misty desolation;—
Seldom foot its border reaches;
Seldom any traveler’s eye
Penetrates its barriers high.
</verse>

[[right]] L<sc>ucy</sc> L<sc>arcom</sc>.

THE record of the weather for a month: showery, cool and delightful. On the coast it was ten degrees hotter; but in this elevated valley, two thousand feet above the sea, the eastern peaks caught the flying clouds from the “trades” and precipitated their burden of moisture.

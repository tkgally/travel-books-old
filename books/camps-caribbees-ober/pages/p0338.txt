It states that “<i>Messire Joseph Gaspard de Tascher, chevalier, seigneur de La Pagerie</i>, native of the parish of <i>St. Jacque du Carbet</i>,—of said island of Martinique, lieutenant in the artillery, son in legitimate marriage of <i>Messire Joseph-Gaspard de Tascher, chevalier, seigneur de La Pagerie</i>, and of <i>Madame Marie-Françoise Boureau de La Chevalerie</i>, living in the town of Port Royal,” was married to “<i>demoiselle Rose-Claire des Vergers de Sannois</i>, native of the parish of Trois-Ilets, daughter in legitimate marriage of <i>Messire Joseph des Vergers de Sannois</i> and of <i>dame Marie-Catherine Brown</i>, natives of and dwellers in the parish of Trois-Ilets,” etc.

Thus we have in this register of marriage, dated November the ninth, 1761, the names and rank of the parents and grandparents of Josephine, and, what is of equal importance, their place of residence at that time, only eighteen months previous to her birth.

Let us turn for a moment to her biographers. One or two will suffice to show how inaccurate are their statements. Thus, in “Memoirs of the Empress Josephine,” by John S. Memes, LL.D., I find that the parents of the Empress were—“both natives of France, though <i>married in St. Domingo</i>, about 1761.” . . . “Of this parentage, the only child, the subject of these Memoirs, was born in St. Pierre, the capital of Martinico, on the 23d of June, 1763.”

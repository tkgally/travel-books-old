Later, returning to Martinique, he found his place occupied by another, and was, as a mark of confidence, appointed <i>procureur général</i> of the mission. In this capacity he visited all the isles of the Antilles, French, English, and Dutch; but passed the greater portion of his time in Martinique and Guadeloupe. In 1703 he founded the town of Basse Terre, and took an active part in the defence of the island against the attack of the English, in March of the same year. The “Bellicose <i>Père Blanc</i>,” as he was called by the people of the island, could not prevent his monastery from being burned, by which disaster he lost all his books, manuscripts, and instruments. He returned to France in 1705, resided in Paris and Rome, and in the former city prepared his different voyages for publication. He there died in 1738. His most important work, “<i>Nouveau Voyage aux Iles d’Amérique</i>,” is as valuable as it is at the present time rare. It was published in Paris in 1722. He wrote, besides, six large works of travel, chiefly from the manuscripts of other travelers. A genus of plants, containing a species indigenous to Cuba and one to Cayenne, was named, in his honor, <i>Labatia</i>. The old Basilique remains, in defiance of earthquakes and hurricanes, a monument to the activity and zeal of this good father. Its front, however, was rebuilt a few years ago.

[[cont]]Meyong’s skin was proclaimed by that worthy in a howl of dismay.

Meyong was a good boy and generally very tractable, but he would never listen to my advice and wear his shirt inside his pantaloons. He said it was the <i>fashion</i> to wear it outside, and used an expression equivalent to that in so common use among the ladies: “To be out of fashion is to be out of the world.”

I argued with him and entreated him, but in this he would have his own way; and I really believe that if every man in the Carib nation were bitten by a bee every day in his mortal life, he would still persist in displaying a flag of distress above his nether coverings.

And thus he went on, with alternate howls and exclamations of sweeter character, such as <i>miel douce</i>, (honey sweet,) until the great palm troughs were full enough and I concluded it would be well to desist.

Early in the proceedings he had whistled shrilly several times, and when I asked the reason, he said it was to call the <i>mal fini</i>. “Mal fini” was the name given to the hawk, from its cry; but this applied to a small bird of the fly-catcher family, which would come and eat the bees and thus diminish the number of Meyong’s assailants. The bird came, a small, shy, gray bird, which approached cautiously, evidently astonished to see a human being up in a tree surrounded with smoke, and another at the foot of the tree. But he did not stop to speculate, but worked assiduously, and soon he was joined by others; though their united efforts failed to lessen perceptibly the angry swarm.

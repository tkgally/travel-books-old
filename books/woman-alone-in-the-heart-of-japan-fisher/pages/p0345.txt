Her little tool-box held a scalpel and a probe, two pairs of scissors, and two of forceps. A specimen hardened in alcohol was ready for drawing. Specimens are certainly plentiful, and one might hope for original work and life study in a land where the cicada singeth unceasingly. Alas! the little ladies are not so science-trained. Perhaps they have not the nerve to tear a creature limb from limb. The cicada had been drawn and quartered, labelled and analyzed in advance, and all his parts were duly named upon the blackboard, “dorsal-ventral-egg-guide,” and again the work was conscientious copy rather than original research. Carefully each pupil sketched, and results were of varied skill. Some were marked by a trained eye and steady hand and a keen observance of winged venation, while others were decidedly imaginative, and indulged in wild flights of colour, blue and red-yellow entering into the scheme, not apparent in the original insect, but suggestive of the solar spectrum. English labels were strangely distorted, but the effort was always honest.

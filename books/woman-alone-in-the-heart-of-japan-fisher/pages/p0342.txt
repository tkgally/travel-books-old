Pattering cautiously in my stockings among the frames, I watched wonderful results grow from satin background. Bird and flower and landscape were evolved with taste and patience and consummate skill on cover, screen, and kakemono.

## <i>Drawing</i>

Drawing, too, is very dear to the Japanese, though, strangely enough, they know nothing of life classes, nature and object work. They usually work from the flat copy, and seldom do they really sketch. Twice I saw a sad attempt to copy a stuffed bird from the show-case. The results were pitiful. The spirit of the feathered fowl would never have recognized himself in the “impressionist” picture, which violated all ornithological conditions. The people are keen copyists, but they have done little with the natural form, which we believe is the foundation of art.

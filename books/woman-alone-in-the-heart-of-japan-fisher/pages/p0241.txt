## <i>Back at Kindayu’s Inn</i>

Never was home more attractive to tired traveller than the cleanly Kindayu house, perched on a parapet, with its real bed and spotless linen. Heihachee left me with an added credential in his budget. He offered to return the sardines and the unused tin of butter. Honest old soul! He said he would keep the bread of his own make, to which we had resorted when the loaves gave out. He threw back his pyramidal head and grinned among the parchment wrinkles, as he said, “Oh, too bad, too bad, Oksan no like, no could eat.” I lied heroically, and said that they were very nice, but I was not hungry for any more. As I analyze the sentence, I believe there was an unconscious glimmer of truth in the statement. I had bolted one down, with saintly grace, to save his feelings, but the memory of that dreadful dab of heavy brown dough will be a terrifying souvenir. I turned with joy to the Kindayu menu, strung with pearls of French which would have astonished the ears of the Académie.

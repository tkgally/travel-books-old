While trying to kill a worthy hero, he found that his would-be victim was an abler fencer than himself, and, from admiration for superior power, he became the hero’s devoted henchman. Benkei settled on the schoolboy trick of depriving the old monks of their monastery bell, so he carried it to the mountain’s top, and beat a hideous racket all the night. The despairing priests pleaded for their treasure, and he promised to surrender the bell if they would make him all the bean porridge he could eat. So they filled him a soup tureen five feet in diameter. Tragic pictures show Benkei in every stage of his crime, and saké cups are sold in triplets to impress his infamy, showing him scrambling wildly up the mountain bearing the big bell, sitting on the height banging the tom-toms, and again delighting in his big porringer.

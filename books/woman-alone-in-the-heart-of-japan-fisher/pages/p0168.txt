The bird claws were brown and shrivelled, as if a snake-skin had been drawn over them, and to follow their tracery was impossible.

“Tank you pleeze varee much,” she said, as we closed the lesson. She was a bundle of good manners and etiquette, but, when caught off her guard, her face in repose had the stern stoicism of a brave samurai. She was the famed coquette of the town, dainty and mincing, with sweet and gentle voice, and the grace of a true-born siren. Her wily ways bowled over the strongest men like ninepins. She submerged them with her wooden wares, and loaded them with trays of carved monkeys and boxes engraved with waterfalls and bridges, which they could not escape.

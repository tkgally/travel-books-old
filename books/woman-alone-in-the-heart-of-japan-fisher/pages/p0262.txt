## <i>A Music Lesson</i>

Of equal interest, and of equal difference from anything we know in the name of music, is the other lesson of the little people, and, as catcalls sounded through the thin partitions, I entered the music-room, to watch the stiff gestures of the clubs, raised parallel, perpendicular, at right angles, to fall with a bang on the drums, and perhaps stir those famed forty-seven ronins from their long, cemetery sleep. Near by stood the native hibachi, and, as the lesson ended, the teacher drew the finely shredded weed from her pouch and bent her long pipe in the embers of the brazier, to puff contentedly the three little puffs which are the native pipe's capacity. Her honest effort had earned her the comfort which came with the smoke.

Other musical aspirants crooned their dismal wails above stringed instruments, and another old lady struck shrill falsetto notes for them to follow.

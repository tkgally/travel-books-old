## <i>Celebrating the Birth of the Crown Prince’s Baby</i>

The day after the birth of the Crown Prince’s baby, all the land was rejoicing. Many weeks had the people discussed the coming event, and the faithful subjects had longed for a baby boy. When the glad news went forth by wire and by press, everybody gave up to a day of feasting and delight. The humblest home floated the national banner, and mirth and music were in order.

As I passed the shop, I tried to slide unnoticed by the collector. But I heard a dash from the door, and the clatter of clogs, and there came the friendly call, “Come in please; do please come see,” and he led me by another door to an unknown realm, cleanly as the shop, and rich in precious trophies. At a low centre-table were rice and fish and saké. His little geisha girl was curled up in a bunch on the floor with her samisen. They were having a beautiful celebration without any chaperon, but a third party was no intrusion, and the girl thought it quite proper to be found alone entertaining her young man.

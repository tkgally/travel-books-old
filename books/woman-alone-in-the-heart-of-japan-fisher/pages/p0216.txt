His broad smile and kindly eyes were full of friendship. His rugged frame was bent with the battles of life, and he had been scarred in the struggles, but he had not lost the gentle demeanour of the unspoiled native. His abbreviated tights were cut off on the thighs. One knee was circled with court-plaster, and one big toe was heavily bandaged. He sucked in a ponderous breath to show respect, as he doubled like a jack-knife, and said: “Me Heihachee. Me go. Oksan like Japanese chow?” No, Oksan did <i>not</i> like Japanese chow even a little bit. He threw back his classic head, emitted a merry roar, and was off. His aide was a stalwart young fellow, and I knew that I was very lucky in my retinue.

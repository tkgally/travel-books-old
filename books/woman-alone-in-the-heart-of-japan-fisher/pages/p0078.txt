If the face were not a blank, it stood for stony indifference, as if the girl were driven blindly on through empty space. Sometimes there were pathos and sadness, a hunger and longing in the eyes which might never again be lighted by hope. Occasionally the girl spoke briefly to her ahmah, but always with a quiet dignity. Not once did a girl show consciousness of the staring crowds.

“They are prostitutes, but very great ladies, so grand that they often keep their noblest patron waiting, and will not see him till it suits their pleasure,” said a guide. The remark was a key to the situation in Japan. The lost girl, isolated, and set apart in her peculiar quarter, yet held mastery among her guests. She was sure of patronage. The highest and the noblest would bid for her, and, while she held her popular rank, she could indulge her petty whims and fancies, and the nobles themselves must do her bidding.

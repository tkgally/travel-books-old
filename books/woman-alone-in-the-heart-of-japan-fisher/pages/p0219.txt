Sometimes they acted as pushman and pullman, at other times they ran in tandem. Often they lifted the rik bodily out of the ruts, and carried it apace. Frequently I alighted and trudged afoot, when I saw the muscles strain and the perspiration run in rivers from the tawny skin. Where the way was almost perpendicular, old Fidelity would say, “Pleeze getty down, leetle walky, moochee uppee.” Where the freshet had washed away the bridge in a wild mountain region, rik and men and passenger were packed away in a primitive sampan, and ferried across by rope and pulley, that we might not be carried down the raging stream.

At noon we took a nap and refreshments at a roadside tea-house. After the midday heat, we trundled on till five <sc>p. m.</sc>, and rattled up to Hagiwara’s inn. The gentleman was fat, fair, and forty, with a retinue of kindly servants and jolly children.

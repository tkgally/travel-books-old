“Indade, don’t I know yer rules, and wud I bay afther makin’ yer throuble? Not a sign av an igg wuz ther in it.” Later she gleefully whispered in my ear, “It’s not meself that makes sponge cake without the iggs, and ther wuz iggs enough ter make it good. The poor, starved crittur shud have wan rich bite in his life.”

We were the only women and the only foreigners on the platform, and it was very <i>infra dig.</i> to cross the spotless matting in our shoes, but the lady, fat and lame, rebelled when sandals were presented, and readily gave account of her remarkable shoes, that they “nivver tuk dust, they have no hales, an’ are worn a-purpose.” The natives did not quite see the logic, and scanned the foreign shoes most critically, but they were too polite to resent her assurance, so the great concession was granted, and we tiptoed along in shoe leather, like guilty sneaks, while every man left his shoes outside, and entered barefoot, or in his stockings.

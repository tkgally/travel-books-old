Associated with the fame of Camoens is that of Chinnery, who died at Macao, alike celebrated with his pen and pencil. He was an eccentric genius, who loved the productions of his talent better than fame or money, and parting with them for neither.

A terrible incident has associated itself with our stay at Macao. A few days ago, a Spanish bark left here for Cuba with a load of coolies, “humanity swapping black skins for yellow ones.” Soon after leaving the harbor, the coolies rose, murdered the crew, and then after plundering the vessel of everything that was valuable, abandoned her. The vessel was discovered this morning by a merchantman bound into Macao, drifting about. Thinking that something must be the matter on board, they sent a boat to ascertain. They found the officers and crew lying about the decks where they had fallen, and everything that was of value removed from the ship.

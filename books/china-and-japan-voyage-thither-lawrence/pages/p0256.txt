Fair, rosy-cheeked, dark-eyed, laughing-mouthed beings, the younger portion of them are almost as well formed and featured as European females, and of about the same size. Nearly all that we saw of a mature age, had their lips and teeth stained black. Their reason for so doing, I was not able to find out, other than that it is the custom for all married women to do so.

Every few rods along our route we would pass a fine collection of temples, situated in beautiful yards like the houses of the better class. These temples are the most costly and stately structures in the empire; in numbers far surpassing the churches of Catholic countries. They ever occupy the most prominent sites, are shaded by the finest trees, and in all their surroundings, there is more skill and beauty displayed than is elsewhere to be found throughout the country. In architecture, the Japanese are less proficient than in other branches of industry. Temples, gateways, and bridges are their principal works, and are the only edifices constructed of stone or brick. The reason of this is, that at any moment the city or town may be visited by an earthquake, and unless the edifice were very solidly constructed, they would be demolished. The houses are rarely more than one and one-half stories high, built of wood, and have tiled roofs. Occupying the most prominent and beautiful site along our route, and having the most charming surroundings, was an old tower, in which was suspended an ancient bell. This is covered over with ancient inscriptions, and is said to be over two thousand years old. The Japanese regard it with excessive veneration.
